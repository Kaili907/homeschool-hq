# School Essentials Pack — Additions 1–8, 12–14

## 1. MUSIC (all five, light-touch, Friday block + littles' weekday option)

No conservatory ambitions — the goal is baseline musical literacy and joy.

**Littles (3rd/4th) + 6th:** one 20-min Friday session: alternate (A) instrument basics — recorder or keyboard, whichever the house has; note names, simple melodies, one new song per month — and (B) listening lab: one great piece per week across the year (a march, a symphony movement, jazz, film score, folk, gospel, taiko for the Japanese tie-in), 5 minutes of listening + "what did you notice?" Optional 10-min weekday practice slots into the free-choice end of their day.
**Teens:** music rides inside their Applied Arts year as listening/analysis moments (design has soundtracks); no separate credit unless one of them wants to pursue an instrument — then it becomes her elective and we formalize it.
**Year outcome:** littles read basic notation and play simple melodies; everyone can name what they're hearing in five genres. Culture, not coursework — grade nothing unless graded work emerges.

## 2. TYPING — schedule side
5-minute daily drill via the MK module (see SE card), first item after sign-in. Paper handwriting stays (below); the two are complements, not rivals.

## 3. JAPANESE ROSTER CHANGE
The 6th grader joins the 11:30 Japanese block with her sisters, effective immediately. Same curriculum, same pace to start; expect her to pull ahead — when she does, she gets the challenge-word list and becomes the block's assistant teacher (best retention tool there is). Add a Japanese tab to her gradebook at the next update.

## 4. EXTERNAL CALIBRATION
**Annual standardized test, all five:** one nationally-normed test (MAP Growth or Iowa/ITBS via a homeschool testing provider — both offer at-home online administration) each spring (April), so an outside yardstick checks our instruments once a year. Budget ~$25–60/girl. Results feed the gradebooks and the next fall's placement.
**Senior SAT block:** decision point is her college list (test-optional or not) — resolve by early October. If testing: 3×/week 30-min prep block (fall mission template carries it), official practice tests November and January, sit the SAT in March, backup May. Prep uses the free official question bank; her Algebra II timing means math prep leans on live coursework — the plan working as designed.

## 5. HANDWRITING (littles)
Tue/Thu, 10 minutes, paper: 3rd grader finishes print mastery then begins cursive in Q2; 4th grader straight to cursive. Copywork is the medium — sentences from the week's read-aloud, spelling words, or Japanese romaji-to-kana practice. Graded only by "did it happen"; quality feedback verbal.

## 6–7. ATTENDANCE & REPORT CARDS
**Attendance:** automatic once SE ships — mission complete = school day logged, hours estimated, CSV exportable. Nothing for Dad to do.
**Report cards:** each quarter break, upload the quarter's gradebook files to Claude and a proper report card per girl comes back — subject grades, mastery highlights, drafted teacher comments (Dad edits), attendance days, and a "next quarter focus" line. Kid-facing, grandparent-shareable, filed with permanent records. Format locks after Q1's first run.

## 8. TRANSCRIPT & DIPLOMA
**Transcript:** generated from the senior's Credits & GPA tab + prior-school credits — which still requires the credit audit (the one outstanding paper item from her plan). The moment the audit is filled, the official transcript document gets produced: standard college-expected format (student info, courses by year, credits, GPA, grading scale, parent-administrator signature block). Updated each semester; final in June.
**Diploma:** issued by Dad as school administrator — document produced near June. Decision required first: the school's official name (worth a family vote). Legally yours to issue in Michigan.
**Sophomore:** her transcript machinery starts now so it builds year by year instead of retroactively.

## 12. SERVICE HOURS (teens)
Logged in-app once SE ships (date / org / hours / Dad-approved lock). Guidance: 30–50 hrs/year each; wrestling-adjacent service counts and is easiest to sustain (helping coach the littles' club, tournament volunteering). Export drops straight into college applications.

## 13. YEARBOOK
A shared photo folder starts now — school moments, mat moments, travel-school days. Assembly is a June capstone owned by one girl (rotate yearly; the 6th grader is a natural first editor). Counts as an applied-arts project week.

## 14. CURRENT EVENTS (6th and up)
One 10-minute weekly mission item (✋): one age-appropriate news story, then aloud or in a line: what happened, who's affected, one question you still have. Sources: a kid-oriented news digest for the 6th grader; real outlets with source-comparison habits for the teens (quietly extending AI-literacy verification to human media). Participation only, no grades.
