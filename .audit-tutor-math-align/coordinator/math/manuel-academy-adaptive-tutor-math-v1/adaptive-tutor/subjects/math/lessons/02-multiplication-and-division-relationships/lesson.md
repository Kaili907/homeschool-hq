# Multiplication and Division Relationships

**Sequence ID:** `math-seq-mult-div-rel-v1`  
**Lesson ID:** `math-lesson-02-multiplication-division-relationships`  
**Version:** `1.0.0`  
**Grade band:** 4–6  
**Summary:** Use arrays, equal groups, fact families, area models, and context to connect multiplication and division.

## Instructional commitments

- Do not reveal a final answer before the learner attempts the current step and explains reasoning.
- Present exactly one step or one question at a time.
- Ask the learner to explain how they know before advancing.
- Treat incorrect answers as evidence for routing, not as a character judgment.
- Use warm, specific, non-shaming language. Never label a learner as bad at math.
- Do not diagnose dyscalculia or any medical, developmental, or learning condition.
- Do not complete graded homework. Offer concept instruction or a similar ungraded example instead.
- Never claim mastery from one correct response; require repeated evidence across representations.
- Never require a camera, microphone, or identifiable child photo.

## Prerequisites

- Skip-count by common factors.
- Represent basic multiplication with equal groups.
- Use addition and subtraction facts accurately.

## Objectives

- Interpret factors as number of groups and group size.
- Use multiplication facts to solve and check division.
- Multiply multi-digit numbers using area-model partial products.
- Interpret remainders according to units and context.

## Diagnostic assessment

Administer one item at a time. Ask the learner to explain the choice before showing correctness. Record the response as evidence and route only after enough evidence is available.

### 1. Which picture represents 4 groups of 6?
- A. An array with 4 rows of 6
- B. An array with 6 rows of 4
- C. A line of 10 objects
- D. 24 objects with no grouping

### 2. Use 7 × 8 = 56. Which division equation is in the same fact family?
- A. 56 ÷ 7 = 8
- B. 56 ÷ 8 = 7
- C. Both A and B
- D. 56 − 8 = 48

### 3. What does 24 ÷ 6 ask in a “groups of 6” interpretation?
- A. Subtract 6 once from 24
- B. Find how many groups of 6 fit in 24
- C. Add 24 and 6
- D. Find 24 groups of 6

### 4. Which partial products correctly decompose 23 × 6?
- A. 20 × 6 and 3 × 6
- B. 2 × 6 and 3 × 6
- C. 23 × 3 and 23 × 3 only
- D. 20 × 3 and 6 × 3

- A. 12 + ? = 156
- B. 12 × ? = 156
- C. 156 × 12 = ?
- D. 156 − 12 = ?

### 6. Thirty students ride in vans that hold 8 students each. Explain what the remainder means and how many vans are needed.
- Response: explain with words, an equation, or the named representation.


## Likely misconception patterns and distinguishing evidence

### Sees multiplication only as repeated addition and cannot use structure
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-md-repeated-add-only`
**Likely evidence:**
- Counts every object one by one in a large array.
- Cannot decompose 23 × 6 into 20 × 6 and 3 × 6.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Ask for two ways to split an array.
- Ask which partial product matches each rectangle.
**First response:** Repeated addition is one meaning. Let’s also use the array’s structure so we do not have to count one object at a time.
**Route:** `reteach-md-array-decompose`

### Confuses number of groups with size of each group
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-md-groups-size-swap`
**Likely evidence:**
- Uses the wrong unit in a word problem.
- Draws 6 groups of 4 when asked for 4 groups of 6 and cannot explain the difference.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Ask the learner to label groups and items per group.
- Compare 4 × 6 and 6 × 4 in context.
**First response:** The product may stay the same, but the story roles are different. Let’s label groups and items in each group.
**Route:** `reteach-md-equal-groups`

### Treats division as subtracting the divisor once
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-md-division-subtraction`
**Likely evidence:**
- Answers 24 ÷ 6 as 18.
- Stops after one subtraction.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Ask how many groups of 6 fit into 24.
- Use repeated subtraction until zero and count jumps.
**First response:** Division asks how many equal groups or how many in each group, not what remains after one subtraction.
**Route:** `reteach-md-division-meaning`

### Does not connect multiplication and division as inverse relationships
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-md-inverse-gap`
**Likely evidence:**
- Knows 7 × 8 but cannot use it for 56 ÷ 7.
- Writes an unrelated fact family.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Cover one factor in an array equation.
- Ask which multiplication fact checks a division result.
**First response:** The same three numbers can tell a multiplication and division family. Let’s use the known product to find the missing factor.
**Route:** `reteach-md-fact-family`

### Omits or misplaces a partial product
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-md-partial-product`
**Likely evidence:**
- Computes 23 × 6 as 18 or 120.
- Adds 20 + 18 instead of 120 + 18.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Label each area-model region.
- Ask what 20 groups of 6 means.
**First response:** You solved one part. Let’s match every part of 23 to a partial product before combining.
**Route:** `reteach-md-area-model`

### Uses one fixed rule for every remainder
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-md-remainder-rule`
**Likely evidence:**
- Always drops the remainder or always rounds up.
- Gives a fractional bus or person when the context needs whole groups.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Compare “full teams,” “left over,” and “containers needed” contexts.
- Ask what the remainder represents in the story.
**First response:** The calculation gives a remainder, but the story decides what that remainder means.
**Route:** `reteach-md-remainder-context`

## Visual explanation plan

Representations: array, equal-groups, area-model, number-line, step-diagram.

- Objects are countable with row and column labels.
- Text equations accompany every model.
- No learner image or camera is used.

## Spoken explanation

**Voice direction:** warm, clear, curious, nonjudgmental

- `md-n01` — Multiplication and division describe related equal-group situations.
- `md-n02` — An array shows the number of groups and the number in each group.
- `md-n03` — A multiplication fact and its related division facts use the same three numbers.
- `md-n04` — Division can ask how many groups fit, or how many items belong in each group.
- `md-n05` — Number-line jumps make repeated groups visible without hiding the total.
- `md-n06` — An area model splits a difficult factor into friendlier parts and keeps every partial product.
- `md-n07` — When you divide, a missing-factor multiplication equation can guide the solution.
- `md-n08` — A remainder is evidence. Its meaning depends on the question and the units.
- `md-n09` — Check multiplication with division, and check division with multiplication.
- `md-n10` — Explain the group size, the number of groups, and what the result means in context.

## Show Me mode

**Purpose:** See an area model and inverse check unfold one step at a time.
**Practice example:** `18 × 7 and 126 ÷ 7`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** How could 18 be split into friendlier parts?
**Evidence sought:** 10 + 8.
**Hint if needed:** Look for a ten and what remains.
**Visual command:** `md-board-03`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** What two partial products belong to those parts?
**Evidence sought:** 10 × 7 and 8 × 7.
**Hint if needed:** Keep the factor 7 with both parts.
**Visual command:** `md-board-03`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** After combining the partial products, which division equation checks the product?
**Evidence sought:** 126 ÷ 7 = 18.
**Hint if needed:** Use product ÷ one factor = the other factor.
**Visual command:** `md-board-05`

## Talk Me Through It mode

**Purpose:** Explain a division plan using a known multiplication relationship.
**Practice example:** `144 ÷ 12`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** What missing-factor equation matches 144 ÷ 12?
**Evidence sought:** 12 × ? = 144.
**Hint if needed:** Replace division with a multiplication question.
**Visual command:** `md-board-05`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** Which known multiples of 12 help you locate the factor?
**Evidence sought:** For example, 12 × 10 = 120 and 12 × 2 = 24.
**Hint if needed:** Build from a friendly ten.
**Visual command:** `md-board-04`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** How will multiplication verify your quotient?
**Evidence sought:** Multiply the quotient by 12 and reach 144.
**Hint if needed:** Use the inverse operation.
**Visual command:** `md-board-05`

## Let’s Do One Together mode

**Purpose:** Alternate array construction and equation writing.
**Practice example:** `24 groups of 8`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** I will draw 20 groups of 8. What smaller rectangle is still needed?
**Evidence sought:** 4 groups of 8.
**Hint if needed:** Split 24 into 20 + 4.
**Visual command:** `md-board-03`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** You find both partial products. What should be combined?
**Evidence sought:** 160 + 32.
**Hint if needed:** Multiply each part by 8.
**Visual command:** `md-board-03`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** I will write the product. You write a related division equation and explain it.
**Evidence sought:** 192 ÷ 8 = 24 or 192 ÷ 24 = 8.
**Hint if needed:** Use the same three numbers.
**Visual command:** `md-board-05`

## Different Example mode

**Purpose:** Transfer the relationship to a remainder context.
**Practice example:** `73 items packed 9 per box`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** What multiplication fact gets closest to 73 without going over?
**Evidence sought:** 9 × 8 = 72.
**Hint if needed:** Use nearby multiples of 9.
**Visual command:** `md-board-04`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** What does the remaining 1 represent?
**Evidence sought:** One unpacked item.
**Hint if needed:** Subtract the packed amount from the total.
**Visual command:** `md-board-02`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** Would the answer be 8 boxes or 9 boxes if every item must be packed? Explain.
**Evidence sought:** Nine boxes, because the remaining item needs another box.
**Hint if needed:** Let the story decide how to use the remainder.
**Visual command:** `md-board-02`

## Reteaching branches

### reteach-md-equal-groups
Targets: math-mis-md-groups-size-swap
- Label groups and items per group.
- Build the same product with two array orientations.
- Explain what changes in a story even when the product stays equal.

### reteach-md-division-meaning
Targets: math-mis-md-division-subtraction
- Circle equal groups in a total.
- Count groups or count items per group.
- Connect repeated subtraction to the number of jumps, not one subtraction.

### reteach-md-fact-family
Targets: math-mis-md-inverse-gap
- Build one array.
- Write two multiplication equations.
- Cover one factor and write two related division equations.
- Use multiplication to check division.

### reteach-md-area-model
Targets: math-mis-md-repeated-add-only, math-mis-md-partial-product
- Partition a factor by place value.
- Label each rectangle.
- Compute every partial product.
- Combine and check with an estimate.

### reteach-md-remainder-context
Targets: math-mis-md-remainder-rule
- Solve one division calculation.
- Place the same quotient and remainder in three different stories.
- Decide whether to drop, report, or use another group based on units.

## Prerequisite-remediation branch

### prerequisite-remediation-md-groups
Targets: equal groups, skip counting, basic facts
- Build groups with counters.
- Write repeated addition and multiplication for the same model.
- Find missing factors within 10 × 10.
**Exit evidence:** Correctly label and explain three equal-group models in two separate attempts.

## Parent or teacher review note

**Look for:**
- Can the learner label groups and group size?
- Does the learner use multiplication to check division?
- Can the learner explain what a remainder means in the specific story?
**Avoid:**
- Drilling isolated facts as the only evidence.
- Accepting an unexplained rounded quotient in a context problem.
- Calling slow counting a failure instead of offering structure.
**Review prompt:** Ask: “What multiplication fact proves your division result?”

## No-media fallback

- Draw arrays as dots in rows.
- Circle equal groups on paper.
- Split rectangles with a vertical line for area models.
- Use tick marks for number-line jumps.

The sequence remains fully functional without images, animation, audio, a camera, or an identifiable learner photo.
