# Personal Finance — Half-Credit Course (Sophomore & Senior, Together)

**Format:** two 30–40 min sessions per week, 36 weeks — one lesson, one applied session (the applied half is where it sticks). Both girls take it together; the projects diverge where their lives do. **Credit:** 0.5 elective credit each — log work in the Elective tab of each gradebook and add a "Personal Finance (0.5)" row to the Credits & GPA sheet. Transcript-legitimate and increasingly expected: Michigan now requires personal finance for recent public-school cohorts, so this reads as standard preparation on the sophomore's transcript and smart preparation on the senior's.

**Teaching stance:** almost nothing here is abstract. Every unit lands in a spreadsheet, a real comparison, or a real decision one of them is actually facing. Dad shares real numbers where comfortable — a real utility bill teaches more than a textbook chapter.

---

## Q1 (Weeks 1–9): Money In — Earning, Taxes, Budgeting, Banking

| Wks | Topic | Applied session |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What money is for: values before budgets | Each girl writes her 3 money priorities for the next 5 years |
| 2 | Income: jobs, gross vs. net, reading a paystub | Decode a real (or sample) paystub line by line |
| 3 | Taxes I: why, what kinds, where it goes | Compute take-home pay on a hypothetical first job |
| 4 | Banking: checking, savings, how banks make money, fees | Compare 3 real banks/credit unions — fees, minimums, interest |
| 5 | Budgeting I: the 50/30/20 frame | Build her own budget spreadsheet (Excel — doubles as a job skill) |
| 6 | Budgeting II: tracking reality vs. plan | Track every dollar she touches for one week |
| 7 | Emergency funds & why cash buffers beat luck | Size a starter emergency fund for a first apartment |
| 8 | Smart spending: unit price, quality vs. price, marketing tricks | Comparison-shop one real intended purchase; present the pick |
| 9 | Q1 review + budget check-in | Quarter quiz → gradebook |

## Q2 (Weeks 10–18): Borrowed Money — Credit, Debt, and Paying for College

| Wks | Topic | Applied session |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Credit: what it is, why it exists, credit scores decoded | Map what raises/lowers a score |
| 11 | Credit cards: grace periods, minimum-payment trap | **The algebra payoff:** compute real interest on a carried balance |
| 12 | Compound interest — enemy edition | Spreadsheet a $2,000 balance at 24% APR paying minimums; watch the horror |
| 13 | Loans: car, personal, mortgage basics; amortization | Read a real amortization table |
| 14 | Student loans: subsidized vs. not, interest during school, repayment reality | **Senior: her real numbers** — cost of attendance at her actual target schools |
| 15 | Paying for college without drowning: grants, scholarships, work, dual enrollment | Senior: FAFSA walkthrough (timed to her real filing); Sophomore: dual-enrollment savings math |
| 16 | Debt strategy: avalanche vs. snowball; good debt vs. bad | Solve two payoff scenarios |
| 17 | When credit goes wrong: collections, repair, why cosigning is dangerous | Case studies |
| 18 | Q2 review + mid-course check | Quarter quiz → gradebook |

## Q3 (Weeks 19–27): Growing Money — Saving, Investing, Protecting

| Wks | Topic | Applied session |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | Compound interest — ally edition | Same spreadsheet, reversed: $100/month from 18 vs. from 30 |
| 20 | Saving vs. investing; risk and time horizon | Match goals to vehicles |
| 21 | The stock market without the mythology: what a share is | Look up 3 real companies they know |
| 22 | Index funds & diversification: why boring wins | Compare an index fund vs. picking single stocks over history |
| 23 | Retirement accounts: 401(k), match = free money, Roth IRA | **Real move:** a teen with earned income can open a Roth IRA — run the math on starting at 16 |
| 24 | Mock portfolio launch | Each girl "invests" $10,000 on paper; tracked through Week 35 |
| 25 | Insurance: the deal you hope loses; auto/health/renters basics | Read a real auto policy's coverage lines |
| 26 | Inflation & purchasing power | Price a 1995 grocery cart today |
| 27 | Q3 review | Quarter quiz → gradebook |

## Q4 (Weeks 28–36): Real Life — Big Decisions, Defenses, Capstone

| Wks | Topic | Applied session |
|---|---|---|
| 28 | Buying a car: total cost of ownership, new vs. used, dealer math | Price a real car she'd want — payment, insurance, gas, maintenance |
| 29 | Renting: leases, deposits, roommates, utilities | **Senior: build her actual year-one budget** (dorm or apartment); Sophomore: same exercise, hypothetical |
| 30 | Paychecks II: benefits, W-4, first-job negotiation | Compare two job offers (salary vs. benefits) |
| 31 | Filing taxes: 1040 walkthrough, when a teen must file | Mock tax return on a part-time income |
| 32 | Scams & defenses: phishing, "guaranteed returns," identity theft, social-media money traps | Spot-the-scam gauntlet — 10 real-world examples |
| 33 | Giving, generosity, and money as a tool (not a scoreboard) | Each girl picks a cause and researches how far $100 goes there |
| 34 | Money & relationships: talking about it, splitting fairly, red flags | Scenario discussions |
| 35 | Mock portfolio results + lessons | Compare outcomes vs. the index; write one paragraph of takeaways |
| 36 | **Capstone: My Five-Year Financial Plan** | Each girl presents: income plan, budget, savings goal, credit plan, one investment commitment |

## Grading & credit
Quarter quizzes + applied projects, photographed/exported to Claude for grade cards like everything else, logged under **Elective** in each gradebook. Capstone = final. On completion, add **Personal Finance — 0.5 credit** to the Credits & GPA tab. Senior note from her math plan still stands: if a target college wants a 4th math credit, a statistics-flavored extension of this course next summer is the natural bridge.

## Why it pairs with what they're already doing
Weeks 11–12 and 19 are algebra with stakes — exponents and percent change doing real work, which reinforces the senior's math year rather than competing with it. The senior's Weeks 14–15 and 29 aren't simulations at all; they're her actual fall. And the star economy downstairs is the kiddie version of this exact curriculum — when the little girls graduate to real money, the course will be waiting for them.
