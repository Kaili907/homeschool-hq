# Life Skills & Electives Pack — 2026–27

Four curricula. For the teens, three of them carry transcript credit: **Wellness = 0.5 Health credit** (required), **Interior + Fashion Design = 1.0 Applied Arts credit** (required), **AI Literacy = graded module** (logged, no separate credit). Personal Finance (0.5 elective) is already built separately. Littles get age-banded versions folded into their existing rhythm — no new schedule blocks for them.

---

## 1. WELLNESS — Sleep, Stress, Relationships (Teens: 0.5 Health credit · 1×/week, 36 weeks)

Delivery: one 30–40 min session weekly — short reading/discussion + one practice tried during the week. Pairs with, never duplicates, Competitor's Mind: mindset handles the athlete's head; this handles the human's life. Wrestling/BJJ remains the 0.5 PE credit — together they complete the Health & PE requirement.

### Q1 — Sleep & Daily Architecture (wks 1–9)
Why sleep is the foundation (memory, mood, athletic recovery — muscle is built in bed, not the gym) · teen circadian reality and why 2am-brain lies · sleep hygiene: light, screens, caffeine timing, consistent wake time · building a personal evening/morning routine · energy management across a day · **project: 2-week sleep log, then design and run her own routine for 2 weeks and report what changed.**

### Q2 — Stress (wks 10–18)
What stress is for (the system that helps you wrestle is the one that ruins your Tuesday) · acute vs. chronic · the body budget: sleep, movement, food, sunlight as stress infrastructure · tools: breathing (they know the reset breath — now the science), movement, writing it down, saying it out loud · workload triage: what's urgent vs. what's loud · overwhelm protocol: what to do the day it's all too much · when stress needs more than tools — and that asking for help is a skill, not a failure · **project: build her personal "bad week protocol" card.**

### Q3 — Relationships & Boundaries (wks 19–27)
What healthy friendship looks and feels like · boundaries: what they are, how to say one out loud, holding one when it's tested · green flags and red flags in any relationship — respect, control, jealousy, isolation patterns named plainly · peer pressure and the exit line · conflict without destruction: repair skills · digital boundaries: what's private stays private, pressure tactics online, when to screenshot-and-tell-Dad · respect and consent as the baseline of every relationship · **project: each girl writes her own non-negotiables list (private — hers to keep).**

### Q4 — The Whole Human (wks 28–36)
Fueling a body you're asking a lot of (athlete framing: food as performance, never punishment) · movement outside sport · media diet: what her feed feeds her · gratitude and attention as trainable · putting it together: her personal operating manual — sleep plan, stress tools, boundary lines, fuel habits · **capstone: "How I Run Me" — a one-page guide she writes for herself, graded on completion, private in content.**

**Littles' version:** no course — three habits woven in: consistent bedtime framed as brain-growing (Wk 1 mindset tie-in), dragon breathing they already have, and two Friday conversations per quarter ("what makes a good friend," "what to do when someone breaks the rules with you"), Dad-led, five minutes each.

**Grading:** effort/completion-based — logs kept, practices tried, projects done. Reflections and personal lists are graded as *done*, never read for content. Log in Elective tab; add **Health — 0.5** to Credits & GPA on completion.

## 2. AI LITERACY (all five girls, age-banded · one quarter, 1×/week, 9 weeks)

They use an AI-built school with an AI tutor — they should understand the tool. Teens run it as a 9-week module (graded, logged under Elective, no separate credit); littles get the 10-minute version of each idea.

| Wk | Teens | Littles (10-min version) |

## 3. INTERIOR DESIGN (Teens: 0.5 Applied Arts credit · Semester 1, 2×/week, 18 weeks)

Project-based and self-directed — Dad facilitates, doesn't teach. Real design skills with a free tool (Canva or similar) and a sketchbook.

**Units:** elements of design (line, light, color, texture, scale) · color theory and palettes · reading a room: function first · measuring and floor plans — drawing her own bedroom to scale (real math: area, ratio, scale conversion) · mood boards · styles tour (mid-century, farmhouse, minimalist, maximalist — finding *her* style) · budget design: transform a room on $100 on paper (Personal Finance crossover) · lighting · small-space problem solving.

**Capstone: redesign her own bedroom** — measured floor plan, mood board, palette, sourced budget under a Dad-set cap, and a presentation to the family. If the budget's real and the plan's good — Dad's call — she gets to execute it, which is the best final exam an elective ever had.

**Littles' version:** Friday art block, 4–6 sessions across the semester — design a dream room in a shoebox diorama, pick a palette, "why did you put that there?"

## 4. FASHION DESIGN (Teens: 0.5 Applied Arts credit · Semester 2, 2×/week, 18 weeks)

Same model: projects, portfolio, real skills.

**Units:** what clothes communicate; a short history of why we wear what we wear · elements: silhouette, proportion, color, fabric · figure sketching (croquis) — no drawing talent required, templates allowed · textiles: natural vs. synthetic, why fabric choice is the design · building a personal style definition (hers, not the feed's) · wardrobe architecture: capsule thinking, cost-per-wear math (Personal Finance crossover again) · basic construction: hand-sewing a hem, a button, one simple machine or no-sew project · upcycling: thrift-flip one real garment · fast fashion economics and ethics — what $6 shirts actually cost · media literacy: how trends are manufactured.

**Capstone: a 5-piece mini-collection** — sketched, fabric-specified, one piece actually made or upcycled, presented as a portfolio. Portfolio + thrift-flip + completion of units = the grade.

**Littles' version:** Friday art block, 4–6 sessions — design an outfit on paper for a story character, decorate a plain tee, sort the sock drawer by color (they'll think it's a game; it's a palette exercise).

## Scheduling & load (honest math for the teens)

Weekly add: Wellness 1×35 + Arts 2×45 + Personal Finance 2×30 (existing) + AI Literacy 1×30 (Q1 only) ≈ **3–3.5 hrs/week** on top of core academics — a normal full high-school load, and most of it is the fun part of school. **Senior flexibility rule:** her math year is the priority; if fall gets heavy, her arts semester can compress (interior design at 1×/week across the full year still earns the credit) and AI Literacy can slide to Q2. The sophomore runs it all as written.

**Transcript impact when the year completes:** Health 0.5 ✓ · PE 0.5 ✓ (wrestling) · Applied Arts 1.0 ✓ · Personal Finance 0.5 — four graduation-requirement boxes closed by courses they'll actually enjoy.
