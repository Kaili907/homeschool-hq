# Grade 7 Health — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g7-health`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 36  
**Typical session:** 35–50  
**Cadence:** weekly

## Course description

A Grade 7 health course emphasizing health literacy, body respect, mental health, relationships and consent, substance-risk reduction, sexual-health foundations, and help-seeking.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A private-safe wellness, communication, and safety portfolio grounded in reliable information and trusted support.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Health Literacy, Systems, and Support | 6 | Michigan Health: Core Concepts, Accessing Information | Create a health-information verification protocol and support map using fictional examples. |
| 2 | Nutrition, Movement, Sleep, and Body Respect | 6 | Michigan Health: Nutrition, Self-Management | Design several flexible wellness routines without calorie targets, dieting, weight cutting, weigh-ins, or body comparison. |
| 3 | Mental and Emotional Health Skills | 6 | Michigan Health: Mental and Emotional Health, Goal Setting | Respond to fictional scenarios with coping, support, and escalation choices while protecting privacy. |
| 4 | Relationships, Consent, and Communication | 6 | Michigan Health: Interpersonal Communication, Advocacy | Practice communication and bystander scripts with opt-out from role-play and no personal disclosure. |
| 5 | Substance, Medication, and Risk-Reduction Skills | 6 | Michigan Health: Substance Use Prevention, Decision-Making | Build a realistic refusal and help-seeking plan for fictional social and household scenarios. |
| 6 | Sexual Health, Safety, and Community Wellbeing | 6 | Michigan Health: Sexual Health, Disease Prevention, Advocacy | Complete an age-appropriate, fact-based safety and advocacy project with a private question channel and family review option. |
