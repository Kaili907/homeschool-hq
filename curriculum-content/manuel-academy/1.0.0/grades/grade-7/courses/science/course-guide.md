# Grade 7 Science — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g7-science`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 108  
**Typical session:** 45–65  
**Cadence:** three times weekly

## Course description

A locally sequenced Grade 7 science course drawn from Michigan’s Grades 6–8 performance-expectation band, emphasizing matter, energy, cells, ecosystems, Earth systems, hazards, and engineering.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **An engineering and evidence exhibition linking a tested design to a scientific model.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Matter, Particles, and Properties | 12 | MS-PS1-1, MS-PS1-3 | Develop and defend particle-level models for several materials and transformations. |
| 2 | Chemical Reactions and Conservation | 12 | MS-PS1-2, MS-PS1-5, MS-PS1-6 | Conduct safe reaction analyses and design a device that manages thermal energy under clear constraints. |
| 3 | Thermal Energy and Energy Transfer | 12 | MS-PS3-3, MS-PS3-4, MS-PS3-5 | Test insulating or thermal-control designs and explain results using particle and energy models. |
| 4 | Cells and Organism Organization | 12 | MS-LS1-1, MS-LS1-2, MS-LS1-3 | Create a multiscale model linking cell structures to organism-level functions. |
| 5 | Body Systems, Senses, and Responses | 12 | MS-LS1-3, MS-LS1-8 | Analyze a fictional stimulus-response scenario and revise a body-systems model without diagnosing individuals. |
| 6 | Ecosystem Interactions and Dynamics | 12 | MS-LS2-1, MS-LS2-2, MS-LS2-3, MS-LS2-4 | Use a dataset to explain an ecosystem change and predict effects across a food web. |
| 7 | Weather, Climate, and Earth Systems | 12 | MS-ESS2-4, MS-ESS2-5, MS-ESS2-6 | Produce and evaluate a short-range weather explanation and a climate-pattern model using provided data. |
| 8 | Natural Hazards and Human Impacts | 12 | MS-ESS3-2, MS-ESS3-3, MS-ESS3-4, MS-ESS3-5 | Develop an evidence-based community resilience proposal with tradeoffs and equity considerations. |
| 9 | Engineering Design Capstone | 12 | MS-ETS1-1, MS-ETS1-2, MS-ETS1-3, MS-ETS1-4 | Design, test, analyze, and improve a solution connected to energy, ecosystems, weather, or community safety. |
