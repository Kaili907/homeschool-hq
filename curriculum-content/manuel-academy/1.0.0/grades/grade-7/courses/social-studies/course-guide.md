# Grade 7 Social Studies — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g7-social-studies`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 108  
**Typical session:** 45–60  
**Cadence:** three times weekly

## Course description

A Michigan-aligned Grade 7 world history and geography course from early human societies through major global interactions by about 1500 CE, with civic inquiry and respectful treatment of cultures and belief systems.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A comparative world-history inquiry exhibition using maps, sources, argument, and a civic reasoning connection.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | World History and Geography Inquiry | 12 | Michigan Grade 7 H1, G1, P1 | Build an inquiry portfolio and evaluate how geography and evidence shape historical explanations. |
| 2 | Era 1: Beginnings of Human Society | 12 | Michigan Grade 7 W1 | Create a migration-and-adaptation model that distinguishes evidence from inference. |
| 3 | Era 2: Early Civilizations and Pastoral Peoples | 12 | Michigan Grade 7 W2, G4 | Compare how two early societies organized power, resources, belief, and daily life. |
| 4 | Classical Mediterranean Societies | 12 | Michigan Grade 7 W3, C1 | Create a civic-ideas exhibit tracing one concept through Greek and Roman contexts and its limits. |
| 5 | Classical Asian and African Societies | 12 | Michigan Grade 7 W3, G4 | Develop a cross-regional comparison centered on institutions, ideas, trade, and innovation. |
| 6 | World Religions and Cultural Traditions | 12 | Michigan Grade 7 W3, W4, G4 | Create a neutral, source-based cultural geography guide that avoids ranking beliefs or asking learners to disclose faith. |
| 7 | Era 4: Expanding and Intensified Hemispheric Interactions | 12 | Michigan Grade 7 W4, G4 | Build an interconnected-world map showing movement of goods, knowledge, people, and ideas. |
| 8 | Trade Networks, Technology, and Change to 1500 | 12 | Michigan Grade 7 W4, E2, G3 | Trace one product or idea across networks and explain economic, cultural, and environmental consequences. |
| 9 | Comparative Civilization and Civic Inquiry Capstone | 12 | Michigan Grade 7 H1, W1-W4, G1-G4, C1, P3, P4 | Answer a comparative world-history question and connect the reasoning tools—not simplistic analogies—to a current public issue. |
