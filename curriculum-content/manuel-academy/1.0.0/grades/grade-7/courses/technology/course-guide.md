# Grade 7 Technology and Computer Science — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g7-technology`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 36  
**Typical session:** 40–60  
**Cadence:** weekly

## Course description

A Grade 7 computer-science and technology course covering programming, web design, data, cybersecurity, research, accessibility, and responsible AI use.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **An accessible human-centered digital product with source, test, privacy, and impact documentation.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Digital Citizenship, Research, and Media Literacy | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing, Networks and Internet | Produce a source-verified digital explainer with an evidence and media-rights log. |
| 2 | Algorithms and Programming Structures | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Algorithms and Programming | Build a small interactive program and document tests, bugs, and revisions. |
| 3 | Web and Interface Design | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Computing Systems, Impacts of Computing | Create a small accessible site or prototype for a curriculum or community purpose. |
| 4 | Data and Visualization | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Data and Analysis | Analyze an approved dataset and publish an honest visualization with limits. |
| 5 | Networks, Cybersecurity, and Digital Safety | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Networks and Internet, Cybersecurity | Complete a fictional security audit and create a response plan without testing real systems. |
| 6 | Human-Centered and AI-Aware Design | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing, Algorithms and Programming | Design and test a tool or workflow that uses no AI or uses AI transparently with human verification. |
