# Grade 3 Science — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g3-science`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 108  
**Typical session:** 40–55  
**Cadence:** three times weekly across 36 weeks

## Course description

Phenomenon-centered Grade 3 science aligned to the Michigan K-12 Science Standards, with adult-approved safe investigations, modeling, data analysis, and engineering design.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan science expectations through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A weather-hazard and habitat-change engineering exhibition with text-only and no-media alternatives.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring 12-day arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Each unit standard is revisited on twelve separate instructional occasions within its unit, and again in the course capstone; the mastery rule requires accurate independent evidence on at least two occasions.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.
- Every investigation has an equivalent text-only path using provided data or a described demonstration; no camera, photograph, or purchase is ever required.
- Hands-on work requires adult approval and supervision, surfaced to the guardian before the lesson begins.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Science and Engineering Practices | 12 | 3-5-ETS1-1, 3-5-ETS1-2, 3-5-ETS1-3 | Define a simple design problem from home or class life, list its criteria and constraints, and plan a fair test. |
| 2 | Forces and Motion | 12 | 3-PS2-1, 3-PS2-2 | Plan and carry out an adult-approved investigation of balanced and unbalanced forces, then use a motion pattern to predict what happens next. |
| 3 | Magnetic and Electric Interactions | 12 | 3-PS2-3, 3-PS2-4 | Investigate magnetic and electric interactions that act at a distance, then define and sketch a simple device that solves a problem using magnets. |
| 4 | Life Cycles of Organisms | 12 | 3-LS1-1 | Develop and revise a model that describes the life cycle of a plant and an animal and names the stages they share. |
| 5 | Inherited Traits and Variation | 12 | 3-LS3-1, 3-LS3-2 | Analyze a provided trait data set, then explain with evidence which traits are inherited and which are influenced by the environment. |
| 6 | Living in Groups and Surviving in Habitats | 12 | 3-LS2-1, 3-LS4-3 | Construct an evidence-based argument about why some animals form groups and why some organisms survive well in a particular habitat while others do not. |
| 7 | Fossils and Environments Long Ago | 12 | 3-LS4-1, 3-LS4-2 | Interpret a provided fossil data set to describe an ancient Michigan-area environment and explain how trait variation can help organisms survive. |
| 8 | Weather Patterns and World Climates | 12 | 3-ESS2-1, 3-ESS2-2 | Represent a season of provided weather data in a table and graph, then combine sources to compare climates in two world regions. |
| 9 | Capstone: Weather Hazards and Changing Habitats | 12 | 3-ESS3-1, 3-LS4-4, 3-5-ETS1-1, 3-5-ETS1-2, 3-5-ETS1-3 | Design, fair-test, and improve a model solution that reduces the impact of a weather hazard or a habitat change, then defend its merit with evidence. |
