# Grade 4 Science — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g4-science`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 108  
**Typical session:** 40–55  
**Cadence:** three times weekly across 36 weeks

## Course description

Phenomenon-centered Grade 4 science aligned to the Michigan K-12 Science Standards, including Michigan-specific Earth science expectations, with adult-approved safe investigations, modeling, and engineering design.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan science expectations through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **An energy-resource and Michigan-hazard engineering exhibition with text-only and no-media alternatives.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring 12-day arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Each unit standard is revisited on twelve separate instructional occasions within its unit, and again in the course capstone; the mastery rule requires accurate independent evidence on at least two occasions.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.
- Every investigation has an equivalent text-only path using provided data or a described demonstration; no camera, photograph, or purchase is ever required.
- Hands-on work requires adult approval and supervision, surfaced to the guardian before the lesson begins.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Science and Engineering Practices | 12 | 3-5-ETS1-1, 3-5-ETS1-2, 3-5-ETS1-3 | Define a simple design problem with criteria and constraints, generate two possible solutions, and plan a fair test that controls variables. |
| 2 | Energy and Speed | 12 | 4-PS3-1, 4-PS3-3 | Use evidence from an adult-approved motion investigation to explain how speed relates to energy, then predict and check what changes when objects collide. |
| 3 | Energy Transfer | 12 | 4-PS3-2 | Make observations that provide evidence energy moves from place to place by sound, light, heat, and simple battery-powered electric currents. |
| 4 | Designing an Energy Converter | 12 | 4-PS3-4, 3-5-ETS1-2 | Design, test, and refine a safe device that converts energy from one form to another, then compare it against a second design. |
| 5 | Waves and Information | 12 | 4-PS4-1, 4-PS4-3 | Develop a wave model showing amplitude and wavelength, then generate and compare two solutions that use patterns to send information. |
| 6 | Light, Vision, and the Senses | 12 | 4-PS4-2, 4-LS1-2 | Develop a model showing that light reflects from objects into the eye, and a second model tracing how an animal senses, processes, and responds to information. |
| 7 | Structures That Support Survival | 12 | 4-LS1-1 | Construct an evidence-based argument that specific internal and external structures support survival, growth, behavior, and reproduction. |
| 8 | Earth's Changing Landscape | 12 | 4-ESS1-1, 4-ESS2-1, 4-ESS2-2 | Use rock-layer and fossil patterns plus map data to explain landscape change over time, including possible explanations for Michigan's geological changes. |
| 9 | Capstone: Energy Resources and Michigan Hazards | 12 | 4-ESS3-1, 4-ESS3-2, 3-5-ETS1-1, 3-5-ETS1-3 | Obtain and combine information about energy and fuels, then generate, fair-test, and compare solutions that reduce the impact of a natural Earth process on Michigan's people and places. |
