# Grade 4 Social Studies — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g4-social-studies`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 108  
**Typical session:** 40–55  
**Cadence:** three times weekly across 36 weeks

## Course description

United States Studies for Grade 4, aligned to the Michigan K-12 Social Studies Standards, combining U.S. geography, the growth of Michigan after statehood, federal government, market economics, and evidence-based civic reasoning.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan social studies expectations through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A United States public-issue inquiry with a justified written position and an informing action, presented with text-only and no-media alternatives.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring 12-day arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Each unit standard is carried across all twelve lessons of its unit, and every unit topic is the explicit focus of at least one lesson and is named in the unit assessment.
- Separate evidence occasions come from the investigation day, the performance-task build, the unit assessment, and the correction day; the mastery rule requires accurate independent evidence on at least two of them.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.
- All sources are real, provided, and cited. Quotations, documents, and data are never invented.
- The tutor may explain evidence, context, and source types but may not write the learner’s graded historical or civic argument.

## Standards provenance

Every standards code in this course was taken from the official Michigan Department of Education standards document and verified against its text; none was recalled or inferred.

| Subject | Official source |
| --- | --- |
| Science | Michigan K-12 Science Standards (MDE) |
| Social Studies | Michigan K-12 Social Studies Standards, v 6/19 (MDE) |

**Source-document note:** the official PDF prints the first economics expectation as `4 – E1.01`, omitting a dot. It is written here as `4 – E1.0.1` to match the document’s own numbering scheme for every other code. The expectation text is unchanged.

The capstone may be presented publicly, privately, in writing, orally, through a demonstration, or in another accessible format that preserves the learning targets.
