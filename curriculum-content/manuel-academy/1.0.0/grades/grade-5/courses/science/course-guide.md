# Grade 5 Science — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g5-science`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 108  
**Typical session:** 45–65  
**Cadence:** three times weekly

## Course description

Phenomenon-centered Grade 5 science aligned to Michigan performance expectations, with safe investigations, modeling, data analysis, and engineering.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A systems investigation and engineering-design exhibition with text-only and no-media alternatives.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Science and Engineering Practices | 12 | 3-5-ETS1-1, 3-5-ETS1-2, 3-5-ETS1-3 | Design, test, and improve a simple solution to a classroom or home problem using safe materials. |
| 2 | Properties of Matter | 12 | 5-PS1-1, 5-PS1-3 | Create an evidence-based material identification guide using safe samples and measured properties. |
| 3 | Changes and Conservation of Matter | 12 | 5-PS1-2, 5-PS1-4 | Conduct safe change-of-matter investigations and defend whether matter was conserved. |
| 4 | Energy in Food and Motion | 12 | 5-PS3-1, 5-PS2-1 | Create a model tracing energy from sunlight through organisms and connect it to observable motion or work. |
| 5 | Plants, Ecosystems, and Matter Cycles | 12 | 5-LS1-1, 5-LS2-1 | Build and revise an ecosystem model that tracks matter and energy and explains a change. |
| 6 | Earth Systems and Water | 12 | 5-ESS2-1, 5-ESS2-2 | Develop a local or fictional watershed explanation using maps, data, and an Earth-systems model. |
| 7 | Patterns in the Sky | 12 | 5-ESS1-1, 5-ESS1-2 | Create a sky-pattern observation report using safe indirect observations and provided data. |
| 8 | Human Impact and Environmental Decisions | 12 | 5-ESS3-1, 3-5-ETS1-1 | Propose and test a realistic action that protects an Earth resource without shaming individual households. |
| 9 | Integrated Science and Engineering Capstone | 12 | 5-PS1-1, 5-LS2-1, 5-ESS2-1, 5-ESS3-1, 3-5-ETS1-3 | Investigate a local or simulated system, then present a model, evidence-based explanation, and tested improvement. |
