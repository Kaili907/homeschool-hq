# Grade 5 Technology and Computer Science — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g5-technology`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 36  
**Typical session:** 40–60  
**Cadence:** weekly

## Course description

Foundational digital literacy and computer science with strong privacy, source, accessibility, and AI-verification habits.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A user-centered prototype with algorithm, test log, accessibility check, and presentation.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Digital Citizenship and Privacy | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing, Networks and Internet | Create a digital-safety guide using fictional examples and no real credentials. |
| 2 | Computing Systems, Files, and Troubleshooting | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Computing Systems | Organize a sample project folder and write a step-by-step troubleshooting flowchart. |
| 3 | Typing and Productivity Tools | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Computing Systems, Data and Analysis | Create an accessible one-page guide and short presentation on a curriculum topic. |
| 4 | Algorithms and Introductory Coding | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Algorithms and Programming | Build a small story, game, animation, or simulation and document the algorithm. |
| 5 | Data, Media, and Source Responsibility | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Data and Analysis, Impacts of Computing | Create a transparent data story with cited or original media and an AI/source verification note. |
| 6 | Design for a User | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Algorithms and Programming, Impacts of Computing | Design and test a simple digital or paper prototype that helps a real or fictional user. |
