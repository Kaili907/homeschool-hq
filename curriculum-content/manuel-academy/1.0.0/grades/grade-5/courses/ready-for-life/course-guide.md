# Grade 5 Ready for Life — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g5-ready-for-life`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 36  
**Typical session:** 35–60  
**Cadence:** weekly

## Course description

A full-year Grade 5 practical-life course that references the Director-frozen 12-lesson Ready for Life baseline and adds transfer labs without modifying the frozen artifact.

**Frozen baseline:** The first four units reference the Director-frozen Ready for Life v1 lessons. The weekly transfer labs in this package extend application without rewriting the frozen lessons.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A guardian-visible “Finish the Loop” contribution project with checklist, evidence, and reflection.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Caring for Home and Shared Spaces | 6 | Ready for Life v1 Lessons 01–03, Michigan Health/SEL connections | Use the frozen Ready for Life home-care lessons, then design and test a no-shame reset routine. |
| 2 | Clothing and Laundry | 6 | Ready for Life v1 Lessons 04–06 | Use the frozen laundry lessons with guardian visibility, then create a repeatable clothing-care checklist. |
| 3 | Kitchen Care and Basic Food Safety | 6 | Ready for Life v1 Lessons 07–09 | Use the frozen kitchen lessons and safely complete a guardian-approved food-preparation loop. |
| 4 | Body Signals and Building a Fueling Meal | 6 | Ready for Life v1 Lessons 10–12 | Use the frozen body-signal and meal lessons to plan, prepare or model, serve, store, and clean without diet framing. |
| 5 | Time, Belongings, and Communication | 6 | Manuel Academy RFL extension | Build and test a weekly preparation system for one real responsibility. |
| 6 | Family Contribution Capstone | 6 | Manuel Academy RFL extension | Complete a multi-step family contribution and document the finished loop with a no-photo option. |

## Capstone

A guardian-visible “Finish the Loop” contribution project with checklist, evidence, and reflection.

The capstone may be presented publicly, privately, in writing, orally, through a demonstration, or in another accessible format that preserves the learning targets.
