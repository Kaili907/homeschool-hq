# Grade 5 Social Studies — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g5-social-studies`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 108  
**Typical session:** 45–60  
**Cadence:** three times weekly

## Course description

A Michigan-aligned Grade 5 U.S. history and geography course from Indigenous societies and early contact through colonization, Revolution, the Constitution, and civic participation.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A museum-style inquiry exhibition connecting historical evidence to responsible civic participation.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Geography, Historical Thinking, and Civic Inquiry | 12 | Michigan Grade 5 H1, G1, P1 | Create an inquiry toolkit and apply it to a contested question using multiple sources. |
| 2 | Indigenous Peoples Before European Contact | 12 | Michigan Grade 5 U1.1, G4 | Develop a regional comparison that centers Indigenous knowledge, continuity, and diversity rather than stereotypes. |
| 3 | European Exploration and Global Motives | 12 | Michigan Grade 5 U1.2, G4, E1 | Build an annotated exploration map that distinguishes motives, routes, claims, and consequences. |
| 4 | African Societies and the Atlantic World | 12 | Michigan Grade 5 U1.3, U1.4, G4 | Create an evidence-based exhibit correcting a common misconception about African life before and during early Atlantic contact. |
| 5 | Three Worlds Meet | 12 | Michigan Grade 5 U1.4, G2, G4 | Construct a cause-and-consequence web showing how contact changed peoples, environments, and economies. |
| 6 | Colonization and Regional Development | 12 | Michigan Grade 5 U2.1, U2.2, G2, E1 | Compare two colonies or regions through maps, sources, economies, and lived experiences. |
| 7 | Slavery, Resistance, and Colonial Society | 12 | Michigan Grade 5 U2.2, U2.3, P2 | Create a dignity-centered source analysis explaining systems of slavery and forms of resistance without reenactment. |
| 8 | Road to Revolution and the Revolutionary War | 12 | Michigan Grade 5 U3.1, U3.2 | Prepare an evidence-based town-hall briefing representing multiple historically grounded perspectives without identity role-play. |
| 9 | New Nation, Constitution, and Civic Participation | 12 | Michigan Grade 5 U3.3, C1, C2, P3, P4 | Create a child-accessible constitutional guide and use it to propose a civic action on a current local issue. |
