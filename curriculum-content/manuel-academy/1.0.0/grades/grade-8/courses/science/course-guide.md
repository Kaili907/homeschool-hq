# Grade 8 Science — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g8-science`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 108  
**Typical session:** 45–65  
**Cadence:** three times weekly

## Course description

A locally sequenced Grade 8 science course drawn from Michigan’s Grades 6–8 performance-expectation band, covering forces, waves, heredity, evolution, Earth history, space, resources, and engineering.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A research or engineering defense using models, data, tradeoffs, revisions, and clear limitations.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Forces, Motion, and Interactions | 12 | MS-PS2-1, MS-PS2-2, MS-PS2-4, MS-PS2-5 | Design and test a motion or collision system and explain outcomes with force models and data. |
| 2 | Waves and Information Transfer | 12 | MS-PS4-1, MS-PS4-2, MS-PS4-3 | Build or analyze a communication system and evaluate how wave properties affect information transfer. |
| 3 | Growth, Reproduction, and Heredity | 12 | MS-LS1-4, MS-LS3-1, MS-LS3-2 | Develop an inheritance model using fictional organisms and explain what the model can and cannot predict. |
| 4 | Natural Selection and Adaptation | 12 | MS-LS4-1, MS-LS4-2, MS-LS4-3, MS-LS4-4, MS-LS4-6 | Use multiple evidence types to explain a population change without implying that individuals evolve on demand. |
| 5 | Earth History and Geologic Processes | 12 | MS-ESS1-4, MS-ESS2-1, MS-ESS2-2, MS-ESS2-3 | Construct a regional geologic-history explanation from maps, cross-sections, rocks, and fossil data. |
| 6 | Earth, Moon, Sun, and the Solar System | 12 | MS-ESS1-1, MS-ESS1-2, MS-ESS1-3 | Create and critique scale and motion models for an Earth-space phenomenon. |
| 7 | Resources, Climate, and Human Systems | 12 | MS-ESS3-1, MS-ESS3-3, MS-ESS3-4, MS-ESS3-5 | Evaluate a resource or climate-response decision using science, uncertainty, costs, benefits, and affected communities. |
| 8 | Applied Biology and Ecosystem Design | 12 | MS-LS2-4, MS-LS2-5, MS-LS4-5, MS-ETS1-2 | Compare and improve solutions for biodiversity, food systems, habitat, or restoration using an impact matrix. |
| 9 | Engineering and Scientific Argument Capstone | 12 | MS-ETS1-1, MS-ETS1-2, MS-ETS1-3, MS-ETS1-4 | Complete an engineering or research capstone tied to forces, waves, life science, Earth science, or environmental systems. |

## Capstone

A research or engineering defense using models, data, tradeoffs, revisions, and clear limitations.

The capstone may be presented publicly, privately, in writing, orally, through a demonstration, or in another accessible format that preserves the learning targets.
