# Grade 8 Health — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g8-health`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 36  
**Typical session:** 35–50  
**Cadence:** weekly

## Course description

A Grade 8 health course supporting high-school readiness through health literacy, body-respectful wellness, mental-health support, relationships and consent, substance-risk reduction, sexual health, and advocacy.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A fact-checked health communication and self-advocacy portfolio with protected privacy.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Health Systems, Information, and Self-Advocacy | 6 | Michigan Health: Core Concepts, Accessing Information, Advocacy | Create a reliable-information and self-advocacy toolkit using fictional scenarios. |
| 2 | Nutrition, Movement, Sleep, and Performance Without Diet Culture | 6 | Michigan Health: Nutrition, Self-Management | Evaluate fictional wellness plans for adequacy, safety, evidence, and flexibility without calories, weight targets, or moralizing food. |
| 3 | Mental Health, Stress, and Support Networks | 6 | Michigan Health: Mental and Emotional Health, Goal Setting | Respond to fictional situations with boundaries, coping options, trusted support, and urgent escalation when indicated. |
| 4 | Healthy Relationships, Consent, and Digital Boundaries | 6 | Michigan Health: Interpersonal Communication, Sexual Health, Advocacy | Develop and practice age-appropriate communication, refusal, support, and reporting scripts with no personal disclosure. |
| 5 | Substance Use, Medication, and Harm Prevention | 6 | Michigan Health: Substance Use Prevention, Decision-Making | Create a prevention and emergency-response communication campaign grounded in reliable sources and trusted-adult action. |
| 6 | Sexual Health, Disease Prevention, and Community Action | 6 | Michigan Health: Sexual Health, Disease Prevention, Advocacy | Complete a fact-checked community-health project with family review choices, private questions, and no disclosure requirement. |
