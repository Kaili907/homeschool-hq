# Grade 8 Technology and Computer Science — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g8-technology`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 36  
**Typical session:** 40–60  
**Cadence:** weekly

## Course description

A Grade 8 computer-science and technology course preparing learners for high-school work in programming, web/app design, data science, cybersecurity, AI literacy, and human-centered impact analysis.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A documented technology capstone with source, code or logic, testing, accessibility, privacy, security, and impact evidence.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Digital Identity, Information, and AI Literacy | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing, Networks and Internet | Create an evidence-based guide to responsible research and AI-assisted work with transparent verification. |
| 2 | Programming and Software Design | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Algorithms and Programming | Build a small application, simulation, or game that solves a defined user need. |
| 3 | Web, App, and Accessible Interface Design | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Computing Systems, Impacts of Computing | Create an accessible multi-screen prototype or small site and document user-test changes. |
| 4 | Data Science and Computational Models | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Data and Analysis | Analyze an approved dataset and publish a reproducible report with uncertainty and limits. |
| 5 | Networks and Cybersecurity | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Networks and Internet, Cybersecurity | Complete a sandboxed or paper cybersecurity design review; never probe real systems without explicit authorization. |
| 6 | Technology Capstone and Impact Review | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Algorithms and Programming, Impacts of Computing | Deliver a software, data, automation, or design capstone with a full impact, privacy, accessibility, and security review. |

## Capstone

A documented technology capstone with source, code or logic, testing, accessibility, privacy, security, and impact evidence.

The capstone may be presented publicly, privately, in writing, orally, through a demonstration, or in another accessible format that preserves the learning targets.
