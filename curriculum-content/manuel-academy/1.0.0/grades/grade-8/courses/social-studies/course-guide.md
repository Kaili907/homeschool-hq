# Grade 8 Social Studies — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g8-social-studies`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 108  
**Typical session:** 45–60  
**Cadence:** three times weekly

## Course description

A Michigan-aligned Grade 8 U.S. history and geography course from constitutional foundations through the early republic, expansion, reform, sectional crisis, Civil War, Reconstruction, and an introduction to 1870–1898.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A constitutional-history inquiry exhibition and civic reasoning project using primary and secondary evidence.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Constitutional Foundations and Historical Inquiry | 12 | Michigan Grade 8 U3.3, H1, C1 | Create a constitutional-debate dossier using varied sources and a defensible historical claim. |
| 2 | Building the Early Republic | 12 | Michigan Grade 8 U4.1, C2, E1 | Develop a decision brief on an early-republic controversy with historically grounded alternatives. |
| 3 | Expansion, Geography, and Indigenous Sovereignty | 12 | Michigan Grade 8 U4.1, U4.2, G2, G4 | Create a map-based inquiry that centers competing claims, sovereignty, consequences, and source perspective. |
| 4 | Economic and Social Transformation | 12 | Michigan Grade 8 U4.2, E1, G4 | Build a cause-and-consequence exhibit on how economic change reshaped work, family, place, and inequality. |
| 5 | Reform Movements and Democratic Participation | 12 | Michigan Grade 8 U4.3, C2, P3 | Analyze strategies used by reformers and design a historically informed civic-action comparison. |
| 6 | Slavery, Resistance, and Sectional Conflict | 12 | Michigan Grade 8 U4.3, U5.1, C2 | Produce a dignity-centered source analysis explaining how slavery and resistance shaped sectional conflict. |
| 7 | Civil War | 12 | Michigan Grade 8 U5.2, G2, E1 | Create an evidence-based campaign and consequences analysis using maps, statistics, and firsthand accounts. |
| 8 | Reconstruction and Its Contested Legacy | 12 | Michigan Grade 8 U5.3, C2, P3 | Evaluate Reconstruction’s achievements, limits, and contested memory through a multi-source inquiry. |
| 9 | Civic Inquiry and Bridge to 1870–1898 | 12 | Michigan Grade 8 U6.1 introduction, P3, P4 | Complete a capstone connecting constitutional and historical evidence to a current civic question without flattening differences in context. |
