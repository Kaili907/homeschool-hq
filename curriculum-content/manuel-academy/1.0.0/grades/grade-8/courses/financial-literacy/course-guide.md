# Grade 8 Financial Literacy — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g8-financial-literacy`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 72  
**Typical session:** 40–60  
**Cadence:** weekly or twice weekly

## Course description

A 72-session Grade 8 personal-finance course covering Michigan PF1–PF7 expectations through realistic simulations. It is designed around the published high-school content expectations. Under Michigan guidance, middle-school instruction must count for the half credit when those expectations are presented and the learner demonstrates proficiency; this curriculum package alone does not document an individual learner’s completion or proficiency.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A comprehensive one-year fictional financial plan and oral or written defense.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | PF1 — Earning Income | 10 | PF1 | Compare three fictional career pathways using income, education cost, time, benefits, interests, and uncertainty. |
| 2 | PF2 — Buying Goods and Services | 10 | PF2 | Complete a simulated major-purchase dossier and consumer-protection plan. |
| 3 | PF3 — Budgeting and Saving | 10 | PF3 | Build and stress-test a three-month fictional budget and savings plan with irregular income or expenses. |
| 4 | PF4 — Using Credit | 11 | PF4, PF4.1 | Compare credit and student-aid offers, calculate total costs, identify risks, and write a safer financing recommendation. |
| 5 | PF5 — Financial Investing | 10 | PF5 | Create a fictional goal-based investment policy statement and evaluate sample portfolios without recommending real securities. |
| 6 | PF6 — Protecting and Insuring | 10 | PF6 | Conduct a fictional household risk audit and recommend layered prevention, emergency savings, and insurance choices. |
| 7 | PF7 — Paying Taxes and Financial Plan Capstone | 11 | PF7, PF1–PF7 synthesis | Produce and defend a one-year fictional financial plan covering income, taxes, spending, saving, credit, investing, protection, and revision. |
