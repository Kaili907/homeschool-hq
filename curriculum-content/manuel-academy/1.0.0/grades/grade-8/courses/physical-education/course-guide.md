# Grade 8 Physical Education — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g8-physical-education`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 108  
**Typical session:** 35–60  
**Cadence:** three times weekly

## Course description

An inclusive Grade 8 physical-education program emphasizing competence, strategy, fitness literacy, leadership, stress management, and transition to independent lifelong activity.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A lifelong-movement transition plan, portfolio defense, and learner-facilitated inclusive event.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Personal Readiness and Goal Design | 12 | Michigan PE Standards 1, 3, 4 | Create an individualized participation plan based on safe progress and enjoyment rather than comparison. |
| 2 | Training Principles and Fitness Literacy | 12 | Michigan PE Standards 3, 4, 5 | Build and evaluate a six-week activity plan with alternatives for space, equipment, and energy level. |
| 3 | Advanced Game Strategy | 12 | Michigan PE Standards 1, 2, 4 | Analyze and implement a tactical improvement in a chosen invasion, net, target, or striking game. |
| 4 | Individual and Dual Activities | 12 | Michigan PE Standards 1, 2, 5 | Develop and demonstrate a safe progressive routine in an individual or dual activity. |
| 5 | Dance, Rhythm, and Creative Performance | 12 | Michigan PE Standards 1, 2, 5 | Create, learn, or analyze a movement work with accessible performance and attribution options. |
| 6 | Outdoor, Aquatic, and Community Activity Planning | 12 | Michigan PE Standards 1, 3, 5 | Plan a safe lifetime-activity experience including permissions, equipment, cost, transport, and contingency options. |
| 7 | Leadership, Coaching, and Officiating | 12 | Michigan PE Standards 2, 4, 5 | Lead, coach, or officiate part of an activity and evaluate the experience using participant evidence. |
| 8 | Stress Management and Lifelong Movement | 12 | Michigan PE Standards 3, 5 | Test several activity options and design a sustainable routine for a future school season. |
| 9 | Lifetime Activity Capstone | 12 | Michigan PE Standards 1-5 | Present a lifelong-movement portfolio and facilitate an inclusive choice-based activity event. |
