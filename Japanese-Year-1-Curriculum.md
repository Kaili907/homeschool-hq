# Japanese — Year 1 Curriculum (3rd & 4th Grader, Together)

**Format:** 10–15 min daily, Mon–Thu, taught to both girls at once + a longer Friday culture block. Slots naturally at 11:30–11:45, right after read-aloud — the day ends in Japanese. You don't need to speak Japanese to run this: you're the coach, audio sources are the pronunciation model, and you learn alongside them (which they'll love).

## Year 1 goals (realistic for 8–9 year olds)

By June: read and write all 46 hiragana + the modified sounds · recognize the katakana for their own names and common words · count, name colors/family/animals/foods · exchange greetings naturally · give a short self-introduction (jikoshōkai) from memory · genuinely like Japanese. That last one is the real goal — Year 1 wins on enthusiasm, not volume.

## Daily routine (Mon–Thu, ~12 min)

1. **Aisatsu ritual (2 min):** start with the day's greeting — *ohayō gozaimasu*, respond, bow. Non-negotiable and fun; this alone builds real speech habits.
2. **Character practice (5 min):** learn or review hiragana on the whiteboard — Dad draws it big, girls air-write, then write it 3× on paper. ~3 new characters per week, rest of the week reviews.
3. **Word/phrase of the week (5 min):** flashcard drill + use it in a mini exchange. Old cards stay in the shuffle (retrieval practice, same as everything else we do).

**Friday (15–20 min, light day):** songs, games (karuta with their own flashcards is perfect), origami, food days, festival stories — culture is the reward that keeps the dailies willing.

## 36-Week Scope

### Q1 (Weeks 1–9): First sounds & survival phrases
- **Hiragana:** vowels あいうえお, then か–さ–た–な rows (~25 characters by week 9, ~3/week)
- **Speech:** greetings (ohayō, konnichiwa, konbanwa, oyasumi, arigatō, sumimasen), yes/no (hai/iie), numbers 1–10, colors
- **Week 9 check:** write/read the characters learned; count to 10; greeting exchange

### Q2 (Weeks 10–18): Finish hiragana & first sentences
- **Hiragana:** は–ま–や–ら–わ rows + ん (all 46 done ~week 16), then dakuten marks (が, ば, ぱ…)
- **Speech:** family words, animals, "Watashi wa ___ desu" (I am ___), age, numbers 11–20
- **Week 18 check:** full hiragana chart from memory (this is the year's big trophy — make it an event)

### Q3 (Weeks 19–27): Reading + katakana begins
- **Reading:** simple hiragana words and mini-phrases — they can now decode!
- **Katakana:** start with the characters in their own names (write your name = instant motivation), then high-frequency ones
- **Speech:** food, body parts, classroom objects, "Kore wa ___ desu" (this is ___), "___ ga suki desu" (I like ___)

### Q4 (Weeks 28–36): Consolidate & perform
- **Katakana:** continue core set; read simple katakana loanwords (they'll be delighted these are English: パン, テレビ)
- **Reading:** hiragana mini-books / simple sentences
- **Capstone:** each girl memorizes and delivers a jikoshōkai — name, age, likes, family — to the whole family, plus a culture project of her choice
- **Year-end check:** hiragana mastery, katakana name + core set, oral phrases

## Materials (cheap and mostly homemade)

Hiragana wall chart by the school table · printable hiragana practice sheets (grid-style, trace-then-write) · **flashcards the girls make themselves** — character one side, sound the other; making them IS a lesson, and they become the Friday karuta deck · audio: kids' Japanese songs and pronunciation videos (counts toward the 15-min video budget — a hiragana song 2×/week is plenty) · sticky-note labels on household objects in hiragana, added as they learn.

Romaji (English letters) is training wheels: fine in Q1, fade it in Q2, gone by Q3 — they should read the kana, not the romaji under it.

## Grading

Friday mini-quiz: 5 characters (dictated sound → they write it) + 5 vocab (English → Japanese or flashcard read). Photo → upload → grade card → **Japanese tab** in each girl's gradebook (added). Quarter checks as listed above. Grade generously on pronunciation, strictly on character writing — stroke habits formed now last forever.

## One note on the big girls

Your sophomore needs 2 world-language credits of the same language for a college-ready transcript. Sitting in on her sisters' 12 minutes won't earn that — she'd need a genuine high-school-level course (online provider or community college dual enrollment). If she's drawn to Japanese once it's in the house, that's a great Q2 conversation; otherwise any language works. The senior's schedule has no room for a new language and colleges will see her transcript as-is — no action needed.
