# Grade 3 Ready for Life — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g3-ready-for-life`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 36  
**Typical session:** 30–50 minutes  
**Cadence:** weekly across the 36-week Manuel Academy year

## Course description

A full-year Grade 3 practical-life course that builds household routines, kitchen-helper safety, body-signal awareness, and finish-the-loop habits. The Director-frozen Ready for Life v1 baseline is referenced as a safety and no-shame policy source only; no frozen lesson content is copied, rewritten, or modified here.

**Frozen baseline:** The Director-frozen Ready for Life v1 artifact (`a5-ready-for-life-v1.zip`) is referenced as a policy and safety source only. No frozen lesson is copied, rewritten, renumbered, or modified by this course. All Grade 3 lessons here are original, age-appropriate content.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, show a process, or justify a choice rather than supply an unsupported answer.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, setting, or situation.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A guardian-confirmed family helper project with a plan, a safety check, a finished-loop checklist, and a private reflection.**

## Instructional model

Each unit runs a six-session arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application or project; mastery check; and targeted correction with reflection. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation, application, error or safety analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Real-world tasks and guardian sign-off

Real-world household tasks are flagged per lesson and fall on unit 1 days 4–5; unit 2 days 4–5; unit 3 days 4–5; unit 4 days 4–5; unit 5 days 4–5; unit 6 days 4–6. For each of those lessons:

- The guardian gives permission **before** the task begins and decides which steps suit this learner and this home.
- A trusted adult supervises every step the safety policy requires.
- Completion is certified by a household-authorized guardian confirmation. A learner self-report is recorded as activity but **cannot** certify an adult-supervised task on its own.
- Evidence may be a checklist, drawing, planning page, spoken explanation, or guardian confirmation. No identifiable learner photo is ever required.

Lesson-level flags are recorded in `study-integration.json`.

## Safety commitments

- No-shame language throughout. Cleanliness, tidiness, and family resources are never treated as measures of a learner's worth.
- No dieting, calorie targets, weigh-ins, body-size scoring, earned-food framing, or food morality. Nutrition is taught as variety, adequacy, energy, growth, and recovery.
- No learner is required to disclose family circumstances, income, health details, or personal history. Reflection may be kept private.
- Heat, sharp tools, appliances, chemicals, and medication always require the supervision level named in the lesson safety block.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Essential question | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Taking Care of My Own Space | 6 | How does taking care of my own space help me and the people I live with? | Plan, do, and check a five-minute tidy in one shared space with a trusted adult nearby, then record the finished loop on a no-shame checklist. |
| 2 | Clothes and Getting Ready | 6 | How can I get my clothes and myself ready without rushing or feeling rushed? | Build a “ready the night before” clothing routine, run it for three days, and record each finished loop with a guardian check. |
| 3 | Kitchen Helper Safety | 6 | How do I help in the kitchen in a way that keeps everyone safe? | With a trusted adult present, prepare one simple no-heat snack and complete the whole clean-up loop from setup to storage. |
| 4 | Body Signals and Fueling My Day | 6 | What are my body's signals telling me, and who can help me answer them? | Keep a private, no-numbers signal notebook for one week and choose one helpful response to try, with no weight, size, or calorie language. |
| 5 | Time, Belongings, and Asking for Help | 6 | How do I keep track of my time, my things, and my questions? | Design a personal “get ready” checklist for one real weekly responsibility and test it twice, revising it after the first try. |
| 6 | Family Helper Capstone | 6 | How do I plan a helper job, finish the whole loop, and check my own work? | Complete a multi-step family helper job with guardian permission and guardian confirmation, then share the finished loop with no photo required. |

## Accessibility

- Provide readable text plus optional audio or read-aloud support; no voice feature is required.
- Chunk directions into one action at a time and show a worked example or model.
- Allow typed, handwritten, spoken, drawn, manipulatives-based, or demonstrated responses when the standard permits.
- Offer reduced-copying, extended-time, hidden-timer, movement-break, and low-distraction options.
- Use captions, alt text, high-contrast print, keyboard access, and text-only fallbacks for media.
- Preserve the learning target while adjusting quantity, pacing, representation, or response mode.
