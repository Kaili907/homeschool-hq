# Grade 4 Health — Complete Lesson Sequence

**Sessions:** 36
**Schedule:** 36 weeks x 1 session per week

## Unit 1: How My Body Works and Stays Well

**Unit ID:** `ma-g4-health-u01`
**Standards:** Michigan Health Practice 3: Information and Resource Seeking; Michigan Health Practice 5: Self-Management and Goal Setting; Michigan Health Topic (Grades 3-5): Personal Health and Wellness
**Essential question:** How do my body systems and my daily habits work together to keep me well?
**Performance task:** Design a prevention routine that explains what each step protects, why it works, and when adult or professional help is needed.
**Adapted alternative:** Diagrams may be replaced with text descriptions, tactile models, or spoken explanation. Any routine step a learner cannot perform independently is planned as an adult-supported step, which counts fully.

### Session 1 (Unit day 1) — Launch and diagnostic: body systems working together
**Lesson ID:** `ma-g4-health-u01-l01`
**Phase:** Launch and diagnostic
**Focus:** body systems working together
**Objective:** Surface what the learner already knows about body systems working together, without penalty, and establish the unit question.

**Student activity:** The learner applies what they learned about body systems working together to a new fictional situation and records the choice plus the reason behind it.

**Exit ticket:** Name one thing you already knew about body systems working together and one question you now have.

### Session 2 (Unit day 2) — Explicit model: germs and how illness spreads
**Lesson ID:** `ma-g4-health-u01-l02`
**Phase:** Explicit model
**Focus:** germs and how illness spreads
**Objective:** Build an accurate mental model of germs and how illness spreads from an explicit demonstration, worked scenario, or age-appropriate text.

**Student activity:** The learner applies what they learned about germs and how illness spreads to a new fictional situation and records the choice plus the reason behind it.

**Exit ticket:** State the most important rule or fact about germs and how illness spreads in your own words.

### Session 3 (Unit day 3) — Guided practice: handwashing and respiratory etiquette
**Lesson ID:** `ma-g4-health-u01-l03`
**Phase:** Guided practice
**Focus:** handwashing and respiratory etiquette
**Objective:** Practice handwashing and respiratory etiquette with prompts, feedback, and gradually reduced support.

**Student activity:** The learner applies what they learned about handwashing and respiratory etiquette to a new fictional situation and records the choice plus the reason behind it.

**Exit ticket:** Using the practice situation, say what the safest or healthiest next step is and why.

### Session 4 (Unit day 4) — Application or project: dental and skin care
**Lesson ID:** `ma-g4-health-u01-l04`
**Phase:** Application or project
**Focus:** dental and skin care
**Objective:** Apply dental and skin care to the unit performance task and justify the choices made.

**Student activity:** The learner applies what they learned about dental and skin care to a new fictional situation and records the choice plus the reason behind it.

**Exit ticket:** Show one part of your project that uses dental and skin care and say why you made that choice.

### Session 5 (Unit day 5) — Mastery check: sleep quality and routines
**Lesson ID:** `ma-g4-health-u01-l05`
**Phase:** Mastery check
**Focus:** sleep quality and routines
**Objective:** Demonstrate current understanding of sleep quality and routines through scenario response and applied evidence.

**Student activity:** The learner applies what they learned about sleep quality and routines to a new fictional situation and records the choice plus the reason behind it.

**Exit ticket:** Explain sleep quality and routines to someone who has not learned it yet, using one example.

### Session 6 (Unit day 6) — Correction and reflection: checkups and staying well
**Lesson ID:** `ma-g4-health-u01-l06`
**Phase:** Correction and reflection
**Focus:** checkups and staying well
**Objective:** Teach and consolidate checkups and staying well, correct any errors from the unit, explain the corrections, and identify where the unit transfers.

**Student activity:** The learner applies what they learned about checkups and staying well to a new fictional situation and records the choice plus the reason behind it.

**Exit ticket:** Name one thing you corrected or would do differently, and one other place checkups and staying well would be useful.

**Unit assessment:** `ma-g4-health-u01-assessment`

## Unit 2: Balanced Eating, Hydration, and Active Living

**Unit ID:** `ma-g4-health-u02`
**Standards:** Michigan Health Practice 1: Self-Awareness and Analyzing Influences; Michigan Health Practice 5: Self-Management and Goal Setting; Michigan Health Topic (Grades 3-5): Balanced Eating and Physical Activity
**Essential question:** How can I plan food, water, and movement in a way that fits my body, my day, and my family?
**Performance task:** Build a week of flexible meal, snack, hydration, and movement options with substitutions for allergies, preferences, culture, and budget. No calorie counting, dieting, weight goal, or body measurement is used.
**Adapted alternative:** Planning may use pictures, dictation, sorting cards, or a spoken walkthrough. Learners with allergies, medical diets, religious or cultural food practices, or limited food access plan with the foods actually available to them, and any example may be substituted.

## Unit 6: Decisions, Goals, and Health Advocacy

**Unit ID:** `ma-g4-health-u06`
**Standards:** Michigan Health Practice 4: Decision Making and Problem Solving; Michigan Health Practice 5: Self-Management and Goal Setting; Michigan Health Practice 6: Advocacy and Health Promotion; Michigan Health Practice 1: Self-Awareness and Analyzing Influences; Michigan Health Topic (Grades 3-5): Community and Environmental Health
**Essential question:** How do I make a sound decision, set a goal I can reach, and advocate for health beyond myself?
**Performance task:** Set a private-safe personal health goal with a plan and a progress check, and produce an advocacy piece supported by a reliable source. Goals may not target body weight, body size, or appearance.
**Adapted alternative:** Goal tracking may be a checklist, a spoken check-in, a drawing, or an adult-recorded note. The advocacy piece may be a poster, letter, slide, spoken message, or described plan. No public posting, publication, or performance is required.
