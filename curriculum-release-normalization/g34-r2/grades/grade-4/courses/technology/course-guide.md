# Grade 4 Technology and Computer Science — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g4-tech-cs`
**Version:** 1.0.0
**Instructional sessions:** 36
**Typical session:** 40–60
**Cadence:** weekly

## Course description

A digital-literacy and computer-science course building on Grade 3 foundations with deeper privacy practice, troubleshooting, productivity tools, block-based algorithms, data and media literacy, and design thinking.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A user-centered prototype with an algorithm, a test log, an accessibility check, and a presentation.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Digital Citizenship, Privacy, and Kindness Online | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing, Michigan Computer Science: Networks and the Internet | Create a digital-safety guide for a younger learner using fictional examples and no real credentials. |
| 2 | How Computers Work | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Computing Systems | Write a simple troubleshooting flowchart for a common device problem. |
| 3 | Productivity and Collaboration Tools | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Computing Systems, Michigan Computer Science: Data and Analysis | Create an accessible one-page document or short slide set on a class topic. |
| 4 | Algorithms, Loops, and Debugging | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Algorithms and Programming | Build a short story, animation, or simple game using block-based logic and document the algorithm. |
| 5 | Data, Media, and Checking Sources | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Data and Analysis, Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing | Create a short data story with a chart, cited or original media, and a source-check note. |
| 6 | Design Thinking for Real Users | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Algorithms and Programming, Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing | Design, test, and improve a simple prototype that helps a real or fictional user, with a short presentation. |

A user-centered prototype with an algorithm, a test log, an accessibility check, and a presentation.

The capstone may be presented publicly, privately, in writing, orally, through a demonstration, or in another accessible format that preserves the learning targets.
