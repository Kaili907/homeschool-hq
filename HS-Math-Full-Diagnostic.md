**Both teens take this. It replaces the Algebra I Mastery Check as the FIRST math test** (keep that one — it becomes the deeper probe if Section 3 lands in the middle). Untimed, no calculator, show all work. One rule to tell them clearly: **if you genuinely don't know how to start a problem, write SKIP.** A skip tells us exactly where the gap is; a lucky guess hides it. Skips cost nothing — this test can't be failed, it can only be honest or dishonest.

## Placement Decision Tree — read top down, stop at the first line that's true

**Section 1 below 7/10 → foundations come first, full stop.** Nothing above works on broken fractions/decimals/negatives. Plan: a 4–6 week foundations intensive before (or heavily alongside) any course work. This is the single most valuable thing this test can find — most "bad at algebra" is actually this.

**Section 1 fine, Section 2 below 6/8 → pre-algebra layer.** A 3–4 week targeted review of the missed skills (equations, proportions, exponents), then forward.

**Sections 1–2 fine, Section 3 below 6/10 → Algebra I wasn't retained.** She restarts there — but as a fast-moving retake, not a full slog: with 1-on-1 teaching and no broken foundation, an Algebra I rebuild runs 10–14 weeks. *Senior note: if she lands here, we re-plan her year together immediately — realistic shape becomes Algebra I rebuild → Geometry, with Algebra II moving to summer or a community-college course. That's still a college-viable path; pretending the gap isn't there is not.*

**Section 3 at 6–8 → the current plan holds.** Algebra bootcamp on the missed items during weeks 1–3, concurrent with starting Geometry. Give the Algebra I Mastery Check now as the deeper probe to pinpoint exactly which topics get bootcamped.

**Section 3 at 9–10 → green light.** Straight into Geometry; Section 4 tells us how much of it she's already met (a strong Section 4 means her Geometry course can move fast and go deep).

## Reading the errors (both girls, all sections)

For every wrong answer, sort it: **gap** (didn't know how — usually a SKIP or nonsense work) vs. **careless** (right method, arithmetic slip — visible in the shown work). Gaps drive the curriculum; careless patterns drive habits (estimate first, check by substitution). That's why the scratch work gets photographed too — the work is more diagnostic than the answers.
