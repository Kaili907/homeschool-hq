### What could go wrong, and what stops it

- **Chemical hazard.** Rubbing alcohol is flammable, its vapour is heavier than air and pools at bench level, and it is harmful if swallowed.
  - What stops it: STAGE A runs with no battery, charger, flame, hob, pilot light, heater, or lamp anywhere in the room. An adult dispenses no more than 30 mL per cup, caps the bottle, and removes it from the room at once. Nothing is ever tasted.
- **Chemical hazard.** The poured alcohol samples - not just the bottle - are the ignition risk, and a shorting battery is an ignition source.
  - What stops it: STAGE A ends when every POURED SAMPLE is down the drain, the cups washed and dried, and the room ventilated ten minutes - not when the bottle is capped. Only then may a battery enter, and STAGE B runs on a different surface.
- **Chemical hazard.** Passing current through salt water is electrolysis: it produces hydrogen at one lead and chlorine gas at the other.
  - What stops it: Each conductivity test is a touch of one to two seconds. Leads are never left standing in a solution. If bubbles form at a lead, disconnect at once, ventilate, and move to the published table.
- **Physical hazard.** Battery testing risks a short circuit that heats the leads.
  - What stops it: Use one 9V battery only - NEVER mains electricity, never a lithium or button cell. Keep the LED in the circuit at all times, tape the terminals before and after use, keep leads apart between tests, and let an adult make every connection.
- **Physical hazard.** Warm water can scald.
  - What stops it: An adult pours every warm sample and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius. Temperature is judged with the thermometer, never with a hand.
