### What could go wrong, and what stops it

- **Physical hazard.** A domestic smoke detector contains a sealed radioactive source and is safe only while it stays intact.
  - What stops it: A smoke detector is never opened, dismantled, or taken apart, and its source is never removed or handled. A working smoke alarm is a safety device and stays installed and intact. This prohibition is not disableable.
- **Physical hazard.** Large numbers of small counters spread and can be a choking hazard.
  - What stops it: Work in a shallow tray, keep counters away from young children, and count them back at the end.
- **Emotional hazard.** Nuclear topics can raise anxiety about weapons or accidents.
  - What stops it: Content stays on decay physics, energy scale, and engineering trade-offs; the learner may pause or redirect to the materials strand at any point.

### Do these steps in this order — do not reorder them

1. Set the shallow tray down and move young children out of the work area before counters come out.
2. Count the 100 counters in and record the starting number.
3. Run each generation, removing and recording before the next throw.
4. Never open, dismantle, or take apart a smoke detector; it is discussed as a phenomenon and never handled as equipment.
5. Count every counter back in and confirm the total before leaving.
