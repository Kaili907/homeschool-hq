**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- The number of atoms of each element is identical before and after a chemical reaction, so mass is conserved.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- Atoms are neither created nor destroyed in chemical reactions; they are rearranged among substances.
- Conservation of atoms is the reason chemical equations must balance and the reason mass calculations work.
- An apparent mass change in an open system means matter crossed the system boundary, most often as a gas.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Claiming mass is lost during combustion without accounting for oxygen consumed and gases released.
- Claiming atoms are created or destroyed in a chemical reaction.
- Concluding that mass is not conserved from an open-container measurement.

**Grade boundary.** Mass-energy equivalence, which does apply to nuclear reactions, is unit 9 work.

**Declared hazards — each one also appears in the learner's safety brief:**

- chemical: Vinegar stings eyes, and the reaction produces carbon dioxide. — mitigation: Wear eye protection and work in a ventilated room. At the quantities fixed below the carbon dioxide is harmless; the quantities are what keep it that way, so they are part of the procedure and not a suggestion.
- physical: A closed bag holding a gas-producing reaction can burst and spray liquid. — mitigation: The closed trial is SEALED COMPLETELY - a part-sealed bag is not a closed system - and is made safe by volume: half a teaspoon of baking soda and 20 mL of vinegar in a one-litre bag. Flat on a tray, pointed away, never held.

**Safe order the learner is given — read it before the session starts:**

- Work over a tray in a ventilated room with eye protection on.
- Run the OPEN system first: weigh the cup of vinegar and the separate baking soda, combine them, and re-weigh once fizzing has stopped.
- For the CLOSED system measure exactly: no more than half a teaspoon of baking soda and 20 mL of vinegar, in a one-litre freezer bag with the air pressed out.
- SEAL THE BAG COMPLETELY. A part-sealed bag is not a closed system and cannot answer this unit's question; the small quantity and the free space, not a loose seal, are what keep it safe.
- Lay the sealed bag flat on the tray at arm's length, pointed away from every face. Never hold, squeeze, or lift it while it reacts.
- Weigh it without lifting it off the tray, and wait until fizzing has completely stopped before opening a corner.

**Protective equipment:** Eye protection: required.

**Disposal and clearing up:** Open the bag by one corner only, over the sink, away from every face; pour the contents down the drain with the tap running and rinse the bag before recycling or binning it. Wipe the tray and wash hands. Never re-seal a bag that still holds gas, and never store a sealed bag from this investigation.
