### What could go wrong, and what stops it

- **Physical hazard.** Swallowed magnets are a surgical emergency: two or more magnets, or a magnet and any swallowed metal object, attract through the wall of the gut and cut off its blood supply.
  - What stops it: Count the magnets out and back in every session, work over a tray, never bring one near a mouth, and store them closed and labelled out of reach of children and pets. A suspected swallow is an emergency: go at once.
- **Physical hazard.** A wire connected across a battery heats rapidly and can burn skin or start a fire, and a nail is a magnetic core rather than a resistance.
  - What stops it: Use at least 1 m of thin 28-30 AWG enamelled wire so the coil has real resistance; a short or thick wire is a short circuit even with a nail through it. One 1.5 V cell only - NEVER mains, lithium, or button cell.
- **Physical hazard.** A multimeter left on a current range and placed across a battery ruptures its fuse and can arc.
  - What stops it: The multimeter stays on a voltage range for every measurement in this investigation. A current range is never placed across a cell.
- **Physical hazard.** Strong neodymium magnets pinch skin severely and destroy electronics and cards.
  - What stops it: Handle magnets one at a time, keep them well away from phones, laptops, bank cards, and any medical device such as a pacemaker, and store them separated.

### Do these steps in this order — do not reorder them

1. Count the magnets and record the total before anything else, and keep them on a tray so none can reach the floor.
2. Move phones, laptops, and bank cards well away, and keep anyone with an implanted medical device out of the work area.
3. Build the coil with at least 1 m of thin enamelled wire so it has real resistance; the nail is a core, not a resistance.
4. An adult makes every connection, with the coil always in the circuit, for a few seconds at a time, disconnecting between trials.
5. Keep the multimeter on a voltage range throughout; a current range is never placed across a cell.
6. Count every magnet back into its closed labelled box and disconnect the cell before anyone leaves the work area.

## The science information and exact work for this lesson

Everything required below is printed on this sheet. You do not need an unseen case, model, table, prior answer, or outside source.

### Science brief

- Science brief: a voltage is induced in a circuit only when the magnetic flux through it is changing.
- Science brief: relative motion between a conductor and a magnetic field, or a changing field strength, changes the flux and induces a voltage.
- Science brief: a steady magnetic field with no relative motion induces nothing, however strong it is.
- Science brief: the induced effect opposes the change that produced it, which is why generators require work to turn.

### Evidence check: electromagnetic induction

**Candidate claim:** A case report presents this as acceptable science: “a stationary magnet held near a stationary coil induces a steady voltage.”
