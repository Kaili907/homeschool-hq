**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- Electric fields exert forces on charges whether or not they are moving; magnetic fields exert forces only on moving charges and on magnetic materials.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- A field is a model in which one object modifies the space around it, and a second object responds to the field at its own location.
- The electric field direction is defined as the direction of the force on a positive test charge.
- Magnetic field lines form closed loops; isolated magnetic poles have never been observed.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Claiming a magnetic field exerts a force on a stationary charge.
- Claiming isolated magnetic north or south poles can be produced by cutting a magnet.
- Treating field lines as physical objects rather than a representation.

**Grade boundary.** Maxwell's equations, field energy density, and relativistic field transformation are beyond this course.

## Guardian safety record

- Supervision level: `direct-adult`
- Guardian visibility: `confirmation-required`
- Safety completeness check: `VERIFIED`

**Declared hazards — each one also appears in the learner's safety brief:**

- physical: Swallowed magnets are a surgical emergency: two or more magnets, or a magnet and any swallowed metal object, attract through the wall of the gut and cut off its blood supply. — mitigation: Count the magnets out and back in every session, work over a tray, never bring one near a mouth, and store them closed and labelled out of reach of children and pets. A suspected swallow is an emergency: go at once.
- physical: A wire connected across a battery heats rapidly and can burn skin or start a fire, and a nail is a magnetic core rather than a resistance. — mitigation: Use at least 1 m of thin 28-30 AWG enamelled wire so the coil has real resistance; a short or thick wire is a short circuit even with a nail through it. One 1.5 V cell only - NEVER mains, lithium, or button cell.
- physical: A multimeter left on a current range and placed across a battery ruptures its fuse and can arc. — mitigation: The multimeter stays on a voltage range for every measurement in this investigation. A current range is never placed across a cell.
- physical: Strong neodymium magnets pinch skin severely and destroy electronics and cards. — mitigation: Handle magnets one at a time, keep them well away from phones, laptops, bank cards, and any medical device such as a pacemaker, and store them separated.

**Guardian visibility note:** Share the lesson target, completion state, evidence type, and next instructional step. For any hands-on lesson, share the hazard list, the supervision level, the stop conditions, the safe order, the required PPE, and the disposal steps - including any step that runs unattended or overnight - BEFORE the session so the guardian can review them in advance. Do not expose raw reflections, raw answers, or diagnosis language.

**Safe order the learner is given — read it before the session starts:**

- Count the magnets and record the total before anything else, and keep them on a tray so none can reach the floor.
- Move phones, laptops, and bank cards well away, and keep anyone with an implanted medical device out of the work area.
- Build the coil with at least 1 m of thin enamelled wire so it has real resistance; the nail is a core, not a resistance.
- An adult makes every connection, with the coil always in the circuit, for a few seconds at a time, disconnecting between trials.
- Keep the multimeter on a voltage range throughout; a current range is never placed across a cell.
- Count every magnet back into its closed labelled box and disconnect the cell before anyone leaves the work area.

**Protective equipment:** Eye protection: not required.

**Disposal and clearing up:** Disconnect and remove the cell, tape its terminals, and store used cells in a labelled container for household battery recycling - never loose in a drawer, never in the general bin, and never near steel wool. Magnets go back into a closed labelled box, counted, out of reach of children and pets. Wire and nail are put away. No lithium or button cell is used anywhere in this investigation.

**Stop conditions on record, adult wording:**

- Stop and account for every magnet before anyone leaves the work area.
- Stop immediately and disconnect if the wire, battery, or nail becomes warm.
- Stop if a magnet approaches any electronic device, anyone with an implanted medical device, or anyone's mouth.
- Never connect any part of this investigation to a wall outlet.
- Stop for any injury, burn, spill, fume, or allergic reaction and tell the supervising adult.
- Stop if a material, tool, or step is not the one this lesson specifies.
- Burn: cool it under running cool water for 20 minutes. Do not use ice, butter, or ointment.
- Splash in an eye: rinse with running water for 15 minutes, holding the eyelid open, before anything else.
- Fumes or a strong smell: leave the room, open a window from outside the room, and do not go back in to tidy up.
- Fire: do not use water. Get everyone out, close the door, and call the emergency number. Smother a very small contained flame with a metal pan lid or a fire blanket only if that is safe to do without reaching over it.
- If anyone may have swallowed a magnet, a battery, or any material from a lesson, treat it as an emergency and seek medical help at once. Do not wait for symptoms.
- A pause, break, or switch to the alternative activity is never treated as failure.
