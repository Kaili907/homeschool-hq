**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- A free-body diagram shows only the forces acting on the chosen object, not the forces that object exerts on others.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- Each force on the diagram has an identified source: another object that is in contact with, or interacting at a distance with, the chosen body.
- The two forces of an interaction pair act on different objects and therefore never appear on the same free-body diagram.
- Motion itself is not a force, so no arrow is drawn for the object's own movement.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Drawing a force of motion, a force of inertia, or a force the object carries with it.
- Including on one diagram both members of an action-reaction pair.
- Drawing a force with no identifiable source object.

**Grade boundary.** Fictitious forces in rotating frames are beyond this course.
