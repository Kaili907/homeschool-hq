### What could go wrong, and what stops it

- **Chemical hazard.** 3% hydrogen peroxide irritates eyes and can bleach fabric and skin on contact.
  - What stops it: Use the household 3% strength only, keep it in its original bottle, wear eye protection, work over a washable surface, and rinse any splash with water for 15 minutes.
- **Chemical hazard.** Hydrogen peroxide and lemon juice must never be combined; together they make peracetic acid, which is corrosive and irritating to the airway.
  - What stops it: The lemon-juice route and the peroxide route run in separate labelled cups. They are never poured together, never poured into a shared waste cup, and never combined to see what happens.
- **Chemical hazard.** Catalase and peroxide foam vigorously and release oxygen.
  - What stops it: No lid, cap, film, or cover ever goes on a peroxide container. Use a wide open cup on a tray with plenty of headroom.
- **Physical hazard.** Slicing produce with a kitchen knife risks cuts.
  - What stops it: An adult does all cutting, or the learner uses pre-cut produce; no knife work is required by the lesson.

### Do these steps in this order — do not reorder them

1. Put on eye protection and lay everything out on a washable tray before any container is opened.

3. Run the no-chemical browning comparison first - air, water, wrapped - and record it before any chemical appears.

5. Keep the lemon-juice cups and the peroxide cups apart on the tray. Never pour one into the other, and never put a lid on a peroxide cup.

## The science information and exact work for this lesson

Everything required below is printed on this sheet. You do not need an unseen case, model, table, prior answer, or outside source.

### Science brief

- Science brief: typical bacterial cells are on the order of micrometres; typical eukaryotic cells are tens of micrometres; molecules such as proteins are on the order of nanometres.
- Science brief: biological structures span many orders of magnitude, and the appropriate unit and instrument change with the scale being studied.
- Science brief: the scale of a structure constrains what can be observed about it: light microscopy resolves cells and large organelles, not individual proteins.
- Science brief: a measurement is meaningful only with its unit and the method that produced it.
