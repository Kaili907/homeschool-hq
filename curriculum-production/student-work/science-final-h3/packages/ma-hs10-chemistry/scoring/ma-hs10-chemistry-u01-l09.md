**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- Repeated trials estimate random uncertainty; they do not detect or remove systematic error.
- A systematic error shifts every trial in the same direction, so tightly agreeing results can still all be wrong.
- A reported result states the number of trials and the spread among them, or a reader cannot judge it.

**Also correct.** Accept any of these framings — do not require the wording above:

- Describing the two error types as random and systematic, or as scatter and bias, are equivalent.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Claiming repeated trials eliminate systematic error.
- Discarding an outlying trial without recording that it was discarded and why.
- Reporting a mean with no indication of spread or number of trials.

**Grade boundary.** Standard deviation, confidence intervals, and outlier tests are beyond this course.
