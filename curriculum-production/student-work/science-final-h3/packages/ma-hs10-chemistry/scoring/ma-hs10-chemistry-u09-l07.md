**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- The atomic number is the number of protons and identifies the element; the mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- Isotopes of an element share the proton number and differ in neutron number, and therefore in mass number.
- Isotopes of an element have essentially identical chemical behaviour, because chemistry depends on electrons, while their nuclear stability can differ greatly.
- The mass listed on the periodic table is a weighted average over an element's naturally occurring isotopes.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Claiming isotopes of an element differ chemically in a substantial way.
- Claiming the mass number is the mass in grams.
- Claiming an element's isotopes have different proton numbers.

**Grade boundary.** Nuclear shell models and binding energy per nucleon curves are acceptable to reference but not required.

**Declared hazards — each one also appears in the learner's safety brief:**

- physical: A domestic smoke detector contains a sealed radioactive source and is safe only while it stays intact. — mitigation: A smoke detector is never opened, dismantled, or taken apart, and its source is never removed or handled. A working smoke alarm is a safety device and stays installed and intact. This prohibition is not disableable.
- physical: Large numbers of small counters spread and can be a choking hazard. — mitigation: Work in a shallow tray, keep counters away from young children, and count them back at the end.
- emotional: Nuclear topics can raise anxiety about weapons or accidents. — mitigation: Content stays on decay physics, energy scale, and engineering trade-offs; the learner may pause or redirect to the materials strand at any point.

**Guardian visibility note:** Share the lesson target, completion state, evidence type, and next instructional step. For any hands-on lesson, share the hazard list, the supervision level, the stop conditions, the safe order, the required PPE, and the disposal steps - including any step that runs unattended or overnight - BEFORE the session so the guardian can review them in advance. Do not expose raw reflections, raw answers, or diagnosis language.

**Safe order the learner is given — read it before the session starts:**

- Set the shallow tray down and move young children out of the work area before counters come out.
- Count the 100 counters in and record the starting number.
- Run each generation, removing and recording before the next throw.
- Never open, dismantle, or take apart a smoke detector; it is discussed as a phenomenon and never handled as equipment.
- Count every counter back in and confirm the total before leaving.

**Protective equipment:** Eye protection: not required.

**Disposal and clearing up:** Counters go back into a labelled container stored out of reach of young children. No radioactive source is used or acquired at any point and no smoke detector is opened, so there is nothing hazardous to dispose of.

**Stop conditions on record, adult wording:**

- Fire: do not use water. Get everyone out, close the door, and call the emergency number. Smother a very small contained flame with a metal pan lid or a fire blanket only if that is safe to do without reaching over it.
