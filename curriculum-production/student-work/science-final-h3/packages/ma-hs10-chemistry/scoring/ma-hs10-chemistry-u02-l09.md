**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- Valence electrons are the electrons in the outermost energy level, and they are the electrons involved in bonding.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- The number of valence electrons largely determines an element's chemical behaviour, which is why elements in the same main group behave similarly.
- Atoms tend toward outer-electron arrangements resembling those of the noble gases, by transferring, sharing, or delocalising electrons.
- For main-group elements the valence electron count follows from the group: groups 1 and 2 have one and two, and groups 13 to 18 have the group number minus ten, with helium the exception at two.

**Also correct.** Accept any of these framings — do not require the wording above:

- The octet rule and the noble-gas configuration description are equivalent framings; both have known exceptions.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Counting all of an atom's electrons as valence electrons.
- Claiming the octet rule holds without exception.
- Claiming atoms bond in order to satisfy a preference or a need of their own.

**Grade boundary.** Transition metal electron configurations and hypervalency are beyond this course.
