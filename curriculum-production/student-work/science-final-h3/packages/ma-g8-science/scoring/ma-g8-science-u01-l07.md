**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- An object's motion changes only when a net force acts on it.
- Forces come in pairs: when object A pushes on object B, B pushes back on A with equal strength in the opposite direction.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion keeps the same speed and direction, unless a net force acts on it.
- A net force changes an object's motion, and for a given force a more massive object changes motion less.
- Forces always act in pairs between two objects, equal in size and opposite in direction, and the two forces act on different objects.

**Also correct.** Accept any of these framings — do not require the wording above:

- Naming the laws by number, or describing them in plain terms, are both correct; the numbering is not required.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Claiming a force is needed to keep an object moving at constant velocity.
- Claiming the two forces of an interaction pair act on the same object and therefore cancel.
- Claiming a heavier object always pushes harder than a lighter one it collides with.

**Grade boundary.** Vector decomposition, quantitative F equals ma problems, and frames of reference are High School work; correct use of them is not an error.
