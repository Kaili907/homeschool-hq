**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- Temperature measures the average kinetic energy of particles; thermal energy is the total energy of that random motion and also depends on how much material there is.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- Two objects at the same temperature can hold very different amounts of thermal energy, because thermal energy depends on mass and material as well.
- Adding thermal energy to a substance raises its temperature, except during a change of state, when it changes particle arrangement instead.
- Temperature determines the direction of spontaneous thermal transfer; total thermal energy does not.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Treating temperature and thermal energy as the same quantity.
- Claiming the object with more thermal energy is necessarily at the higher temperature.
- Claiming temperature rises steadily while a substance is melting or boiling.

**Grade boundary.** Absolute temperature scales beyond conversion, and kinetic theory derivations, are beyond this course.
