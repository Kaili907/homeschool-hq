## Safety — read this before you touch anything

Read this before you touch any material. If anything here does not match what is in front of you, stop and ask the supervising adult.

### What could go wrong, and what stops it

- **Physical hazard.** Warm water can scald.
  - What stops it: Keep all water below 50 degrees Celsius, an adult fills the container, and the learner never handles boiling water.
- **Emotional hazard.** Health and body topics can be distressing when they touch personal or family medical experience.
  - What stops it: Examples use non-human systems and published cases only; the learner is never asked about their own or a family member's health, and may skip any example without penalty.

### Do these steps in this order — do not reorder them

1. An adult mixes the warm water and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius before the learner touches the container.
2. Set both plants, or both thermal containers, side by side before the timer starts.
3. Read every measurement from outside the container; the learner never carries an open container of warm water.
4. Stop at the scheduled end and let everything cool before it is moved.

### Stop at once if any of these happens

- Stop if the thermometer reads above 50 degrees Celsius. Water temperature is judged with the thermometer, never with a hand.
- Pause and move to the alternative if a health example becomes distressing.
- Stop for any injury, burn, spill, fume, or allergic reaction and tell the supervising adult.
- Stop if a material, tool, or step is not the one this lesson specifies.
- Burn: cool it under running cool water for 20 minutes. Do not use ice, butter, or ointment.
- Splash in an eye: rinse with running water for 15 minutes, holding the eyelid open, before anything else.
- Fumes or a strong smell: leave the room, open a window from outside the room, and do not go back in to tidy up.
- Fire: do not use water. Get everyone out, close the door, and call the emergency number. Smother a very small contained flame with a metal pan lid or a fire blanket only if that is safe to do without reaching over it.
- If anyone may have swallowed a magnet, a battery, or any material from a lesson, treat it as an emergency and seek medical help at once. Do not wait for symptoms.
- A pause, break, or switch to the alternative activity is never treated as failure.
