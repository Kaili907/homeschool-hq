**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- Exponential growth has no limiting factor and cannot be sustained; logistic growth slows as the population approaches carrying capacity.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- A population grows exponentially when resources are effectively unlimited, with growth rate proportional to population size.
- As resources become limiting, per-individual growth rate declines and the curve levels off near the carrying capacity.
- No real population grows exponentially indefinitely, because no environment supplies unlimited resources.

**Also correct.** Accept any of these framings — do not require the wording above:

- Describing the curves as J-shaped and S-shaped is correct and equivalent.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Claiming a population can sustain exponential growth indefinitely.
- Claiming logistic growth means the population stops changing once it reaches carrying capacity.
- Confusing the growth rate with the population size.

**Grade boundary.** Differential equation forms, intrinsic rate of increase, and age-structured models are beyond this course.
