**Declared hazards — each one also appears in the learner's safety brief:**

- chemical: Rubbing alcohol is flammable, its vapour is heavier than air and pools at bench level, and it is harmful if swallowed. — mitigation: STAGE A runs with no battery, charger, flame, hob, pilot light, heater, or lamp anywhere in the room. An adult dispenses no more than 30 mL per cup, caps the bottle, and removes it from the room at once. Nothing is ever tasted.
- chemical: The poured alcohol samples - not just the bottle - are the ignition risk, and a shorting battery is an ignition source. — mitigation: STAGE A ends when every POURED SAMPLE is down the drain, the cups washed and dried, and the room ventilated ten minutes - not when the bottle is capped. Only then may a battery enter, and STAGE B runs on a different surface.
- chemical: Passing current through salt water is electrolysis: it produces hydrogen at one lead and chlorine gas at the other. — mitigation: Each conductivity test is a touch of one to two seconds. Leads are never left standing in a solution. If bubbles form at a lead, disconnect at once, ventilate, and move to the published table.
- physical: Battery testing risks a short circuit that heats the leads. — mitigation: Use one 9V battery only - NEVER mains electricity, never a lithium or button cell. Keep the LED in the circuit at all times, tape the terminals before and after use, keep leads apart between tests, and let an adult make every connection.
- physical: Warm water can scald. — mitigation: An adult pours every warm sample and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius. Temperature is judged with the thermometer, never with a hand.

**Stop conditions on record, adult wording:**

- Stop and put the battery back in its packaging if any cup, spill, or smell of alcohol is still in the room.
- Never dip a lead, electrode, or wire into alcohol or into any liquid that is not on the STAGE B list.
- Stop and disconnect if bubbles appear at a lead, if a lead or the battery warms, or if the LED stays lit after a test.
- Stop if the thermometer reads above 50 degrees Celsius.
- Never connect anything in this lesson to a wall outlet.
- Stop for any injury, burn, spill, fume, or allergic reaction and tell the supervising adult.
- Stop if a material, tool, or step is not the one this lesson specifies.
- Burn: cool it under running cool water for 20 minutes. Do not use ice, butter, or ointment.
- Splash in an eye: rinse with running water for 15 minutes, holding the eyelid open, before anything else.
- Fumes or a strong smell: leave the room, open a window from outside the room, and do not go back in to tidy up.
- Fire: do not use water. Get everyone out, close the door, and call the emergency number. Smother a very small contained flame with a metal pan lid or a fire blanket only if that is safe to do without reaching over it.
- If anyone may have swallowed a magnet, a battery, or any material from a lesson, treat it as an emergency and seek medical help at once. Do not wait for symptoms.
- A pause, break, or switch to the alternative activity is never treated as failure.
