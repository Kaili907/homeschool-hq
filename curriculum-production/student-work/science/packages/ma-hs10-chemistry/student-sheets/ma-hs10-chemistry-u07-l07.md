### What could go wrong, and what stops it

- **Chemical hazard.** A glow stick holds an internal glass ampoule, irritant esters, and hydrogen peroxide.
  - What stops it: Bend to activate; never cut, bite, open, or mouth a glow stick. If one splits, nobody touches it: rinse any contact 15 minutes and an adult bags and bins it. This prohibition is not disableable.
- **Chemical hazard.** Vinegar and effervescent tablets irritate eyes.
  - What stops it: Wear eye protection, work over a tray, and rinse splashes with water.
- **Physical hazard.** Warm water can scald and glass can break with thermal shock.
  - What stops it: An adult pours every warm sample and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius. Use plastic or unbreakable cups only.

### Do these steps in this order — do not reorder them

1. Bend the glow stick to activate it; never cut, bite, or open one, and never put one in a mouth.
2. An adult prepares the warm water and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius before any cup is placed.
3. Put on eye protection and set every cup on a tray.
4. Run one condition at a time, from cold to warm, recording each timing before anything is changed.
5. Never cap, seal, or cover a cup while it is fizzing.
