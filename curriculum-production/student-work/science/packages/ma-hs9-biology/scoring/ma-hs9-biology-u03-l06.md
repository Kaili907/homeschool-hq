**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- As an object grows with its shape unchanged, its volume increases faster than its surface area, so the surface-area-to-volume ratio falls.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- Exchange with the environment happens across the surface, while demand scales with volume, so cells are limited in the size they can reach.
- Multicellular organisms overcome the limit with many small cells and with structures such as folds, villi, and branching that increase surface area.
- The ratio explains why cells divide rather than simply growing larger.

**Also correct.** Accept any of these framings — do not require the wording above:

- Explaining the constraint through diffusion distance, or through the ratio of surface to volume, are both correct.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Claiming surface area and volume increase in the same proportion as an object grows.
- Claiming a larger cell exchanges materials more efficiently than a smaller one.
- Claiming the ratio increases as a cell grows.

**Grade boundary.** Quantitative diffusion modelling and allometric scaling laws are beyond this course.
