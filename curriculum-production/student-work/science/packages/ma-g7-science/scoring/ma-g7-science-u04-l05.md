**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- A microscope image is a two-dimensional view of a thin slice or a thin sample, so what is seen depends on how the sample was prepared.
- Magnification and the scale bar are needed before any size can be read from an image.
- Stains and preparation can introduce features that are artefacts rather than parts of the living cell.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Reading a size off a micrograph with no scale or magnification given.
- Claiming a structure is absent because it was not visible in one preparation.
- Treating a preparation artefact as a cell structure.

**Grade boundary.** Resolution limits, electron microscopy, and staining chemistry are High School work.
