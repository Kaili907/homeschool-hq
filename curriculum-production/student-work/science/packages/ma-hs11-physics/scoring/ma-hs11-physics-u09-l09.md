**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- Digital signals encode information in discrete values; analog signals vary continuously.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- Noise added to an analog signal becomes part of it, while a digital signal can be restored by rounding each value to the nearest allowed level.
- That restorability is why digital signals survive repeated transmission and copying far better than analog ones.
- Digitising a continuous signal involves sampling and rounding, so it loses some information; how much depends on the sampling and the levels used.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Claiming digital signals are unaffected by noise or loss.
- Claiming digitisation preserves the original signal exactly.
- Claiming an analog signal can be cleaned of accumulated noise as a digital one can.

**Grade boundary.** Sampling theorem, quantisation error analysis, and coding theory are beyond this course.
