**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- Mechanical waves require a medium; electromagnetic waves do not.
- Sound travels faster in solids than in liquids and faster in liquids than in gases.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- The speed of a mechanical wave is set by properties of the medium, principally its stiffness and its density.
- Light slows on entering a denser transparent medium and returns to its vacuum speed on leaving.
- The wave's frequency is a property of the source and does not change with the medium.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Claiming sound travels faster in air than in water or steel.
- Claiming sound can travel through a vacuum.
- Claiming light travels faster in glass or water than in vacuum.

**Grade boundary.** Acoustic impedance and refractive index calculation are acceptable but not required.

**Investigation day — the key bounds the conclusion, never the observations.**

This is an investigation day, so the key above bounds the learner's conclusion and never their observations. Do not score any recorded measurement against it.
