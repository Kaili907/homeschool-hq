**Fixed for this topic.** These are settled, and work that contradicts one is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness:

- A digital signal encodes information as discrete values; an analog signal varies continuously.

**Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- An analog signal varies continuously and any noise added to it becomes part of the signal.
- A digital signal takes discrete values, so small amounts of noise can be removed by restoring each value to the nearest allowed one.
- That is why digital signals can be copied and transmitted many times with far less degradation than analog ones.

**Also correct.** Accept any of these framings — do not require the wording above:

- Describing digital encoding as binary, or as discrete levels, are both correct.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Claiming digital signals are immune to all noise and loss.
- Claiming digital means the information itself is not physical.
- Claiming an analog signal can be restored to its original form after noise is added, as a digital one can.

**Grade boundary.** Sampling theory, bit depth, and error-correcting codes are High School work.
