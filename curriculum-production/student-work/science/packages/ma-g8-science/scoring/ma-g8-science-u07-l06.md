## There is no answer key, and that is deliberate

Science work in this course is scored against criteria, not against a model response. **Accepted relationships and models.** What this lesson's learning target asserts:

- Environmental burdens and benefits are not distributed evenly across communities, and the pattern is documented rather than assumed.
- Who is exposed, who decides, and who benefits are separate questions, and a full account addresses all three.
- Evidence about distribution comes from data on exposure, health, and access, not from assertion.

**Disqualifying errors.** Each of these is `Not yet` on Scientific correctness however well the reasoning is documented and however complete the evidence is:

- Claiming environmental burdens fall equally on all communities.
- Treating who is affected as outside the scope of the scientific evidence.
- Making a distributional claim with no data behind it.

**Grade boundary.** Policy analysis and formal equity frameworks are High School work.

## Rubric

The full four-level rubric is in `policy/scoring-and-safety-policy.md` and is printed in full on the learner's own sheet. It is scored against this lesson's success criteria:

- The learner completes the central task about environmental justice foundations.
