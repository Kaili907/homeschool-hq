### What could go wrong, and what stops it

- **Physical hazard.** Balloons pop and are a choking hazard for young children.
  - What stops it: Keep fragments away from young children; anyone with a latex allergy uses the elastic-band model.
- **Emotional hazard.** Cosmological scale and deep time can be unsettling for some learners.
  - What stops it: Pace the content, keep the focus on evidence and reasoning, and allow the learner to pause at any point.

### Do these steps in this order — do not reorder them

1. Ask about latex allergy before a balloon is opened; if anyone present has one, use the elastic-band model instead.
2. Mark the dots on the deflated balloon and measure their spacing before inflating.
3. Inflate in stages, measuring after each stage, keeping the balloon away from every face.
4. Deflate it deliberately rather than popping it.

### Stop at once if any of these happens

- Switch to the elastic-band model if anyone present has a latex allergy.
- Pause if the scale content becomes distressing.
- Stop for any injury, burn, spill, fume, or allergic reaction and tell the supervising adult.
- Stop if a material, tool, or step is not the one this lesson specifies.

- Fire: do not use water. Get everyone out, close the door, and call the emergency number. Smother a very small contained flame with a metal pan lid or a fire blanket only if that is safe to do without reaching over it.
