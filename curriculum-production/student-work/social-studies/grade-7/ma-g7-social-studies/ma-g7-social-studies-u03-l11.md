# Unit assessment: writing law and religion

**Lesson ID:** `ma-g7-social-studies-u03-l11`
**Course:** ma-g7-social-studies -- Grade 7
**Unit 3:** Era 2: Early Civilizations and Pastoral Peoples
**Day 11 of unit -- Phase:** Unit assessment
**Standards:** Michigan Grade 7 W2, G4
**Essential question:** How can understanding era 2: early civilizations and pastoral peoples help us explain, decide, create, or solve something that matters?
**Materials:** course notebook or digital equivalent; pencil or accessible response tool; unit-specific source, model, manipulatives, safe materials, or approved digital tool

## 1. Source / evidence task

**Task shape for this phase (Unit assessment):** work with the sources required by the unit assessment prompts.

This package names real repositories rather than a specific pre-selected document. Before this lesson is used with a student, a teacher/tutor attaches one or more real, dated, citable items from:
- Library of Congress World Digital Collections
- Fordham University Internet History Sourcebooks
- David Rumsey Historical Map Collection
If no suitable item has been attached yet, the student notebook must say so plainly rather than substituting an imagined source.

**Primary vs. secondary:** For every source used, the learner names whether it is primary (made at/near the time by someone present or directly involved) or secondary (created later, analyzing or describing the event), and gives one reason for the label.

## 2. Map / data / document analysis

Using the Source Record below, log every source consulted for "writing law and religion" (unit: Era 2: Early Civilizations and Pastoral Peoples).

- Creator or organization
- Title
- Date
- Where it was found
- Original source or repost?
- Evidence used

Then apply the Claim–Evidence–Reasoning frame: state a claim about writing law and religion, cite the specific evidence from the logged source(s), explain the reasoning that connects them, and name one limit -- what the evidence does not show or where it might be biased or incomplete.

Tie the analysis back to the unit's essential question: "How can understanding era 2: early civilizations and pastoral peoples help us explain, decide, create, or solve something that matters?"

## 3. Independent response

Complete the unit assessment prompts independently, in the order given, showing sourced evidence for every prompt that asks for it:

- **concept and vocabulary** (4 pts): Explain agricultural revolution in your own words and give or identify a valid example.
- **representation or source** (5 pts): Use a diagram, model, quotation, data display, map, demonstration, or worked process to show understanding of river-valley civilizations.
- **application** (6 pts): Apply pastoral societies in a new realistic situation. Show the process or cite evidence.
- **error or claim analysis** (6 pts): Analyze a plausible error, weak claim, unsafe choice, or design flaw involving social hierarchy; correct it and explain why.
- **connection** (5 pts): Connect agricultural revolution with trade and environmental change. Explain how the ideas support, constrain, contrast with, or build on one another.
- **performance evidence** (8 pts): Present the strongest evidence from the unit project: Compare how two early societies organized power, resources, belief, and daily life.
- **reflection and transfer** (4 pts): Identify one skill you can now transfer, one check that improves accuracy or quality, and one next question.

Total: 38 points. Rubric dimensions: accuracy or fidelity, evidence and reasoning, application or performance, checking and revision.

Mastery interpretation -- Secure: At least 85% with accurate independent application and adequate evidence/reasoning. Developing: 70–84% or inconsistent explanation; assign targeted review and a fresh transfer check. Not yet: Below 70% or a missing prerequisite; reteach the smallest gap and reassess with new evidence.

## 4. Evidence requirements

- Cite the sources required by the unit assessment prompts, each logged with the Source Record fields above.
- Every claim is backed by a specific, cited piece of evidence -- not an unsupported opinion.
- For every source used, the learner names whether it is primary (made at/near the time by someone present or directly involved) or secondary (created later, analyzing or describing the event), and gives one reason for the label.
- Every claim used as evidence names its source (creator/organization, title, date, where found) in enough detail that another person could find it again. The learner transcribes any quotation directly from the retrieved source; nothing here is pre-written as though quoted.

## 6. Acceptable-answer criteria

An answer is acceptable when it:
- meets: The learner completes the central task about writing law and religion.
- meets: The learner gives evidence, reasoning, a model, or a documented process rather than an unsupported answer.
- meets: The learner checks or revises the work and can identify a next step.
- Do not mark mastery from one answer. Use source analysis, map or timeline, discussion, evidence-based claim, and inquiry product; require accurate independent evidence and successful transfer or retrieval on at least two occasions when feasible.
- meets the "At least 85% with accurate independent application and adequate evidence/reasoning." bar from the unit assessment, or is scored Developing/Not Yet per: Developing = 70–84% or inconsistent explanation; assign targeted review and a fresh transfer check. Not yet = Below 70% or a missing prerequisite; reteach the smallest gap and reassess with new evidence.
Multiple valid approaches are accepted when they meet the criteria above; do not require one specific phrasing.

## 7. Remediation

Match the observed pattern to a route below. Ask a question or re-model the smallest missing piece; do not supply the corrected answer directly.

- If the learner shows **prerequisite gap**: Return to the smallest prerequisite needed for writing law and religion, model it with a concrete or text-only representation, then retry one fresh item.
- If the learner shows **procedure without understanding**: Ask the learner to explain or represent why the process for writing law and religion works before continuing.
- If the learner shows **correct but low confidence**: Confirm the reasoning specifically, offer one varied example of writing law and religion, and avoid unnecessary remediation.
- If the learner shows **repeated error pattern**: Name the observable pattern neutrally, contrast it with a worked example, and schedule a short same-day or next-session review.
- If the learner shows **mastery evidence**: Require accurate independent application plus explanation on a later or different occasion before marking writing law and religion mastered.

## 8. Extension

Apply writing law and religion under a new constraint, compare two approaches or perspectives, or teach the idea with an original example without completing another learner’s graded work.
