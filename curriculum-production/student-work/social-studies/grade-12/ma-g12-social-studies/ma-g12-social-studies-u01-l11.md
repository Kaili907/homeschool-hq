## 3. Independent response

Complete the unit assessment prompts independently, in the order given, showing sourced evidence for every prompt that asks for it:

- **concept and vocabulary** (4 pts): Explain compelling and supporting questions in your own words and give or identify a valid example.
- **representation or source** (5 pts): Use a map, data display, timeline, document analysis, or worked process to show understanding of research design and feasibility.
- **application** (6 pts): Apply research ethics and consent in a new realistic situation. Show the reasoning and cite the evidence.
- **error or claim analysis** (6 pts): Analyze a plausible error, unsupported claim, or misuse of a source involving literature and source surveys; correct it and explain why.
- **connection** (5 pts): Connect compelling and supporting questions with peer critique of research design. Explain how the ideas support, constrain, contrast with, or build on one another.
- **source provenance and citation** (5 pts): For one source you used, identify it as primary or secondary, state who produced it and when, explain what perspective it represents, name one thing it does not show, and cite it so another reader could find it.
- **performance evidence** (8 pts): Present the strongest evidence from the unit project: Produce a reviewed research proposal with a compelling question, supporting questions, candidate sources, methods, and ethical safeguards.
- **reflection and transfer** (4 pts): Identify one skill you can now transfer, one check that improves accuracy or source reliability, and one next question.

Total: 43 points. Rubric dimensions: accuracy and historical or economic context, evidence, reasoning, and citation, source use, provenance, and perspective, application or performance, checking and revision.

Mastery interpretation -- Secure: At least 85% with accurate independent application, cited evidence, and fair treatment of perspectives. Developing: 70–84% or inconsistent explanation or citation; assign targeted review and a fresh transfer check. Not yet: Below 70% or a missing prerequisite; reteach the smallest gap and reassess with new evidence.

## 6. Acceptable-answer criteria

An answer is acceptable when it:
- meets: The learner completes the central task about documenting a research plan.
- meets: The learner gives cited evidence and reasoning rather than an unsupported answer.
- meets: The learner distinguishes primary from secondary evidence and notes each source's perspective.
- meets: The learner checks or revises the work and can identify a next step.
- Do not mark mastery from one answer. Use source analysis, map or data work, discussion, an evidence-based claim with citation, and an inquiry product; require accurate independent evidence and successful transfer or retrieval on at least two separate occasions.
- meets the "At least 85% with accurate independent application, cited evidence, and fair treatment of perspectives." bar from the unit assessment, or is scored Developing/Not Yet per: Developing = 70–84% or inconsistent explanation or citation; assign targeted review and a fresh transfer check. Not yet = Below 70% or a missing prerequisite; reteach the smallest gap and reassess with new evidence.
Multiple valid approaches are accepted when they meet the criteria above; do not require one specific phrasing.

## 7. Remediation

Match the observed pattern to a route below. Ask a question or re-model the smallest missing piece; do not supply the corrected answer directly.

- If the learner shows **prerequisite gap**: Return to the smallest prerequisite needed for documenting a research plan, model it with a concrete or text-only representation, then retry one fresh item.
- If the learner shows **procedure without understanding**: Ask the learner to explain or represent why the reasoning for documenting a research plan works before continuing.
- If the learner shows **correct but low confidence**: Confirm the reasoning specifically, offer one varied example of documenting a research plan, and avoid unnecessary remediation.
- If the learner shows **repeated error pattern**: Name the observable pattern neutrally, contrast it with a worked example, and schedule a short same-day or next-session review.
- If the learner shows **unsupported or uncited claim**: Ask which source supports the claim and how it was corroborated. Do not supply the claim, the wording, or the citation.
- If the learner shows **request to write the argument**: Decline to draft or revise graded argumentative prose. Offer evidence analysis, questions, structure options, or feedback on the learner's own draft instead.
- If the learner shows **mastery evidence**: Require accurate independent application plus explanation on a later or different occasion before marking documenting a research plan mastered.

## 8. Extension

Apply documenting a research plan under a new constraint, compare two interpretations or regional perspectives, or teach the idea with an original example without completing another learner's graded work.
