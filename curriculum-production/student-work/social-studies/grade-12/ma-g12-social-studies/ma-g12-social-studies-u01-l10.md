| Level | Knowledge and accuracy | Evidence and reasoning | Application and transfer | Checking and revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 -- Secure and transferable | Accurate and complete | Evidence/reasoning is specific, connected, and convincing | Applies independently in a new context or representation | Checks strategically and improves work substantively |

## 6. Acceptable-answer criteria

An answer is acceptable when it:
- meets: The learner completes the central task about literature and source surveys.
- meets: The learner gives cited evidence and reasoning rather than an unsupported answer.
- meets: The learner distinguishes primary from secondary evidence and notes each source's perspective.
- meets: The learner checks or revises the work and can identify a next step.
- Do not mark mastery from one answer. Use source analysis, map or data work, discussion, an evidence-based claim with citation, and an inquiry product; require accurate independent evidence and successful transfer or retrieval on at least two separate occasions.
Multiple valid approaches are accepted when they meet the criteria above; do not require one specific phrasing.

## 7. Remediation

Match the observed pattern to a route below. Ask a question or re-model the smallest missing piece; do not supply the corrected answer directly.

- If the learner shows **prerequisite gap**: Return to the smallest prerequisite needed for literature and source surveys, model it with a concrete or text-only representation, then retry one fresh item.
- If the learner shows **procedure without understanding**: Ask the learner to explain or represent why the reasoning for literature and source surveys works before continuing.
- If the learner shows **correct but low confidence**: Confirm the reasoning specifically, offer one varied example of literature and source surveys, and avoid unnecessary remediation.
- If the learner shows **repeated error pattern**: Name the observable pattern neutrally, contrast it with a worked example, and schedule a short same-day or next-session review.
- If the learner shows **unsupported or uncited claim**: Ask which source supports the claim and how it was corroborated. Do not supply the claim, the wording, or the citation.
- If the learner shows **request to write the argument**: Decline to draft or revise graded argumentative prose. Offer evidence analysis, questions, structure options, or feedback on the learner's own draft instead.
- If the learner shows **mastery evidence**: Require accurate independent application plus explanation on a later or different occasion before marking literature and source surveys mastered.
