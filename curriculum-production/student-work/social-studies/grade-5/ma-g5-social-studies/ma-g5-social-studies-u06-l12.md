**Day 12 of unit -- Phase:** Correction and reflection

**Essential question:** How can understanding colonization and regional development help us explain, decide, create, or solve something that matters?
**Materials:** course notebook or digital equivalent; pencil or accessible response tool; unit-specific source, model, manipulatives, safe materials, or approved digital tool

## 1. Source / evidence task

**Task shape for this phase (Correction and reflection):** work with the source(s) already used in this unit, revisited for revision.

This package names real repositories rather than a specific pre-selected document. Before this lesson is used with a student, a teacher/tutor attaches one or more real, dated, citable items from:
- Library of Congress (loc.gov/collections)
- National Archives DocsTeach (docsteach.org)
- David Rumsey Historical Map Collection
If no suitable item has been attached yet, the student notebook must say so plainly rather than substituting an imagined source.

**Primary vs. secondary:** For every source used, the learner names whether it is primary (made at/near the time by someone present or directly involved) or secondary (created later, analyzing or describing the event), and gives one reason for the label.

## 3. Independent response

Learner completes a new application of daily life and inequality and records both the result and the reasoning, evidence, process, or design choice that produced it.

Exit-ticket check: In one concise response, show or explain the most important idea about daily life and inequality; then identify one check that would catch an error or weak claim.

## 4. Evidence requirements

- Cite the source(s) already used in this unit, revisited for revision, each logged with the Source Record fields above.

## 5. Rubric

| Level | Knowledge and accuracy | Evidence and reasoning | Application and transfer | Checking and revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 -- Secure and transferable | Accurate and complete | Evidence/reasoning is specific, connected, and convincing | Applies independently in a new context or representation | Checks strategically and improves work substantively |
| 3 -- Secure | Mostly accurate with no major misconception | Gives relevant evidence or reasoning | Completes the expected application independently | Uses criteria to correct or clarify |

A Level 1 or 2 is an instructional signal, not a character judgment. Reassess with fresh evidence after targeted teaching. (Source: Manuel Academy shared four-level mastery rubric.)

## 6. Acceptable-answer criteria

An answer is acceptable when it:
- meets: The learner completes the central task about daily life and inequality.
- meets: The learner gives evidence, reasoning, a model, or a documented process rather than an unsupported answer.
- meets: The learner checks or revises the work and can identify a next step.
- Do not mark mastery from one answer. Use source analysis, map or timeline, discussion, evidence-based claim, and inquiry product; require accurate independent evidence and successful transfer or retrieval on at least two occasions when feasible.
Multiple valid approaches are accepted when they meet the criteria above; do not require one specific phrasing.

## 7. Remediation

Match the observed pattern to a route below. Ask a question or re-model the smallest missing piece; do not supply the corrected answer directly.

- If the learner shows **prerequisite gap**: Return to the smallest prerequisite needed for daily life and inequality, model it with a concrete or text-only representation, then retry one fresh item.
- If the learner shows **procedure without understanding**: Ask the learner to explain or represent why the process for daily life and inequality works before continuing.
- If the learner shows **correct but low confidence**: Confirm the reasoning specifically, offer one varied example of daily life and inequality, and avoid unnecessary remediation.
- If the learner shows **repeated error pattern**: Name the observable pattern neutrally, contrast it with a worked example, and schedule a short same-day or next-session review.
- If the learner shows **mastery evidence**: Require accurate independent application plus explanation on a later or different occasion before marking daily life and inequality mastered.
