## 1. Get ready: background and vocabulary

Parliament issued rules for Britain's American colonies. People in the colonies did not respond as one group. This lesson studies an official law and a Boston print, then names the viewpoint evidence that is still missing.

- **protest:** an action or statement that shows disagreement with a rule, decision, or condition
- **loyalism:** continued support for British rule during the conflict
- **perspective:** a viewpoint shaped by a person's position, experience, purpose, and information
- **primary source:** evidence made during the time being studied or by someone directly involved
- **corroborate:** compare sources and check where their evidence agrees, differs, or leaves a gap

**Respectful history rule:** Analyze evidence. Do not act out an identity or invent a person's thoughts. “The source does not show that” is a strong historical answer.

## 3. Watch a historian's evidence move

### Model source

- **Title:** Lee Resolution (1776)
- **Creator/form:** Second Continental Congress record
- **Repository:** U.S. National Archives
- **Treatment:** short verbatim excerpt from the official transcript
- **Canonical source:** [National Archives — Lee Resolution (1776)](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/lee-resolution)
- **Source key:** `nara-lee-resolution`

> “free and independent States”

1. **Notice an exact detail.** The resolution uses political-status words from the source.
2. **Name only what it supports.** The phrase supports a claim that the resolution proposed a change from colonies to independent states.
3. **Explain the link.** The phrase is relevant because it names the political change being proposed.
4. **Check the boundary.** One congressional resolution does not explain every person's reason for supporting or opposing independence.

**Transfer move:** exact detail → supported idea → because → limit.

The model uses a different source and question from the central independent response.

## 4. Source A: Parliament's stated reason

- **Title:** Great Britain : Parliament - The Stamp Act, March 22, 1765
- **Creator:** Parliament of Great Britain
- **Repository:** The Avalon Project, Yale Law School
- **Source form:** law; verified transcript
- **Treatment:** short verbatim excerpt; original spelling retained
- **Canonical source:** [Avalon Project — Stamp Act transcript](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/stamp_act_1765.asp)
- **Source key:** `avalon-stamp_act`

Before interpreting: The law states Parliament's official reason for raising revenue in Britain's American colonies. Cite Avalon as the transcription host, not as the author.

> “defending, protecting, and securing, the British colonies”

**Source limit:** An official law states what Parliament claimed and required. It does not tell us how every colonist understood the law. Parliament's institutional voice is not a Loyalist colonist's personal voice.

### Guided analysis 1 — full support

Copy the short phrase that tells Parliament's stated purpose. Then explain what that phrase supports and one thing it does not prove.

Optional neutral frame: The words “…” support the idea that Parliament said ____. They do not prove ____.

## 5. Source B: a persuasive print

- **Title:** The bloody massacre perpetrated in King Street Boston on March 5th 1770 by a party of the 29th Regt.
- **Creator:** Paul Revere, engraver, printer, and seller
- **Date/place:** 1770; Boston
- **Repository:** Library of Congress
- **Source form:** engraving with watercolor on laid paper; digital file from the original
- **Treatment:** full source image
- **Canonical source:** [Library of Congress item 2008661777](https://www.loc.gov/item/2008661777/)
- **Source key:** `loc-2008661777`

![Paul Revere's 1770 print. At right, a line of uniformed soldiers fires toward civilians at left. Several civilians lie or are carried in the foreground. Buildings line the background. The title calls the event a bloody massacre.](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/pnp/ppmsca/01600/01657v.jpg)

Look in layers: people and positions; actions; words and title; details that create a feeling.

The Library of Congress item record describes the portrayal as sensationalized. Historians therefore inspect both the image's details and its persuasive choices.

**Source limit:** The print is evidence of how Revere chose to portray the event. It is not a complete or neutral record of everything that happened.

### Guided analysis 2 — faded support

Name one detail in the print. What message might that detail send a viewer? Why should a historian check another source?

## 6. Compare without blending viewpoints

| Comparison question | Source A | Source B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who made it? | Parliament of Great Britain | Paul Revere |
| What form is it? | A law | A printed engraving |
| What can it show? | Parliament's stated purpose and requirements | Revere's chosen portrayal of the Boston event |
| What can it not show alone? | Every colonist's response | A complete, neutral event record |

**Missing perspective alert:** Neither source directly records a Loyalist colonist explaining loyalty. Do not relabel Parliament's law as a Loyalist colonist's voice. A claim about Loyalist reasons needs another verified, attributable source.

## 7. Independent source-and-evidence work

Make a careful claim about why viewpoints toward British rule could differ.

Your response must:

1. identify one detail from Source A;
2. identify one detail from Source B;
3. explain how each detail supports or limits the claim; and
4. name the missing Loyalist-colonist evidence.

Use your own words. Optional neutral frame: My claim is ____. Source A shows ____. Source B shows ____. Together, this evidence suggests ____. A limit is ____.

## 8. Fresh mastery evidence

These questions use a fresh source detail and a changed reasoning demand. Complete them independently. A read-aloud, vocabulary reminder, source chunking, or alternate response mode may preserve access; no helper may supply the reasoning.

### Fresh check 1 — cause and effect

A different Stamp Act clause placed duties “upon every pamphlet and paper.” Explain one possible cause-and-effect connection between a tax on printed material and protest. Label any step that is an inference, not a fact stated by the law.

### Fresh check 2 — perspective and corroboration

A learner uses only Revere's print to explain both protest and loyalism. What kind of verified source should the learner add, and what question should it help answer? Explain why.

Do not invent a title, quotation, author, or source.

## 9. Real remediation: evidence boundaries

Use this path only when a response states an idea without an exact source detail or makes a claim larger than the detail can support.

### Different model

- **Source:** [National Archives — Treaty of Paris (1783)](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/treaty-of-paris)
- **Source key:** `nara-treaty-of-paris`
- **Treatment:** short verbatim excerpt from Article 1

> “free sovereign and independent states”

- The phrase supports a claim that the treaty recognizes independent political status.
- The phrase does not prove why every person protested or stayed loyal years earlier.

This is different from the failed comparison: it temporarily removes comparison and sorts one claim inside a single source boundary and one claim outside it.

### Guided boundary sort

Point to the exact treaty words. State one claim they support and one claim they cannot support.

### Fresh retry

- **Source:** [National Archives — Declaration of Independence (1776)](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/declaration-of-independence)
- **Source key:** `nara-declaration-of-independence`
- **Treatment:** short verbatim excerpt from the official transcript

> “deriving their just powers from the consent of the governed”

Write one claim this phrase supports about an argument for changing government. Then state one claim it cannot support about all colonists.

After the fresh retry, return to the two mastery questions. Do not mark mastery from the repeated failed response or from the modeled treaty answer.

## 10. Learner success criteria

- I keep Parliament, Revere, protesting colonists, and Loyalist colonists from becoming one blended voice.
- I point to an exact phrase, clause, image detail, or title feature and identify its source.
- I explain how the evidence supports my claim instead of placing a detail beside a claim.
- I separate source facts from my inferences.
- I name what the evidence does not show and identify a missing perspective instead of inventing it.
- I complete two fresh mastery questions independently.
