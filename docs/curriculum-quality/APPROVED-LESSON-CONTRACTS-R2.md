# Approved Lesson Contracts R2

**Status: FROZEN**

The binding artifact is [`curriculum/approvals/director-samples-r2-approved.json`](../../curriculum/approvals/director-samples-r2-approved.json). These contracts describe the approved learner experience represented by all 36 frozen Director samples. They are controlling requirements for the Math, ELA, Science, and Social Studies rewrites.

## Common Lesson Contract

- The learner experiences the lesson through the real Rich Study Player and its real learner-response runtime.
- TEACH is written to the learner, with age-appropriate language and a clear purpose.
- A WORKED EXAMPLE is clearly labelled and shows both the answer and the reasoning. Looking at an example is not mastery.
- YOUR TURN always has a real supported response control. A question must never lead to nowhere to type, choose, or enter a response.
- FEEDBACK is instructional: it explains the reasoning, names the next move, and supports correction or reteaching.
- PRACTICE collects real learner-response evidence.
- TAUGHT, PRACTICED, and DEMONSTRATED remain distinct. Viewing an example is not mastery, and one response is not by itself sufficient evidence of mastery.
- LESSON REVIEW is meaningful and includes: What you learned; How you did; What you did well; What to practice/review; Review this lesson; Course progress; and Next action. It is never only “Great work” or “Done”.
- AGE PROGRESSION is real: Grade 3 reads like Grade 3, Grade 8 reads like middle school, and Grade 12 reads like mature high-school work. There is no single generic voice.

## Mathematics model

Math lessons use small-step teaching, visual organization where useful, a clearly separated worked example and YOUR TURN, real numeric/choice/text controls, instructional feedback, reteach or scaffold support, and meaningful practice and review.

The controlling Grade 3 example is place value: show that in 3,000 versus 300 the same digit is worth 10 times as much one place to the left, then provide a separate YOUR TURN with a real response control. The example and the learner response must remain distinct.

## ELA model

ELA lessons use age-appropriate passages. Model analysis is separate from the learner response. Learners receive real constructed-response surfaces, opportunities for revision, and useful multiple-choice feedback. The system must not pretend to deterministically score an essay. Where judgment is required, the lesson provides Parent Review. The end review is meaningful and reflects what the learner actually did.

## Science model

The approved science rhythm is:

**NOTICE → LEARN → MODEL → YOUR TURN → FEEDBACK → APPLY → REVIEW**

Science lessons maintain scientific accuracy and use phenomena, tables, data, and models where appropriate. They require evidence and reasoning, provide real response controls, and use subject-specific review rather than generic completion language.

## Social Studies model

The approved Social Studies rhythm is:

**QUESTION/CONTEXT → BACKGROUND → EVIDENCE → MODEL THINKING → YOUR TURN → FEEDBACK → REVIEW**

Social Studies lessons maintain source integrity and support history, geography, civics, and economics reasoning. They distinguish fact from interpretation, use evidence, handle contested perspectives fairly, and never invent quotations. Learner responses are real and supported by actual controls.
