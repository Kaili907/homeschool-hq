# Physical Education Director Sample R1

Status: **READY FOR DIRECTOR REVIEW**

Lesson: `ma-g12-physical-education-u08-l07`

Title: **The Stop Rule: Act Without Waiting for a Coach**

Grade: **12**

Primary type: **`SAFETY_STOP_DECISION`**

Standards:

- Michigan PE Standard 3: knowledge and skills for health-enhancing physical fitness, Grades 9–12 Level 2.
- Michigan PE Standard 4: responsible personal and social behavior that respects self and others, Grades 9–12 Level 2.

## Why this sample

The R1 audit selected this lesson because it concentrates several high-school PE risks in one safety-critical package. The baseline attached generic locomotor cues to a stop-decision outcome, lacked a warning-sign/stop/escalation model, combined many learner actions inside five broad stages, and required an unsupervised full training block with a real stop decision even though its adaptation and completion text promised a fully described route.

That makes the lesson representative of the exact design problem a high-school PE standard must solve: authentic self-management without demanding exercise volume, a real safety event, standard-body performance, adult impersonation, or body/norm scoring.

## R1 repair

The learner now uses three explicit safety lanes—`REST / ADJUST`, `STOP AND TELL`, and `DO NOT RESUME`—through the cue sequence `NOTICE–PAUSE–ACT–HOLD`. Each lane includes a starting condition, action, key cue, common error, and correction. A worked environmental-stop model leads to three coached contrasts, then four fresh fictional cards and a five-line protocol.

The required lesson is entirely non-movement decision work. It never asks the learner to induce a symptom, injury, head impact, collision, equipment failure, or real stop event. The old unsupervised full-block demand is removed. Warm-up and cool-down are explicitly not applicable for that reason.

## Equal-credit access

The primary route is already seated, solo, low-space, no-equipment, and mobility-aid compatible. The package also supplies supported, reduced-range, reduced-pace, spoken, signed, drawn, eye-gaze, sorted-card, object-modeled, and described paths. Every route is scored against the same decision evidence. The learner may choose or change a route without explaining why.

## Authority and evidence

Learner evidence is four fresh scenario decisions, one five-line safety protocol, and an optional process reflection. No physical completion is claimed. A human reviews academic decision quality; a learner, browser, camera, wearable, or future Tutor cannot certify physical performance or safe return.

Guardian permission remains authoritative before the learner later uses a personal activity plan, household contact path, location, equipment, partner, or progression. The guardian confirms only permission and the household escalation path. If guardian review or real activity is unavailable, the supplied fictional decision set remains the complete equal-credit lesson.

## Mastery and retry

Mastery requires accurate independent evidence on at least two occasions separated by time, setting, or meaningful scenario variation. Reflection, attendance, physical occurrence, standard-route use, Tutor confidence, body data, or speed cannot establish mastery.

Retry identifies the exact decision gap, returns to three visible lane labels, introduces a different two-question cue, contrasts a comfortable break with a warning-sign stop, practices one bounded card, and ends with a fresh loose-equipment scenario. A safety stop is never failure.

## Tutor boundary

The advisory manifest is curriculum metadata only. A future Tutor may explain the three lanes, cue the approved model, offer an approved presentation adaptation, and coach how the learner presents evidence. It may not diagnose, prescribe, observe, certify completion, give return clearance, replace guardian authority, or choose an answer for a fresh independent scenario.
