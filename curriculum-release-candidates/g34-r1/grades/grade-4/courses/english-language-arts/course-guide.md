# Grade 4 English Language Arts — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g4-english-language-arts`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 180  
**Typical session:** 45–65 minutes  
**Cadence:** daily

## Course description

A fourth-grade literacy course organized around evidence. Learners summarize, determine theme, analyze how informational texts are structured, weigh the reasons and evidence an author supplies, integrate two sources, and write multi-paragraph explanatory, opinion, and narrative pieces for a stated audience. A leaner but real foundational thread keeps morphology-driven decoding and fluency in daily practice. All practice texts are original Manuel Academy passages or clearly identified public-domain works.

## Independent progression

This course is authored independently. It is not the Grade 5 course with lowered difficulty labels: it uses its own unit sequence, its own 18-day unit arc, its own daily cadence, its own text bank, and standards verified for this grade.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned Grade 4 reading, writing, language, and speaking and listening expectations through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Support statements about a text with evidence the text actually contains.
3. Write for a purpose across narrative, explanatory, and opinion forms, and revise on criteria rather than on correction alone.
4. Use sources honestly: notes in the learner's own words, and a record of where information came from.
5. Transfer reading and writing skills to unfamiliar texts and tasks.
6. Complete the course capstone: **A year-end literacy portfolio containing an original narrative, an explanatory piece, an opinion piece, a research brief with a source list, a two-text comparison, and a growth reflection, presented publicly, privately, in print, or in another accessible form.**

## Instructional model

Each unit runs an 18-day arc:

1. Launch and diagnostic
2. Word study and morphology
3. Concept model A
4. Guided practice A
5. Close reading with evidence
6. Independent application A
7. Concept model B
8. Guided practice B
9. Reteach and varied practice
10. Craft or structure analysis
11. Source or research work
12. Writing plan and draft
13. Writing revision workshop
14. Discussion or presentation practice
15. Transfer challenge
16. Retrieval and assessment preparation
17. Unit assessment
18. Targeted correction, publication, and reflection

Each session runs the same cadence:

| Segment | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Welcome and retrieval | 4–6 |
| Word study and fluency | 5–8 |
| Model or mini-lesson | 8–14 |
| Guided practice | 10–15 |
| Independent application | 12–20 |
| Exit ticket and next step | 3–6 |

Lessons are resumable by segment. Media is optional everywhere and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Guided evidence and independent evidence are recorded separately. Only independent evidence supports a mastery decision.
- Mastery requires accurate independent evidence on at least two occasions separated by time, text, or representation.
- Suggested reporting is **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, private presentation choices, and alternate response modes are never failures.

## Academic integrity

The learner writes every assessed response. The tutor may question, model on a separate example, name a criterion, restate directions, or point to where an idea is unclear. The tutor must not draft, dictate, outline, complete, reword, or supply sentences for the learner's assessed essay or extended response.

Fixed-answer items and their keys are scorer-visible only. The tutor surface, the learner surface, and any hint path must not expose, restate, or narrow toward a fixed answer before the learner responds. Feedback after a response addresses reasoning, not the key.

## Text and copyright

Every passage supplied by this course is an original Manuel Academy text. Public-domain works are listed as references with creator, first-publication year, and public-domain rationale, and are not reproduced here. Facilitators may substitute library, licensed, or family-approved texts at the same target; do not paste copyrighted text into this package. See `policies/source-integrity.md`.

## Accessibility

- Every lesson has a complete text-only path; no image, audio, video, or chart is required to reach the target, and every visual carries a written description that conveys the same information.
- All reading content is read-aloud capable by an adult, a screen reader, or a text-to-speech tool, and the learner is never required to read aloud.
- A no-audio path is provided: any listening or paraphrasing-from-media task may be completed from a printed transcript instead.
- Where media is used it must carry captions and a transcript, or it must not be used.
- Directions are chunked, and a model or exemplar is available for every extended task.
- Typed, handwritten, spoken, dictated-to-scribe, or structured-organizer responses are accepted whenever the standard does not name the mode.
- Reduced copying, extended time, hidden timers, movement breaks, high-contrast print, larger type, keyboard access, and low-distraction settings are available on request and are not recorded as failures.
- A private presentation alternative exists for every speaking or presenting task; no learner voice or video recording is ever required.
- The learning target is preserved while quantity, pacing, representation, or response mode is adjusted.

## Adaptive English boundary

The frozen Adaptive English package covers approximately Grades 4-6. Grade 4 lessons may expose compatible intervention capability through a future adapter. No adapter is implemented, mounted, or required by this package.

## Capstone

A year-end literacy portfolio containing an original narrative, an explanatory piece, an opinion piece, a research brief with a source list, a two-text comparison, and a growth reflection, presented publicly, privately, in print, or in another accessible form.
