# Grade 4 English Language Arts — Year Overview

**Course ID:** `ma-g4-english-language-arts`  
**Year model:** 36 weeks / 180 instructional days  
**Session length:** 45–65 minutes  
**Cadence:** daily

A fourth-grade literacy course organized around evidence. Learners summarize, determine theme, analyze how informational texts are structured, weigh the reasons and evidence an author supplies, integrate two sources, and write multi-paragraph explanatory, opinion, and narrative pieces for a stated audience. A leaner but real foundational thread keeps morphology-driven decoding and fluency in daily practice. All practice texts are original Manuel Academy passages or clearly identified public-domain works.

## Weekly map

| Weeks | Unit | Title | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | 1 | An Evidence Habit: Details, Examples, and Inferences | Publish an evidence-habits page holding two text-based responses, one literary and one informational, each with quoted or paraphrased support and a self-check. |
| 4–8 | 2 | Summary and Theme: Saying What a Story Means | Write an objective summary and a theme statement for one story, each supported by two details drawn directly from the text. |
| 8–11 | 3 | In-Depth Study: Character, Setting, Event, and Point of View | Produce an in-depth character, setting, or event study drawing on at least four specific details, one point-of-view observation, and one cross-text pattern. |
| 11–15 | 4 | Informational Main Idea, Summary, and Explanation | Write a main-idea and summary brief for an original informational passage, including an explanation of one process or event the text describes and why it occurs. |
| 15–18 | 5 | How Informational Texts Are Structured and Shown | Build a structure-and-features analysis showing how one passage is organized and how a chart, timeline, or diagram adds to it, including a written description that carries the same information for text-only access. |
| 19–22 | 6 | Explanatory Writing: Grouping, Precision, and Development | Publish a three-to-five paragraph explanatory piece with grouped sections, precise domain vocabulary, linking words, a formatting choice, and a conclusion related to the information presented. |
| 22–26 | 7 | Opinion and Argument: Reasons, Evidence, and Fair Reading | Write and deliver an opinion piece with a clear position, grouped reasons supported by facts and details from at least two sources; delivery may be spoken, read by another person, printed, or private. |
| 26–29 | 8 | Poetry, Drama, and Prose: Structure, Craft, and Figurative Language | Create a craft study containing one original poem or dramatic scene and an author's note analyzing its structure, one figurative device, and one allusion, idiom, adage, or proverb; presentation may be silent, printed, recorded, or private. |
| 29–33 | 9 | Research, Sources, and Honest Note-Taking | Complete a research brief with the question, at least three categorized sources and a source list, paraphrased notes, an integrated answer drawing on two texts, and one question the sources raised that you did not answer. |
| 33–36 | 10 | Narrative Craft, Comparison, and the Year-End Portfolio | Publish a year-end portfolio containing an original narrative, an explanatory piece, an opinion piece, a research brief, a comparison of two texts, and a written growth reflection. |

## Capstone

A year-end literacy portfolio containing an original narrative, an explanatory piece, an opinion piece, a research brief with a source list, a two-text comparison, and a growth reflection, presented publicly, privately, in print, or in another accessible form.

## Family use
