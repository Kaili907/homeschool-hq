# Grade 3 Financial Literacy — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g3-financial-literacy`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 36  
**Typical session:** 30–50 minutes  
**Cadence:** weekly across the 36-week Manuel Academy year

## Course description

An introductory Grade 3 money course covering needs and wants, earning, spending choices, saving, money tools, advertising awareness, and generosity. Every amount, price, and account in the course is fictional and simulated, and no real financial information is ever requested.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, show a process, or justify a choice rather than supply an unsupported answer.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, setting, or situation.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A pretend classroom or paper market with a simple make-believe money-in and money-out record and a reflection. No real money, account, or purchase is involved.**

## Instructional model

Each unit runs a six-session arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application or project; mastery check; and targeted correction with reflection. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation, application, error or safety analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Simulated finance and privacy commitments

- Every amount, price, wage, budget, account, and business in this course is **fictional and simulated**. No real money moves at any point.
- The course never requests, collects, displays, or records real bank data, account numbers, balances, card numbers, PINs, passwords, or tax identifiers.
- The course gives general concepts only. It never provides individualized financial, tax, credit, insurance, or investment advice to a learner or a family.
- No purchase, real account, subscription, or app download is required for any lesson, project, or assessment.
- Family income and spending are never compared, ranked, scored, or requested, and reflection may be kept private.

## Practice and mastery authority

Simulated practice, budget games, and marketplace play record **completion only** and do not by themselves advance official mastery. Only the unit mastery check and the unit assessment carry tutor-core mastery authority, and mastery still requires independent evidence on at least two occasions. Lesson-level authority is recorded in `study-integration.json`.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Essential question | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Needs, Wants, and Choosing | 6 | Why can't we buy everything we want, and how do we choose? | Build a pretend choice board that compares two make-believe options and names the tradeoff for each one. |
| 2 | Where Money Comes From | 6 | Where does money come from, and what work does it stand for? | Create a pretend job card for an age-appropriate helper job, listing the make-believe pay and the skills the job needs. |
| 3 | Spending and Comparing | 6 | How do I compare two pretend prices and decide which is the better choice? | Complete a make-believe ten-dollar shopping trip, compare two pretend options, and explain the choice in writing, speech, or drawing. |
| 4 | Saving for a Goal | 6 | How does saving a little at a time help me reach a goal? | Make a pretend savings plan for one make-believe goal and track four weeks of imaginary deposits on a chart. |
| 5 | Money Tools and Ads | 6 | What do ads want me to do, and what should I keep private? | Describe or collect two pretend advertisements and write one question to ask about each claim before believing it. |
| 6 | Our Pretend Market Capstone | 6 | What does it take to run a pretend market and keep an honest record? | Run a paper or classroom pretend market and produce a simple make-believe money-in and money-out record with a short reflection. |

## Documented curriculum anchors

- Michigan Personal Finance foundations — introductory
- Grade 3 economics and mathematics connections
- Manuel Academy Financial Literacy elementary sequence — Grade 3

Each unit adds one unit-level anchor; see the `standards` field in `units.json` and `lessons.jsonl`. These are locally documented anchors, not a claim of state approval, accreditation, licensure, or automatic credit.

## Schedule

The course runs one session per week for 36 weeks. `course-schedule.csv` maps each week to exactly one lesson; every lesson appears exactly once.

## Accessibility

- Provide readable text plus optional audio or read-aloud support; no voice feature is required.
- Chunk directions into one action at a time and show a worked example or model.
- Allow typed, handwritten, spoken, drawn, manipulatives-based, or demonstrated responses when the standard permits.
- Offer reduced-copying, extended-time, hidden-timer, movement-break, and low-distraction options.
- Use captions, alt text, high-contrast print, keyboard access, and text-only fallbacks for media.
- Preserve the learning target while adjusting quantity, pacing, representation, or response mode.
