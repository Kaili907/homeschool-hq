# Grade 3 Technology and Computer Science — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g3-tech-cs`
**Version:** 1.0.0
**Instructional sessions:** 36
**Typical session:** 40–60
**Cadence:** weekly

## Course description

An introductory digital-literacy and computer-science course building safe online habits, computer basics, typing, step-by-step thinking, simple data, and human-centered design.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A simple, accessible prototype with step-by-step directions, a test note, and a short presentation or demonstration.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling; guided practice; independent application; investigation or close reading; performance-task planning and creation; synthesis; unit assessment; targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation/source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Digital Citizenship and Online Safety Basics | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing, Michigan Computer Science: Networks and the Internet | Create a digital-safety poster using fictional examples and no real personal information. |
| 2 | Computers Around Us | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Computing Systems | Label a diagram of computer parts and demonstrate three careful-use habits. |
| 3 | Typing and Getting Organized | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Computing Systems, Michigan Computer Science: Data and Analysis | Create and save one clearly named, organized digital or paper folder of practice work. |
| 4 | Patterns, Sequences, and Introductory Algorithms | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Algorithms and Programming | Write and test a step-by-step set of directions for a partner or character to follow. |
| 5 | Pictures, Sounds, and Simple Data | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Data and Analysis, Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing | Collect a small set of data and create a simple picture graph with a short conclusion. |
| 6 | Designing for Someone Else | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Algorithms and Programming, Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing | Design and share a simple prototype that solves a problem for a chosen user. |
