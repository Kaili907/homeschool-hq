# Grade 3 English Language Arts — Year Overview

**Course ID:** `ma-g3-english-language-arts`  
**Year model:** 36 weeks / 180 instructional days  
**Session length:** 40–55 minutes  
**Cadence:** daily

A third-grade literacy course built for the year learners move from learning to read toward reading to learn. Foundational decoding and fluency are taught every day beside literary and informational reading, sentence and paragraph development, grammar, vocabulary, small-scale research, and speaking and listening with private and no-audio alternatives. All practice texts are original Manuel Academy passages or clearly identified public-domain works.

## Weekly map

| Weeks | Unit | Title | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | 1 | Readers and Writers Begin: Habits, Print, and Sentences | Publish a Reader-and-Writer Plan card with three habits, one text-based answer written in complete sentences, and one goal, using school-safe examples only. |
| 4–8 | 2 | Word Builders: Syllables, Prefixes, Suffixes, and Roots | Build a Word-Parts toolkit that decodes and explains ten longer words taken from this year's reading, with a sentence showing each word used correctly. |
| 8–11 | 3 | Story Elements: Characters, Problems, and Lessons | Write a retelling and a lesson statement for one story, each supported by two details taken directly from the text. |
| 11–15 | 4 | Reading to Learn: Main Idea and Key Details | Produce a one-page main-idea card for an original informational passage with three supporting details and either a labeled diagram or a written description that carries the same information. |
| 15–18 | 5 | How Information Texts Are Built: Sequence, Cause, and Comparison | Make a text-structure map for two passages on one topic showing sequence, cause and effect, and what each text adds that the other does not. |
| 19–22 | 6 | Paragraph Power: Informative and Explanatory Writing | Publish a two-paragraph explainer with a topic sentence, facts and definitions, linking words, and a closing sentence. |
| 22–26 | 7 | Opinion Writing: Reasons That Convince | Write and present an opinion piece with a clear opinion, three ordered reasons, linking words, and a conclusion; presentation may be spoken, read aloud by another person, printed, or private. |
| 26–29 | 8 | Poems, Plays, and Words That Mean More | Create a small poem-and-scene set with an author's note explaining two craft choices; the presentation may be printed, silently displayed, recorded, or shared privately with one adult. |
| 29–33 | 9 | Small Research: Questions, Sources, and Notes | Complete a short research card: the question, two named sources, notes written in your own words, the answer, and one thing you still wonder. |
| 33–36 | 10 | Narrative Craft and the Year-End Portfolio | Publish a year-end portfolio with an original narrative, one explainer, one opinion piece, one research card, and a written reflection on growth. |

## Capstone

A year-end literacy portfolio containing an original narrative, an explanatory piece, an opinion piece, a research card, and a growth reflection, presented publicly, privately, in print, or in another accessible form.

## Family use

- `schedule.csv` gives a ready 180-day calendar with one ELA session per day.
- `course-guide.md` gives the scope and sequence and the mastery policy.
- `lesson-sequence.md` is the readable day-by-day plan.
- `lessons.jsonl` and `assessments.json` are the machine-readable records.
- `original-text-bank.md` holds every passage the course supplies.
- No learner photograph, voice recording, precise location, or account credential is required anywhere in this course.
