# Grade 9 Algebra and Functions I — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g9-mathematics`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 180  
**Typical session:** 45–60  
**Cadence:** daily  
**Credit:** Michigan Merit Curriculum mathematics credit 1 of 4

## Prerequisite

Grade 8 Mathematics (`ma-g8-mathematics`). Assumes 8.NS.1-2, 8.EE.1,3-8, 8.F.1-5, 8.G.1-9, and 8.SP.1-4. Unit 2 explicitly bridges 8.EE.2 (square and cube root symbols), which the Grade 8 course does not cover.

## Course description

A first high-school mathematics course that begins exactly where Grade 8 ends. Grade 8 closes with linear equations, linear functions, systems of linear equations, integer exponents, and an introduction to irrational numbers. This course formalizes that work into quantitative reasoning, rational exponents, expression structure, equation creation, formal reasoning about linear equations and systems, the function concept, and linear/quadratic/exponential model comparison, closing with a full data investigation.

## Standards coverage

This course carries **41 Michigan high school mathematics standards** across 9 domains: A-CED, A-REI, A-SSE, F-BF, F-IF, F-LE, N-Q, N-RN, S-ID.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A complete data investigation that poses a question, models it with an appropriate function family, defends every unit and accuracy choice, and states honestly what the data cannot establish.**

## Instructional model

Each unit follows a recurring 18-day arc: low-stakes launch and diagnostic; explicit modeling;
guided practice; independent application; investigation; reteaching from error evidence;
problem seminar; performance-task planning and creation; consolidation; transfer; assessment;
targeted correction; and reflection or publication. Lessons are resumable by segment, media is
optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Every lesson carries the Academy mastery rule: independent evidence plus explanation on at least two occasions.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment is specified per unit in `assessments.json`: targeted reteaching of the smallest gap, then fresh items in different contexts. A reassessment is never averaged against the original attempt.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Accessibility and no-media support

No lesson in this course requires media, audio, or a voice feature. Every lesson specifies a
readable fallback that carries the same information as readable steps, alt text, transcript,
tactile or concrete model, or facilitator demonstration. Response mode may be typed,
handwritten, spoken, drawn, manipulatives-based, or demonstrated wherever the standard permits.

## Capstone

A complete data investigation that poses a question, models it with an appropriate function family, defends every unit and accuracy choice, and states honestly what the data cannot establish.

The capstone may be presented publicly, privately, in writing, orally, through a demonstration,
or in another accessible format that preserves the learning targets.
