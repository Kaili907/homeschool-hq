# Grade 11 Arts and Music — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g11-arts-and-music`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 72  
**Typical session:** 45–75 minutes (60–90 in capstone units)  
**Cadence:** twice weekly

## Course description

A Grade 11 arts and music course organized around a sustained investigation, extended composition and production, interdisciplinary applied practice, advanced performance and direction, and curation.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A Grade 11 concentration portfolio with a sustained investigation, an extended composition or production, an interdisciplinary applied project, curatorial work, a full rights review, and a private-capable presentation.**

## Instructional model

Six-session units run: Launch and diagnostic; Explicit model; Guided practice; Application or project; Mastery check; Correction and reflection. Twelve-session units run: Launch and diagnostic; Concept model A; Guided practice A; Independent application A; Concept model B; Guided practice B; Investigation or close reading; Reteach and varied practice; Performance task build; Synthesis and review; Unit assessment; Correction and reflection.

Every topic in a unit receives at least one instructional lesson before any
assessment or correction lesson. Mastery-check, assessment, synthesis, and
correction lessons are scoped to the whole unit rather than to a single topic.
Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is
always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation or source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection. Capstone units add a defense-and-response-to-critique prompt.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Concentration: Sustained Investigation | 12 | Michigan Arts Education Standards: Visual Arts — Creating | Launch a sustained investigation with a driving question, a research base, and documented iteration cycles. |
| 2 | Advanced Composition, Production, and Literacy | 12 | Michigan Arts Education Standards: Music — Creating; Music — Responding | Compose or produce an extended original work with score or session documentation and analytical commentary. |
| 3 | Interdisciplinary and Applied Design Practice | 12 | Michigan Arts Education Standards: Media Arts — Connecting; Visual Arts — Connecting | Deliver an applied interdisciplinary project meeting a stated brief with accessibility and stakeholder evidence. |
| 4 | Advanced Performance, Direction, and Production | 12 | Michigan Arts Education Standards: Theatre — Performing; Music — Performing | Lead or execute an advanced performance or production element in a chosen private or shared format, with a rehearsal, direction, and feedback record. |
| 5 | Criticism, Aesthetics, and Curation | 12 | Michigan Arts Education Standards: Visual Arts — Responding; Visual Arts — Presenting | Curate a small exhibition or programme — physical, digital, or as a paper-only curatorial dossier — with a curatorial statement, a sequencing rationale, and access supports. |
| 6 | Junior Portfolio, Documentation, and Rights Review | 12 | Michigan Arts Education Standards: Visual Arts — Presenting; Media Arts — Producing | Complete a junior portfolio with documentation standards, a rights review, and a capstone-year proposal. |

## Capstone

A Grade 11 concentration portfolio with a sustained investigation, an extended composition or production, an interdisciplinary applied project, curatorial work, a full rights review, and a private-capable presentation.

The capstone may be presented publicly, privately, in writing, orally, through a demonstration, or in another accessible format that preserves the learning targets.
