# Credit and Coverage Map — Michigan Merit Curriculum Audit

**Contract:** `manuel-academy-high-school-9-12-release-contract`
**Audit date:** 2026-08-12
**Scope:** Curriculum and coverage design audit. **Not** a legal opinion.

## Scope and standing of this audit

This document asks one question: *if the Michigan Merit Curriculum (MMC) is used as
the coverage target, does the ten-family Manuel Academy programme have a home for
every requirement?* It answers with evidence and names what is missing.

It does **not** decide whether the MMC binds this family. MDE's own documents state
the MMC credit requirements are not required of home schools, with an inconsistency
around Civics/Government that `standards-reference.md` records verbatim. Whether to
treat the MMC as binding, as a voluntary quality target, or as transfer and
college-readiness insurance is a Director decision. No legal conclusion is drawn
here.

## Headline ruling

**This programme is NOT graduation-complete against the Michigan Merit Curriculum.**
Nine of the ten families cover their requirements and several exceed them. One
requirement — **World Language, 2 credits** — has no home in the ten-family model,
and at least **½ credit of it cannot be substituted away**. A second requirement,
the **online learning experience**, has been assigned a home in this contract but
nothing has yet been built to evidence it.

## The 18-credit frame

MCL 380.1278a and 380.1278b set the requirements; MDE's *Michigan Merit Curriculum
Overview* supplies the total of 18. The named requirements sum to exactly 18:

```
ELA 4 + Math 4 + Science 3 + Social Studies 3 + Health/PE 1 + VPAA 1 + World Language 2 = 18
```

Personal Finance (½ credit) is **not additive**. The statute directs that it "must
fulfill" ½ credit of mathematics, of visual/performing/applied arts, **or** of a
language other than English, "as determined by the board". The total stays 18. An
audit that adds Personal Finance on top overstates the requirement by ½ credit and
misses that whichever family absorbs it now needs ½ credit less.

## §A — Final-year mathematics is a scheduling constraint, not a content one

Statute: each pupil "must successfully complete at least 1 mathematics course
during his or her final year of high school enrollment", and the subparagraph
"does not require completion of mathematics courses in any particular sequence".

Three precision points the Mathematics session must carry:

1. It says **final year of enrolment**, not "senior year" and not "grade 12". For a
   student who finishes early or late, the requirement follows enrolment.
2. It requires a **course**, not a credit.
3. A student who banks all four mathematics credits by Grade 11 and takes no
   mathematics in the final year **fails this requirement with four credits in
   hand.** This is the failure mode that a content-only map cannot see.

The matrix places a mathematics course in Grade 12 for exactly this reason.
`ma-g12-mathematics` must not be treated as an optional capstone.

## §B — Civics credit value

The statute names economics at ½ credit and then cross-references the civics course
in §1166(2) **without stating a credit value**. The ½ figure is MDE guidance, not
statutory text; MCL 380.1166 was not read. The *coverage* is not in doubt — the
Grade 11 course carries civics as a distinct half-year component with its own
credit line. Only the authority for the number is partially verified.

This matters more than it looks: MDE's MMC FAQ carve-out for home schools is
specifically about "the one-semester credit requirement in Civics/Government". It
is the one MMC element MDE's own guidance declines to waive. Civics is therefore
the requirement to be least casual about.

## §C — Online learning experience

Not credit-bearing, which is precisely why it is easy to lose: it will never appear
in a credit tally. Statute requires at least one course or learning experience
presented online; the alternative satisfaction path is online experience integrated
into every MMC course.

This contract assigns it to `ma-g9-technology` (Computer Science Principles and
Digital Citizenship). For a home programme the discrete-course path is far cleaner
to evidence than the integrated path.

Verdict is `PARTIALLY_COVERED` rather than `COVERED` because assignment is not
delivery. It becomes `COVERED` when the Technology session builds an identifiable
online learning experience and says where the evidence lives. Until then this row
stays open.

## §D — Personal finance displaces; it does not add

Verified facts:

- Required at ½ credit, MCL 380.1278a(3), added by 2022 PA 105 (effective
  2023-03-29).
- Applies "[b]eginning with pupils entering grade 8 in 2023" — for a pupil entering
  Grade 8 in autumn 2023, the graduating class of 2028.
- Total credits stay at 18.
- It **must** fulfil ½ credit of mathematics, **or** ½ credit of visual/performing/
  applied arts, **or** ½ credit of a language other than English — "as determined by
  the board". Under the statute this is the board's election, not the family's.
- It **cannot** fulfil the ½ economics credit. Economics and personal finance are
  distinct requirements.
- Older doors are closed for current cohorts: financial literacy as the fourth
  mathematics credit, and a personal-economics course satisfying the ½ economics
  credit, applied only to pupils entering Grade 8 **before** 2023.

**REQUIRES_DIRECTOR_DECISION.** Which credit the ½ is applied against is an open
choice with a real consequence, because it interacts with §E:

| If applied against | Effect | Interaction with World Language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mathematics | Mathematics requirement drops to 3.5; the programme's 4.0 still exceeds it | No help with World Language |
| Visual/performing/applied arts | VPAA drops to 0.5; the programme's 1.0 still exceeds it | No help with World Language |
| **A language other than English** | World Language drops from 2.0 to 1.5 | **Reduces the World Language gap by ½ credit** |

Applying it against world language is the only option that shrinks the programme's
one genuine gap. The other two apply a discount to requirements the programme
already exceeds — which is to say, they buy nothing.

**A note on the published Grade 8 course.** `ma-g8-financial-literacy` already
covers PF1–PF7 across 72 sessions, and MCL 380.1278b(2) provides that a
high-school credit completed before entering high school "must be given". If the
Director holds evidence that the Grade 8 course met the high-school expectations
with demonstrated proficiency — assessed, per MDE, at least in part on assessments
measuring those expectations — then `ma-g9-financial-literacy` may be re-scoped
from a repeat to an extension. That is a Director determination about a specific
learner's evidence, not something a curriculum package can establish. This contract
does not assume it, and places a full high-school Personal Finance course in
Grade 9 by default.

## §E — World Language: the ruling

**NOT_COVERED. REQUIRES_DIRECTOR_DECISION.**

This is the programme's largest structural gap and it cannot be closed by renaming
or re-tagging existing courses.

### What the statute requires

MCL 380.1278a(2) sits **outside** the §1278a(1) credit block — it is a separate,
standalone diploma condition. It requires 2 credits "grade-appropriate in a language
other than English", or coursework substantially equivalent to 2 credits under MDE
guidelines.

### What helps

- **Credits may be earned in grades K through 12.** This is the single most useful
  fact in this document. The requirement is *not* confined to Grades 9–12. Language
  study begun now counts. Districts are "strongly encouraged" to ensure a credit in
  grades K-6.
- **American Sign Language qualifies** as a language other than English.
- **Online delivery qualifies**, wholly or in part.
- Languages with no written form require listening and speaking only; languages no
  longer spoken (Latin, Ancient Greek) require reading and writing only.

### What can be substituted — and the limit

> "A pupil may partially or fully fulfill **1 credit** of this requirement by
> completing a department-approved formal career and technical education program or
> curriculum or by completing visual or performing arts instruction that is **in
> addition to** the requirements under subsection (1)(a)(iv)."

Two constraints follow, and both are easy to get wrong:

1. **Only one of the two credits is substitutable.** The other must be actual
   language study.
2. The arts substitution must be **additional** arts instruction. The same arts
   credit cannot count twice — once for VPAA and once for world language.

### The arithmetic

| Step | Credits remaining |
| --- | --- |
| Requirement | 2.0 |
| Less 1 credit via additional VPAA or a department-approved formal CTE programme | 1.0 |
| Less ½ credit **if and only if** the Director applies the personal finance half-credit here | 0.5 |
| **Irreducible remainder** | **0.5** |

**At least ½ credit of genuine language study remains, and it must reach Novice
High proficiency** in the required skills — the MMC World Languages standards
explicitly mark Novice High as the point at which "[s]tudents have met the minimum
two-credit graduation requirement". Outside a Personal Curriculum this cannot be
reduced to zero.

**The two substitutions compete.** The personal finance half-credit can be applied
in exactly one place. Model this as a single budget, not three independent
discounts.

### What the programme can already offer

The matrix allocates 2.0 arts credits across Grades 9–12 (0.5 each year). The first
1.0 carries the VPAA requirement; the **second 1.0 is additional arts instruction**
and is therefore available as the substitutable world-language credit. That
allocation was made deliberately. It is the reason the arts sequence runs all four
years rather than compressing into one.

### Internal evidence of prior awareness

`Japanese-Year-1-Curriculum.md` at the repository root already records the family's
own view that a high-school learner "needs 2 world-language credits of the same
language for a college-ready transcript", and that sitting in on a younger
sibling's short daily block "won't earn that" — pointing instead to an online
provider or community-college dual enrolment. That document is family planning
context, not an official source, and it frames the need around college admission
rather than the MMC. It is cited only to show the gap is already known, not as
authority for any requirement.

### The decision required

The Director must choose one, and record it:

1. **Create an eleventh subject family, World Language.** This is a runtime contract
   change — `ACADEMY_SUBJECTS` in `src/types.ts` is a closed ten-element tuple — and
   is out of scope for every session in this wave. See `authoring-boundaries.md` §5.
2. **Source the remaining language study externally** (online provider, dual
   enrolment, community college) and carry it on the transcript without authoring it
   here. This is what the family's own earlier note anticipated.
3. **Accept the gap explicitly**, on the basis that MDE's documents state the MMC
   credit requirements are not required of home schools. This is a legitimate
   choice; it is not one this contract may make silently, and it has transfer and
   college-admission consequences that are outside this audit.

Whichever is chosen, this contract will not report the programme as
graduation-complete until it is.

## §F — Ready for Life has no standards anchor

**REQUIRES_DIRECTOR_DECISION.** Alone among the ten families, Ready for Life has no
coded MDE standards document to map against — see the caveat in
`standards-reference.md`. The published Grade 8 course tags its units against
"Manuel Academy RFL Grade 8 progression", a local progression.

Two acceptable resolutions, and one unacceptable one:

- Bind the family to an official Michigan framework, accepting that the Career
  Development Model and CTE cluster documents are frameworks rather than coded
  standards; or
- State plainly that the family is locally defined and makes no state standards
  claim.
- **Not acceptable:** tagging lessons in a way that implies state alignment that
  cannot be traced to a document.

The family still earns its place: CTE-shaped work is the mechanism behind several
MMC substitutions — Algebra II, the third science credit, world language, VPAA and
personal finance — so Ready for Life is where a formal CTE pathway would be
documented if one is ever pursued.

## Levers not currently used

Recorded so the Director knows they exist.

- **Computer science may satisfy the third science credit.** MCL 380.1278b(1)(b)
  permits the third science credit to be fulfilled by a department-approved computer
  science program. The programme carries 4.0 science credits against a requirement
  of 3, so this lever is not needed — but it is available if science load becomes a
  problem.
- **Credit is proficiency-based, not seat-time**, and per MDE a single course may
  count toward more than one credit area where proficiency is demonstrated in both,
  with an evidence trail for each. This is the main mechanism for fitting MMC
  coverage into ten families without inflating the schedule.
- **Personal Curriculum.** MCL 380.1278b(5) permits a modification after Grade 9,
  bounded: ELA and science credits are not modifiable at all; mathematics only down
  to 3½ credits including Algebra I and Geometry plus one mathematics credit in the
  final two years; social science, health/PE and VPAA only by substituting an
  additional ELA, mathematics, science or world-language credit, or a formal CTE
  programme. It is a district instrument and is noted here for completeness.

## Programme load — a concern worth raising

The matrix recommends **26.25 credits across Grades 9–12** against an MMC frame of
18. Ten families running every year in a home programme is a heavy schedule: 1,188
sessions in Grade 9 and 1,134 in each of Grades 10–12.

The continuity rule — no skipped grade in any family — is what produces this, and
it was a stated requirement of the contract. It is satisfiable, but the Director
should review annual load deliberately rather than discover it during Grade 10. The
lightest families (Ready for Life at 36 sessions, Health at 36 in Grades 10–12) are
already sized as weekly seminars for this reason.
