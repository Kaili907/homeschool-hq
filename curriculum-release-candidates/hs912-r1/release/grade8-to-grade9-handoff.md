# Grade 8 → Grade 9 Handoff

**Contract:** `manuel-academy-high-school-9-12-release-contract`
**Authored:** 2026-08-12

The Grade 8 side of every row below is **observed**, read directly from the
published release at `curriculum-content/manuel-academy/1.0.0` (`unit-index.json`,
`course-index.json`, `grades/grade-8/grade-overview.md`). It is not a summary from
memory and not an aspiration. The Grade 9 side is the **entry expectation** each
subject session must satisfy, and is a
`MANUEL_ACADEMY_COURSE_DESIGN_DECISION` unless a state requirement is named.

The rule this document exists to enforce: **no unexplained gap.** Where Grade 9
does not begin exactly where Grade 8 stopped, the discontinuity is named, and the
reason is stated.

---

## Mathematics — `ma-g8-mathematics` → `ma-g9-mathematics` (Algebra I)

**Grade 8 exit (observed, 10 units / 180 days).** Real and irrational numbers
(8.NS.1–2); integer exponents and scientific notation (8.EE.1, 8.EE.3–4); linear
equations (8.EE.7); slope and proportional reasoning (8.EE.5–6); functions
(8.F.1–5); systems of linear equations (8.EE.8); transformations, congruence and
similarity (8.G.1–5); Pythagorean theorem and coordinate geometry (8.G.6–8);
volume and bivariate data (8.G.9, 8.SP.1–4); an integrated algebraic modelling
capstone.

**Grade 9 entry expectation.** Algebra I opens assuming fluency with linear
equations in one variable, slope as a rate of change, the function concept and
function notation readiness, and solving a two-variable linear system. It must
**not** re-teach these as new content; it may retrieve and extend them.

**Continuity ruling: CONTINUOUS.** Grade 8 completes the Grade 8 mathematics
standards and stops immediately below Algebra I. Algebra I is the conventional
next course.

**Named discontinuity: none.** One note for the Director rather than a gap:
Michigan permits high-school credit to be earned before Grade 9 under conditions
recorded in `standards-reference.md`. The published Grade 8 course is Grade 8
mathematics, **not** Algebra I, so this sequence does not assume any high-school
mathematics credit was earned in Grade 8, and the Grade 12 course still carries the
final-year mathematics expectation.

## English Language Arts — `ma-g8-english-language-arts` → `ma-g9-english-language-arts`

**Grade 8 exit (observed, 10 units / 180 days).** Evidence and academic
conversation; theme and narrative craft; central ideas and explanatory synthesis;
poetry and rhetorical language; argument and counterclaim; research and citation;
rhetoric, media and information ecosystems; grammar and editing; comparative
literature in historical context; a publication and transition portfolio capstone.
Coverage spans 8.RL, 8.RI, 8.W, 8.SL and 8.L.

**Grade 9 entry expectation.** English 9 assumes a student who can locate and cite
textual evidence, sustain a claim with counterclaim, run a short research cycle
with citation, and revise from criteria. It raises text complexity to the 9–10
band and formalises rhetorical analysis.

**Continuity ruling: CONTINUOUS.** The Grade 8 capstone is explicitly a transition
portfolio; English 9 begins from that portfolio.

**Named discontinuity: none.**

## Science — `ma-g8-science` → `ma-g9-science` (Biology)

**Grade 8 exit (observed, 9 units / 108 days).** Forces and motion (MS-PS2);
waves and information transfer (MS-PS4); growth, reproduction and heredity
(MS-LS1-4, MS-LS3); natural selection and adaptation (MS-LS4); Earth history and
geologic processes (MS-ESS1-4, MS-ESS2); Earth, Moon, Sun and solar system
(MS-ESS1); resources, climate and human systems (MS-ESS3); applied biology and
ecosystem design (MS-LS2, MS-ETS1); an engineering and scientific argument
capstone.

**Grade 9 entry expectation.** Biology assumes the middle-school life-science band
is complete — heredity, natural selection, ecosystems and matter/energy flow — and
that the student can build a model, analyse data and construct an evidence-based
explanation. Biology deepens these to the high-school performance expectations
rather than introducing them.

**Continuity ruling: CONTINUOUS, WITH A NAMED DESIGN DECISION.**

**Named discontinuity — cadence.** Grade 8 science runs 108 sessions (three times
weekly). Grade 9 Biology is specified at 180 sessions (daily) with laboratory work,
because a full high-school science credit and its laboratory expectation do not fit
a three-times-weekly cadence. This is a deliberate step up in intensity at the
Grade 8 → 9 boundary, not an accidental gap. Subject sessions must plan for it and
should open Biology with an explicit laboratory-practice and safety onboarding
sequence, since Grade 8 did not require one at this intensity.

**Named discontinuity — band split.** The published
`standards/standards-reference.md` states that Grades 7 and 8 distribute
Michigan's Grades 6–8 performance-expectation band into a *local* sequence that the
state does not itself prescribe. Any Grade 6–8 expectation that the local split left
to Grade 7 is therefore assumed complete before Grade 9. The science session must
verify against the published Grade 7 course that no middle-school expectation is
orphaned before assuming it as Biology prior knowledge.

## Social Studies — `ma-g8-social-studies` → `ma-g9-social-studies` (U.S. History and Geography, 1870–Present)

**Grade 8 exit (observed, 9 units / 108 days).** Constitutional foundations and
historical inquiry; the early republic; expansion, geography and Indigenous
sovereignty; economic and social transformation; reform movements; slavery,
resistance and sectional conflict; the Civil War; Reconstruction and its contested
legacy; and a final unit titled **"Civic Inquiry and Bridge to 1870–1898"**, tagged
against a Michigan Grade 8 U6.1 *introduction*.

**Grade 9 entry expectation.** United States History and Geography begins at
roughly 1870 — industrialisation and urbanisation — picking up precisely where the
Grade 8 bridge unit stops.

**Continuity ruling: CONTINUOUS. This is the chronological seam of the whole
programme and it drove the sequence choice.**

**Named design decision.** Grade 9 is U.S. History and Grade 10 is World History,
which is the reverse of the more common Michigan pattern. The reason is this seam:
Grade 8 ends mid-narrative at 1870 with an explicit bridge unit, and placing World
History in Grade 9 would leave that bridge dangling for a full year before Grade 10
resumed it. Running U.S. History immediately in Grade 9 closes the seam. Grade 10
World History then stands on its own and is not chronologically dependent on Grade
9. Classification: `MANUEL_ACADEMY_COURSE_DESIGN_DECISION`. Michigan does not
mandate the order in which the two credits are taken; see
`credit-coverage-map.md`.

**Open item for the social-studies session.** Grade 8 covers world history through
approximately 1500 CE at Grade 7 and U.S. history to 1870 at Grade 8. Confirm
whether Grade 10 World History should begin at 1500 to meet Grade 7, and record the
answer in `subject-overview.md`.

## Health — `ma-g8-health` → `ma-g9-health` (Health and Wellness I)

**Grade 8 exit (observed, 6 units / 36 days).** Health systems, information and
self-advocacy; nutrition, movement and sleep without diet culture; mental health
and support networks; healthy relationships, consent and digital boundaries;
substance use and harm prevention; sexual health, disease prevention and community
action.

**Grade 9 entry expectation.** Health and Wellness I revisits every Grade 8 strand
at high-school depth and carries the credit-bearing half. It assumes prior
vocabulary and a non-shaming baseline, not prior mastery.

**Continuity ruling: CONTINUOUS, WITH A NAMED CADENCE CHANGE** — 36 weekly sessions
in Grade 8 to 90 daily sessions in one Grade 9 semester, to carry a half credit.

**Binding policy carry-forward.** The published policy at
`curriculum-content/manuel-academy/1.0.0/policies/instruction-mastery-accessibility-safety.md`
prohibits calorie targets, dieting, weight cutting, weigh-ins, body-size scoring,
earned-food framing and food morality, and requires guardian visibility for
safety-critical activity. These prohibitions apply unchanged to all four
high-school health courses. They are not Grade 8 conventions to be outgrown.

## Physical Education — `ma-g8-physical-education` → `ma-g9-physical-education`

**Grade 8 exit (observed, 9 units / 108 days).** Personal readiness and goal
design; training principles and fitness literacy; advanced game strategy;
individual and dual activities; dance and creative performance; outdoor, aquatic
and community activity planning; leadership, coaching and officiating; stress
management; a lifetime activity capstone.

**Grade 9 entry expectation.** Personal Fitness and Lifetime Activity assumes
fitness literacy and goal-setting are already established and moves to independent
programme design carrying the credit-bearing half.

**Continuity ruling: CONTINUOUS.**

**Binding policy carry-forward.** Body size, body weight, weight change, peer
comparison and speed alone remain non-assessable in all four high-school courses.

## Ready for Life — `ma-g8-ready-for-life` → `ma-g9-ready-for-life`

**Grade 8 exit (observed, 6 units / 36 days).** Independent planning and executive
routines; home operations and maintenance; meal planning and food management;
transportation, appointments and community navigation; work, communication and
career readiness; an independent-living capstone. Standards tag: *"Manuel Academy
RFL Grade 8 progression"* — a **local** progression, not a state framework.

**Grade 9 entry expectation.** Ready for Life 9 extends executive systems toward
self-directed high-school study management and opens the educational development
planning thread.

**Continuity ruling: CONTINUOUS.**

**Named gap — standards traceability, not content.** This is the one family whose
Grade 8 standards tags point at a Manuel Academy progression rather than a
published state framework. Grades 9–12 will inherit that. The Ready for Life
session must either bind the progression to an official Michigan employability or
career-development framework, or state plainly in `subject-overview.md` that the
family is locally defined and carries no state standards claim. Silently implying
state alignment is a contract violation. See the corresponding row in
`credit-coverage-map.md`.

## Technology and Computer Science — `ma-g8-technology` → `ma-g9-technology`

**Grade 8 exit (observed, 6 units / 36 days).** Digital identity, information and
AI literacy; programming and software design; web, app and accessible interface
design; data science and computational models; networks and cybersecurity; a
technology capstone with impact review. Tagged against Michigan Computer Science
strands (Impacts of Computing, Networks and the Internet, Algorithms and
Programming, Computing Systems, Data and Analysis, Cybersecurity).

**Grade 9 entry expectation.** Computer Science Principles and Digital Citizenship
assumes exposure to each strand and takes the student to a first formal treatment
of computing systems and algorithmic thinking, with Programming I following in
Grade 10.

**Continuity ruling: CONTINUOUS, WITH A NAMED CADENCE CHANGE** — 36 weekly sessions
to 90 daily sessions in a semester.

**Binding policy carry-forward.** The published policy forbids teaching filter
bypass or probing real systems, and requires disclosure of meaningful AI
assistance. The Grade 12 cybersecurity course is where this is most likely to be
strained; it is defensive and conceptual only.

## Arts and Music — `ma-g8-arts-and-music` → `ma-g9-arts-and-music`

**Grade 8 exit (observed, 6 units / 72 days).** Artistic voice and advanced
composition; design, media and materials; music composition, production and
performance; theatre, film and production collaboration; arts, identity, culture
and ethics; portfolio and public-or-private exhibition.

**Grade 9 entry expectation.** Foundations of Visual and Media Arts assumes a

**Continuity ruling: CONTINUOUS.** Cadence is preserved at 72 sessions (twice

**Note.** The credit-bearing arts requirement is carried across Grades 9 **and** 10
(0.5 + 0.5), not compressed into Grade 9. See `credit-coverage-map.md` for the
credit-hour basis, which needs a Director ruling.

## Financial Literacy — `ma-g8-financial-literacy` → `ma-g9-financial-literacy` (Personal Finance)

**Grade 8 exit (observed, 7 units / 72 days).** PF1 Earning Income; PF2 Buying
Goods and Services; PF3 Budgeting and Saving; PF4 Using Credit; PF5 Financial
Investing; PF6 Protecting and Insuring; PF7 Paying Taxes, plus a synthesis
capstone. The published release states Grade 8 explicitly covers Michigan PF1–PF7.

**Grade 9 entry expectation.** Personal Finance re-presents PF1–PF7 at the
high-school expectation level and is the course this contract treats as carrying
the personal finance requirement.

**Continuity ruling: DELIBERATE OVERLAP, NOT A GAP.**

**Why the overlap is intentional.** The published release notes that Michigan
guidance allows middle-school personal finance coursework to count for the required
half credit when the high-school expectations are presented and the learner
demonstrates proficiency — and that possessing the package does not by itself
establish proficiency or credit. Rather than depend on a Grade 8 proficiency
determination that may or may not have been made and recorded, this contract places
a full high-school Personal Finance course in Grade 9. If the Director has
evidence that the Grade 8 course met the high-school expectations with demonstrated
proficiency, Grade 9 may be re-scoped as an extension rather than a repeat; that is
a Director decision, recorded in `credit-coverage-map.md`, not a builder decision.

## World Language — NO GRADE 8 ANCHOR, NO GRADE 9 COURSE

There is no World Language subject family at Grade 8 and none is created here.
There is therefore no handoff to describe. This is the programme's largest
coverage gap and it is ruled on separately in `credit-coverage-map.md`. It is
recorded in this document so that the Grade 8 → 9 transition is not read as
complete.
