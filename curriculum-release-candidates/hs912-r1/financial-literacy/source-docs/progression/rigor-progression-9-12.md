# Rigor Progression, Financial Literacy 9-12

## The distribution decision, stated rather than implied

Michigan publishes **one** set of Personal Finance content expectations for the whole 9-12
band. It does not publish grade-9, grade-10, grade-11, and grade-12 sets. Any four-year
course sequence must therefore make a curricular decision that the state did not make, and
this document records ours so a reviewer can disagree with it explicitly.

**Decision: each high-school year sweeps all seven expectations at increasing sophistication,
rather than dividing the seven expectations between the four years.**

### Why sweep rather than divide

- **The requirement is a half credit, not four years.** Section 380.1278a requires a ½ credit
  course. A district or family may use any single year of this lane for that purpose. Dividing
  PF1-PF7 across four years would mean *no individual year* covered the expectations, so no
  single year could satisfy the requirement on its own — the opposite of useful.
- **It matches the predecessor.** The published Grade 8 course already sweeps all seven. A
  learner arriving in Grade 9 meets a familiar spine.
- **Personal finance topics are mutually entangled.** Taxes change take-home pay (PF1), which
  constrains a budget (PF3), which determines borrowing capacity (PF4), which is priced by
  risk (PF6). Teaching credit two years after income means teaching credit without income.
- **Spacing and retrieval.** Returning to each expectation annually at greater depth is a
  spaced-retrieval schedule, which is what the mastery rule in this lane already assumes.

### What the four years actually differ in

| Grade | Theme | The move that defines the year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | Foundations in Practice | One well-scoped decision, figures supplied. Compute it, don't just define it. |
| 10 | Comparison and Systems | Compare options; understand the institutions, contracts, and disclosures that structure them. |
| 11 | Analysis and Tradeoffs | Quantify multi-year consequences; reason about education, career, and opportunity cost under uncertainty. |
| 12 | Adult Finance Integration | Run all seven at once as one coherent simulated adult year, ending in the capstone. |

Concretely, PF4 "Using Credit" is:

- **Grade 9** — compute total cost of two fictional offers; distinguish aid from a loan.
- **Grade 10** — understand scoring, amortisation, and how aid packaging actually decides.
- **Grade 11** — compare repayment strategies across a debt set on total interest.
- **Grade 12** — finance a full pathway with a personal borrowing limit set before shopping,
  and project repayment against expected income.

The same escalation applies to each of the other six expectations; the per-grade topic sets in
`../authoring/blueprints.py` are the authoritative statement of it, and each course guide
prints its own year's version.

## Grade 12 capstone

Grade 12 unit 7 is the **simulated adult-finance capstone**: one fictional adult financial
year operated end to end — income and onboarding, spending, budget, credit and financing,
investing, protection, and a simulated tax filing — then presented and defended, including
what it sacrificed and what would break it.

It requires **no real transaction, account, application, or credential**. The validator
asserts the capstone exists, is flagged simulation-only, and demands no real transaction.

## Limits

Alignment is not approval. This is locally authored curriculum aligned to published
expectations; it is not a claim of state approval, accreditation, licensure, credit award, or
satisfaction of the Section 380.1278a requirement by any particular school or district. The
half-credit determination, and which credit it offsets, is a district or public school academy
decision.
