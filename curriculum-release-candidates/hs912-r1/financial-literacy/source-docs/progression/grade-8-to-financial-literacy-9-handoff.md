# Grade 8 → Financial Literacy 9 — Handoff

## What Grade 8 leaves

The published Grade 8 financial-literacy course
(`curriculum-content/manuel-academy/1.0.0/grades/grade-8/courses/financial-literacy`) is
**not modified by this lane**. It is read for continuity only.

It already organises its seven units as the seven Michigan Personal Finance content
expectations, tagging unit 4 with both `PF4` and `PF4.1`:

| Unit | Codes | Title |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `PF1` | Earning Income |
| 2 | `PF2` | Buying Goods and Services |
| 3 | `PF3` | Budgeting and Saving |
| 4 | `PF4`, `PF4.1` | Using Credit |
| 5 | `PF5` | Financial Investing |
| 6 | `PF6` | Protecting and Insuring |
| 7 | `PF7`, `PF1–PF7 synthesis` | Paying Taxes and Financial Plan Capstone |

Its shape is 7 units with lesson counts `[10, 10, 10, 11, 10, 10, 11]` = 72 lessons.

## What Financial Literacy 9 picks up

Grade 9 keeps **all three** of those inherited properties deliberately:

1. **The same seven-unit expectation spine.** A learner moving from Grade 8 into Grade 9
   meets the same seven organisers in the same order. Nothing is renamed or renumbered.
2. **The same code vocabulary.** `PF1`–`PF7` and `PF4.1`, matching both the published course
   and the retrieved source document.
3. **The same unit-length shape.** `[10, 10, 10, 11, 10, 10, 11]` = 72 lessons, so pacing,
   scheduling, and unit-boundary assumptions carry over unchanged. The validator asserts this
   equality against the Grade 8 file directly rather than restating it here.

## What changes at Grade 9

Grade 8 establishes the expectations. Grade 9 makes each one *operational* on a single,
well-scoped decision with supplied fictional figures — computing take-home pay rather than
describing it, building a budget that survives a stress test rather than listing categories,
comparing two credit offers on total cost rather than defining interest.

The escalation from there through Grades 10, 11, and 12 is set out in
`rigor-progression-9-12.md`.

## What does not change, ever

Simulated figures only. No real transaction. No credential, account number, government
identifier, or real balance requested at any point. No individualized financial advice. No
shaming of financial difficulty, and no requirement that a learner disclose real household
finances. These hold identically in Grade 8 and in all four high-school years, and the
validator enforces them on every one of the 288 high-school lessons.
