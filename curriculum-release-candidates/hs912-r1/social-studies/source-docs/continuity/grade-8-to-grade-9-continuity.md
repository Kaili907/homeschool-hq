# Grade 8 — Grade 9 Continuity

## The handoff

| | Grade 8 endpoint | Grade 9 entry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Course | `ma-g8-social-studies` | `ma-g9-social-studies` |
| Final / first unit | Unit 9: Civic Inquiry and Bridge to 1870–1898 | Unit 1: Foundations of High School United States History and Geography |
| Standards | Michigan Grade 8 U6.1 introduction, P3, P4 | USHG F1.1, USHG F1.2, USHG F1.3, P1, P2 |
| Then | — | Unit 2: Industrial and Urban America, 1870-1900 (USHG 6.1.1, USHG 6.1.2, USHG 6.1.3, USHG 6.1.4, P2) |

## Why this is the correct seam

The Michigan K-12 Social Studies Standards place USHG expectation **6.1** exactly at this boundary. The
source document marks 6.1 as introduced in Grade 8 and as the point at which high school USHG begins.

Grade 8 Unit 9 is titled "Civic Inquiry and Bridge to 1870–1898" and carries "Michigan Grade 8 U6.1
introduction". Grade 9 therefore:

1. **Unit 1** establishes high school disciplinary foundations (USHG F1.1–F1.3) — sourcing,
   corroboration, spatial reasoning, and argument from evidence, plus the foundations review of the
   ideals and transformations to 1877 that the source document assigns to high school F1.
2. **Unit 2** resumes content at **USHG 6.1.1–6.1.4**, the Grade 8 endpoint, and continues forward.

## No gap

No USHG era falls between the Grade 8 endpoint and the Grade 9 starting point. Grade 8 ends inside
Era 6.1; Grade 9 Unit 2 takes up Era 6.1 and runs through Era 9.

## No duplication

Grade 9 does not re-teach Grades 5 and 8 content as a second survey. The F1 foundations unit is framed
as disciplinary practice at high school rigor, which is what the source document specifies for high
school foundations, rather than a repeated narrative of Eras 1–5.

## Vertical progression of research and argument

| Grade | Argument demand | Source demand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | Evidence-based claim in a supported inquiry | Primary and secondary sources supplied or curated for the learner |
| 9 | Claim with named counterclaim; position paper | Learner retrieves and cites sources; distinguishes primary from secondary |
| 10 | Comparative claim across regions and scales | Multi-regional sources; maps and quantitative series |
| 11 | Policy and constitutional argument | Legal texts, statutes, and official economic data |
| 12 | Independent research argument defended publicly | Learner-assembled annotated dossier with documented provenance |
