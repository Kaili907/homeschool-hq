# Grade Placement and Course Sequence Rationale

## What Michigan requires

The Michigan Merit Curriculum requires **three social studies credits**:

| Component | Credit |
| --- | --- |
| United States History and Geography | 1.0 |
| World History and Geography | 1.0 |
| Economics | 0.5 |
| Civics | 0.5 |

## What Michigan does not require

**Michigan does not require a specific course, and it does not assign any of these credits to a specific
grade level.** The Michigan Department of Education states that there are no required courses for meeting
the social studies requirement, and that students may earn the credit in traditional courses or in
courses that integrate the social studies standards.

Every placement decision below is therefore a **Manuel Academy decision**, not a state mandate. Any
statement in this package that a course belongs in a particular grade describes Manuel Academy's
sequence only.

## The Manuel Academy sequence

| Grade | Course | Michigan component | Credit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | United States History and Geography | USHG | 1.0 |
| 10 | World History and Geography | WHG | 1.0 |
| 11 | Civics and Economics | Civics 0.5 + Economics 0.5 | 1.0 |
| 12 | Advanced Civic Inquiry, Research, and Policy Analysis | none — elective capstone | 1.0 elective |

The three required credits are complete at the end of Grade 11. Grade 12 is an additional elective.

## Why this order

**Grade 9 — U.S. History and Geography.** This is the one placement with a real curricular constraint
behind it. Michigan's own standards mark USHG expectation 6.1 as introduced in Grade 8 and as the point
where high school USHG begins. Manuel Academy's Grade 8 course ends at exactly that seam, in a unit
titled "Civic Inquiry and Bridge to 1870–1898". Putting USHG anywhere later would leave that thread
hanging for a year or more. The constraint is continuity, not state mandate.

**Grade 10 — World History and Geography.** WHG builds on the Grade 7 world history course and widens
scale from national to global. Placing it after Grade 9 lets learners carry a completed national
narrative into comparative work, and lets the two history credits sit next to each other while
disciplinary reading is being built.

**Grade 11 — Civics and Economics.** Both half-credits are analytic rather than narrative, and both
reward the historical grounding built in grades 9 and 10. Placing civics in Grade 11 also puts it close
to the age at which most learners register to vote. Combining them in one year keeps a single social
studies course per grade, consistent with the rest of Manuel Academy's schedule.

**Grade 12 — capstone.** With the required credits complete, the senior year is free for advanced,
independent work that would not fit inside a survey course.

## Alternate placements a family may choose

The package does not break if a family reorders it. Two common alternatives:

- **World history in Grade 9, U.S. history in Grade 10.** Workable, but it delays the Grade 8 seam by a
  year. If chosen, review Grade 8 Unit 9 before starting Grade 10.
- **Civics and economics split across grades 11 and 12.** Workable. The unit sets are already segmented
  by credit (civics units 1–5, economics units 6–9) and can be transcripted separately.

Course IDs, unit IDs, and lesson IDs encode the Manuel Academy grade placement. A family that reorders
courses should keep the identifiers stable and record the actual year of completion separately, rather
than renumbering.

## Credit and seat time

Each course is 108 sessions of 55–75 minutes across 36 weeks, which is 99–135 hours. Michigan
permits credit by demonstrated proficiency rather than seat time. Mastery in this package is evidenced by
independent application on at least two separate occasions, not by hours logged.

For Grade 11, the civics segment is 60 sessions and the economics segment is 48 sessions. Both exceed a
half-credit equivalent and should be transcripted separately.

## The personal finance credit is separate — do not count this course for it

Michigan separately requires a **0.5 credit in personal finance** (Public Act 165 of 2022, amending
section 380.1278a of the Revised School Code), beginning with students who entered 8th grade in 2023.

Grade 11 Unit 9 covers the Michigan **Economics** standard E4.1 Decision Making, which is personal
finance content inside the economics half-credit. **That coverage does not by itself satisfy the separate
personal finance credit.** Under the statute, the personal finance half-credit is applied against the
mathematics, arts, or world language requirement, as the district or academy determines — not against
social studies.

Families should satisfy the personal finance requirement through a dedicated course. Manuel Academy's
financial literacy course sits outside this package. Confirm the current requirement against MDE's
personal finance guidance before building a transcript.
