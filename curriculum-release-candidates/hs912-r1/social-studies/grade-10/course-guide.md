# Grade 10 Social Studies: World History and Geography — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g10-social-studies`
**Version:** 1.0.0
**Instructional sessions:** 108
**Typical session:** 55–75 minutes
**Cadence:** three times weekly across 36 weeks
**Credit:** 1.0 credit — World History and Geography (Michigan Merit Curriculum social studies requirement)

## Course description

A Michigan-aligned high school world history and geography course covering WHG Foundations and Eras 4 through 7, concluding with Contemporary Global Issues. Learners work across scales of time and space using maps, data, and multi-regional sources.

## Michigan alignment and placement

This course is aligned to the Michigan K-12 Social Studies Standards (v 6/19, Michigan Department of
Education). Michigan requires three social studies credits and specifies their content, but it does
**not** require a particular course in a particular grade. The grade placement used here is a Manuel
Academy decision, not a state mandate. See `../../../../course-sequence-and-placement.md`.

Alignment is locally authored. It is not a claim of state approval, state review, or automatic credit
award by any district.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and disciplinary practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Distinguish primary from secondary sources, establish provenance, and cite every source used.
3. Represent more than one well-sourced perspective on contested questions without flattening real differences.
4. Build and defend an argument from evidence, and revise it when the evidence changes.
5. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar source set, region, data set, or civic question.
6. Complete the course capstone: **A world-historical and geographic investigation of a contemporary global issue, defended with maps, data, and cited multi-regional evidence.**

## Instructional model

Each unit runs a recurring 12-session arc: launch and diagnostic; two concept models; two guided
practices; independent application; investigation or close reading; reteach and varied practice;
performance-task build; synthesis and review; unit assessment; correction and reflection. Lessons are
resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is always specified.

## Source integrity

This package names sources; it never reproduces their wording. No quotation text is supplied anywhere
in this course. Learners retrieve the actual document, map, or data set from the named public
repository and transcribe any quotation themselves. See `../../../../source-integrity/source-and-citation-policy.md`.

## Tutor boundaries

The adaptive tutor may help locate, read, contextualize, and analyze evidence, and may question a
learner's reasoning. The tutor may not draft, rewrite, or supply the wording of a graded argument,
thesis, or citation. A static, tutor-free path is always available. See
`../../../../tutor/tutor-boundaries.md`.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery. Mastery requires accurate independent evidence and
  successful transfer or retrieval on at least two separate occasions.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation or source use, application, error or claim analysis,
  connection, source provenance and citation, performance evidence, and reflection (43 points).
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, and alternate response modes are not failures.
