Michigan's K–12 Computer Science standards are organized into five core strands, used consistently across
the Manuel Academy elementary technology courses (Grades 3, 4, and 5): Computing Systems; Networks and the
Internet; Data and Analysis; Algorithms and Programming; Impacts of Computing. Michigan's own standards
document does not prescribe an exact grade-by-grade lesson split within the K–5 band; the unit order and
specific activities below are Manuel Academy's local curricular sequencing decision, scaffolded so each
strand reappears at increasing depth from Grade 3 to Grade 4 to Grade 5.

## Mapping status

Every unit and lesson standards entry in this package uses `mapping_status: canonical`-equivalent, human-
authored strand labels drawn directly from the source list above (no auto-generated or inferred codes). A
human curriculum reviewer should confirm final strand-to-lesson granularity against the published Michigan
K–12 Computer Science Standards document before this package is promoted out of `curriculum-authoring/`.

## Vertical alignment

This Grade 3–4 sequence is the direct on-ramp to the existing Grade 5 Technology and Computer Science
course (`curriculum-content/manuel-academy/1.0.0/grades/grade-5/courses/technology`), which uses the same
five strands at greater depth (introductory coding with sequence/events/loops/conditionals/variables, data
and source responsibility including AI-generated content checks, and design for a user). Grade 3 introduces
each strand concretely and largely unplugged; Grade 4 adds block-based logic, troubleshooting, and source
evaluation; Grade 5 formalizes algorithms, data storytelling, and user-centered design.
