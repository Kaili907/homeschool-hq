# Source Integrity — Text, Copyright, and Provenance

A literacy course lives on texts, which makes copyright a curriculum design constraint rather than a legal footnote. This package uses four categories of text and nothing else.

## The four permitted categories

| Category | What it means | Where it lives | Provenance recorded |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Original** | Written for Manuel Academy for this package. | `grades/grade-N/original-text-bank.json` and `.md`, embedded in full. | `source_type: original`, title, genre, author, rights statement, units used. |
| **Public domain** | A work whose United States copyright has expired. | `grades/grade-N/public-domain-register.json` — **reference only, never reproduced**. | Title, creator, first-publication year, translator and translation year where the work is a translation, form, the public-domain rationale, and the units that use it. |
| **Short lawful excerpt** | A brief passage quoted for teaching, with attribution, where the use is lawful. | Not embedded in this release. Permitted at facilitator discretion under the rules below. | Creator, title, date, where found, and the exact extent quoted. |
| **Learner or family selected** | A library book, a licensed text, or a family-approved text chosen locally. | Never in the package. Named in the local source record. | Recorded by the facilitator in the source record; not pasted into the package. |

## Hard rules

1. **No full copyrighted trade text is copied into this package.** Not a chapter, not a story, not a poem, not a passage of one.
2. **Public-domain works are referenced, not reproduced.** The register gives the facilitator everything needed to obtain the text lawfully from a public-domain repository, a library, or a printed edition. The package ships the citation, not the copy.
3. **Every text carries a source type.** Each lesson names the text it uses and that text's `source_type`. There is no unattributed passage anywhere in either course.
4. **Substitution is allowed; pasting is not.** A facilitator may substitute a library, licensed, public-domain, or family-approved text at the same target. That substitution is recorded in the local source record. Copyrighted text is not added to the package.
5. **Translations have their own copyright.** Where a public-domain work is a translation, the register records the translator and the translation year, because it is the translation that must be in the public domain, not only the original.

## Public-domain rationale used here

Works first published in the United States before 1930 are in the public domain in the United States. Every entry in both registers meets that test, and the register states the rationale on each entry. Facilitators outside the United States should confirm local status before reproducing a work.

## What the courses teach about sources

Source integrity is taught, not merely enforced.

- **Grade 3, Unit 9** teaches that copying is not note-taking, that a note is written in the learner's own words, and that a source is named by who made it, what it is, and where it was found.
- **Grade 4, Unit 9** teaches paraphrase as a checked skill: rewriting a source idea without borrowing its wording, then checking the paraphrase against the original for both accuracy and independence. It requires a source list, note categorization, and a statement of what the sources do not settle.
- **Grade 4, Unit 7** adds firsthand versus secondhand accounts, so learners see that provenance changes what a text can tell them.

## Sensitive content in source selection

Two public-domain entries are flagged in the registers because their period context requires care: a nineteenth-century narrative poem drawing on Indigenous material, and a late nineteenth-century story set in a colonial context. Both are usable, and both are taught under the existing sensitive-history-and-literature policy: accuracy, context, dignity, age-appropriate detail, no identity role-play, no required autobiographical disclosure, and an alternate text or private response mode available on request.
