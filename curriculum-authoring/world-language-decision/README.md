## Two findings that reframe the question

**1. 🟥 The MMC does not bind this family.** MCL 380.1278a(2) binds *school boards*, not
parents; MDE's *Nonpublic and Home School Information 2025–2026* states home-school
students "are not required to meet the MMC credit requirements"; and MCL 380.1561(3)(f)
lists the nine subjects Michigan actually requires of a homeschool — **world language is
not among them.**

**2. 🟦 The binding constraint is therefore the college transcript, not the statute.**
U-M (LSA) requires **2 years of the same language for admission**; MSU names 2 years in
its college-prep curriculum. **No MMC substitution touches either.** Arts, CTE, and the
personal-finance half-credit reduce a state requirement that does not apply here, while
leaving the requirement that does apply untouched.

## The substitution trap

Michigan's requirement is "2 credits **based on students meeting a 'Novice High'
proficiency level**." Substitutions reduce the credits, not the proficiency:

| Route | Actual Japanese | Reached | Novice High? |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Both substitutions | 0.5 cr (~90 hrs) | Novice Low–Mid | ❌ |
| Arts only | 1.0 cr (~180 hrs) | Novice Mid | ❌ |
| **No substitution** | **2.0 cr (~360 hrs)** | **Novice High** | ✅ |

Japanese is FSI **Category IV**. A fully-substituted pathway is credit-compliant and
**proficiency-non-compliant** — and must not be labelled Novice High on a transcript.

## Recommendation

**Source 2 credits of high-school Japanese externally** — online provider or
community-college dual enrolment — and elect the substitutions as free insurance rather
than as the plan. Two credits is the only figure that reaches Novice High *and* clears
the U-M/MSU floor, and it is what `Japanese-Year-1-Curriculum.md` anticipated in its own
closing section. Pair it with an external proficiency artifact (**JLPT N5/N4, ACTFL
AAPPL/OPI, STAMP, or AP Japanese**), which on a homeschool transcript is worth more than
a self-issued credit line.

## Standing

This is a curriculum and coverage design analysis. **It is not a legal opinion.** The
documentary evidence that the MMC does not bind a Michigan homeschool is clear and
one-directional, but whether to *rely* on it is the Director's call, with counsel if
wanted. Statutory and standards findings were produced by a single standards reviewer;
its carried uncertainties are recorded verbatim in
[`standards-and-proficiency-gap.md`](standards-and-proficiency-gap.md) §9 and include the
fact that **no framework named "MWLS" could be found** as distinct from MDE's 2007
*World Languages: Standards and Benchmarks*.
