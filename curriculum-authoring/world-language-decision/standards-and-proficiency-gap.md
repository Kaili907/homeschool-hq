# Standards and Proficiency Gap

**Reviewed:** 2026-08-12 by a single World Language standards reviewer.
**Standing:** curriculum and coverage design analysis. **Not a legal opinion.**

Layer tags: 🟥 STATE REQUIREMENT · 🟨 PERMITTED SUBSTITUTION · 🟦 MANUEL ACADEMY DECISION

## 1. 🟥 The state requirement

**MCL 380.1278a(2)**, Revised School Code (1976 PA 451). Verbatim:

> "the board of a school district or board of directors of a public school academy
> shall not award a high school diploma to a pupil unless the pupil has successfully
> completed **during grades K to 12** at least 2 credits that are grade-appropriate
> in a language other than English or course work or other learning experiences that
> are substantially equivalent to 2 credits in a language other than English, based
> on guidelines developed by the department."

| Fact | Status |
| --- | --- |
| **2 credits** | **Unchanged as of 2026.** Last amendment to §1278a is 2022 PA 105 (personal finance). Compilation read "complete through PA 20 of 2026" |
| **Grades K–12**, not 9–12 | Confirmed. The 2006 original said "grades 9 to 12"; 2014 legislation changed it to K–12 |
| Applies from | Graduating class of 2016 (pupils entering grade 3 in 2006) |
| ASL | Qualifies as a language other than English |
| Online delivery | Qualifies, wholly or in part |
| **Both credits must be the same language** | MDE World Language FAQ Q10: "the two credits must be in the same world language" |

**A pending bill is not law.** HB 4156 of 2025 would amend §1278a/§1278b and would allow
computer coding to count as a language other than English. It passed the House 4/17/2025,
was referred to Senate Education 4/22/2025, and **no further action is recorded.** It is
not law. Do not design against it.

> ⚠️ **Correction to this lane's own brief.** The reviewer was asked about "HB 4328" as the
> amending vehicle. That is wrong: HB 4328 of 2023 is a liquor-licence bill and HB 4328 of
> 2025 is a supplemental appropriations bill. Neither touches the Merit Curriculum. The
> premise was mistaken and is corrected here rather than carried forward.

## 2. 🟥 The requirement does not bind this family

This is the most consequential finding in the review, and it is better evidenced than
the repository's prior treatment of it.

**Two independent grounds:**

**(a) The statute binds boards, not families.** The operative clause governs what "the
board of a school district or board of directors of a public school academy" may do when
awarding a diploma. It regulates issuance of a *public* diploma. A parent issuing their
own diploma is outside its scope.

**(b) MDE says so in terms.** *Nonpublic and Home School Information 2025–2026*,
"Graduation Requirements":

> "The Michigan Merit Curriculum (MMC) graduation requirements are specific to public
> school districts and public-school academies (PSA). **Nonpublic schools and students
> being taught in home school environments are not required to meet the MMC credit
> requirements.**"

And, under "Course of Study": "**The state does not require specific content in the basic
courses.**"

**What Michigan actually requires of a homeschool — MCL 380.1561(3)(f):** an organized
educational program in

> "reading, spelling, mathematics, science, history, civics, literature, writing, and
> English grammar."

Nine subjects. **World language is not among them.** Neither are arts, PE, health, or
computer science. Under exemption (f) there is no reporting to MDE at all.

🟥 **Verdict: there is no state-mandated world-language requirement for a Michigan
homeschooler.** Any world-language study here is elective — driven by college admission
and transfer risk, not by law.

This does **not** dissolve the question. It relocates it: the binding constraint is the
college transcript (§6), not the statute.

## 3. 🟨 Permitted substitutions — and the ceiling

Three, all statutory, all currently in force:

| # | Substitution | Cap | Authority |
| ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| 1 | Department-approved formal CTE programme | 1.0 | MCL 380.1278a(2) |
| 2 | Visual/performing arts instruction **in addition to** the §(1)(a)(iv) requirement | 1.0 | MCL 380.1278a(2) |
| 3 | Personal finance half-credit directed at world language | 0.5 | MCL 380.1278a(3) |

**Ceiling: #1 and #2 share a single 1.0-credit cap** — the statute says "1 credit." At
least one credit can never be substituted away by CTE or arts.

🟨 **Computer science / coding is NOT a permitted substitute today.** That is precisely
what the stalled HB 4156 would add. The programme carries four technology courses; none
of them may be pointed at this requirement.

**Stale-source note, now resolved.** MDE's *Credit/Experience Guidelines — World
Languages* (9/2014) still limits the CTE and arts substitutions to pupils graduating
"in 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, or 2020 only." `standards-reference.md` already flagged
that sunset as absent from the current statute and advised treating the FAQ as superseded.
**The reviewer identified the mechanism: the date limits were repealed by 2020 PA 158**
(Imd. Eff. 2020-09-17). The repository's instinct was right; this supplies the citation.

### The arithmetic, re-derived

| Step | Remaining |
| --- | ---: |
| Requirement | 2.0 |
| − 1.0 (CTE **or** additional arts — one shared cap) | 1.0 |
| − 0.5 (personal finance, **iff** directed here) | 0.5 |
| **Irreducible remainder** | **0.5** |

This matches `credit-coverage-map.md` §E. One interpretive caveat the reviewer did not
resolve: whether the 1.0 substitution cap applies against the original 2.0 or against a
personal-finance-reduced 1.5. **Under either reading the remainder is at least 0.5**, so
the map's figure is safe.

## 4. 🟥 Standards framework and the proficiency target

**Document:** *Michigan Merit Curriculum — World Languages: Standards and Benchmarks*
(MDE, generally cited as 2007), plus *Credit/Experience Guidelines — World Languages*
(9/2014).

Michigan adopts the national **5 C's verbatim** as its five strands — Communication
(1.1 Interpersonal, 1.2 Interpretive, 1.3 Presentational), Cultures, Connections,
Comparisons, Communities — having "depended heavily on" *Standards for Foreign Language
Learning in the 21st Century* and the ACTFL Performance Guidelines and Proficiency
Guidelines.

**The repository's recorded code format is confirmed.** `standards-reference.md` lists
`<strand>.<standard>.<proficiency>.<domain>.<benchmark>` with examples `1.1.N.SL.a`,
`1.1.M.SL.d`, `1.1.A.SL.c`. The reviewer confirms the three proficiency tiers those
letters encode:

| Code | Tier |
| --- | --- |
| **N** | Novice High |
| **M** | Intermediate Low–Mid |
| **A** | Pre-Advanced |

Michigan uses three tiers, not ACTFL's nine sublevels. Benchmarks above `N` are explicitly
"provided to guide districts in developing curriculum" — **beyond** the graduation
requirement, not part of it.

**🟥 The proficiency target is Novice High, attached to the 2-credit requirement as a
whole.** MDE: *"The world language requirement is two credits based on students meeting a
'Novice High' proficiency level. Students can meet this credit requirement in any amount
of time."*

> ⚠️ **"MWLS" could not be verified as a distinct current framework.** The reviewer found
> no MDE document formally titled "Michigan World Language Standards" separate from the
> 2007 *Standards and Benchmarks*, and no evidence Michigan has adopted the 2015 ACTFL
> World-Readiness Standards as such. `standards-reference.md` is right to leave the version
> unstated. Treat the acronym as unconfirmed.

**Michigan publishes no per-credit proficiency benchmark** — only the 2-credit endpoint.

## 5. 🟥 Credit is proficiency-based, not seat-time

MDE MMC FAQ:

> "The MMC requires that credit be awarded based on a student's demonstration that he or
> she has successfully met the content expectations for the credit area, **not by the
> commonly used Carnegie unit, which is based on seat time.**"

**Test-out is statutory: MCL 380.1279b.** Credit on a grade of **not less than C+** on a
final exam, or on the course's basic assessment — "a portfolio, performance, paper,
project, or presentation." Recorded as pass, excluded from GPA, and it **must** count
toward subject-area requirements.

For world language specifically, MDE permits demonstrating one-year or two-year equivalent
proficiency on a district-approved assessment: two-year equivalent completes the
requirement outright.

🟦 **This is the most under-used lever available to this programme** — see §7.

**No Michigan hours-per-credit figure exists.** The reviewer searched and found none. The
~120-hour Carnegie convention and the 180-hour school year are non-Michigan reference
points and must not be attributed to MDE. MDE's own caution is pointed: *"Seat time alone
in a K-8 program does not ensure equivalent high school level 1 and 2 language
proficiency."*

## 6. 🟥 College expectations — the constraint that actually binds

| Institution | Requirement | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **U-M (LSA)** | **2 years of the same language required for admission**; 4 strongly encouraged; ASL counts | U-M KB art. 7514 |
| **MSU** | **"Two years of a single foreign language"** in the college-prep curriculum; absence "will put these applications at a disadvantage" | MSU first-year admission criteria |

Both converge on **2 years of the same language as the floor, 4 as the competitive
signal.** More selective out-of-state peers commonly want 3–4.

**No MMC substitution touches this.** Arts, CTE, and the personal-finance half-credit
reduce the *state* requirement; they do not produce a world-language line on a transcript.
🟦 **A programme that fully substitutes its way to MMC compliance still presents zero years
of language to U-M and MSU.**

**Homeschool transcripts need external corroboration.** U-M evaluates homeschooled
applicants on the same criteria but encourages supplementing with AP scores or graded
coursework from an accredited institution in the required academic subject areas,
world language included. For Japanese the artifacts that travel: **JLPT (N5/N4), ACTFL
AAPPL or OPI, STAMP, community-college coursework, or AP Japanese Language and Culture.**

*Partially verified:* U-M's main admissions page returned HTTP 403 and could not be read;
per-college variations beyond LSA rest on a secondary source. MSU publishes no
homeschool-specific world-language policy.

## 7. Japanese: the proficiency reality — and the finding that changes the build

**FSI classifies Japanese as Category IV** — "exceptionally difficult for native English
speakers" — at **88 weeks / 2,200 class hours**, against Category I Spanish and French at
30 weeks / 690 hours. That is a **~3.2× ratio**.

⚠️ **Do not apply that ratio to a high-school course.** It is calibrated to ILR 3
(roughly ACTFL Advanced/Superior) under full immersion, and it does not scale linearly
downward: most of the extra 1,500 hours is spent on the writing system, not on early
communicative competence. The Novice-band gap is far smaller than 3.2×.

**Realistic outcomes** (Delaware DOE proficiency targets, which publish a separate
**logographic**-language table — the cleanest published Japanese-applicable benchmark
found; ~180 hours per course year):

| Study | ≈ hours | Japanese outcome |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 1 credit | ~180 | **Novice Mid** |
| **2 credits** | **~360** | **Novice High** ✅ |
| 3–4 credits | ~540–720 | Intermediate Low speaking/listening; reading/writing lag ~1 further level |

The logographic table runs roughly a full course level behind the alphabetic one, and the
reading/writing lag is closer to two.

### 🟦 The finding that matters

**Two credits of Japanese lands exactly on Michigan's Novice High target.** The script does
not push a learner below the MMC bar on a full 2-credit sequence.

**But the substitution route does.** Substituting 1.0 credit (arts/CTE) plus 0.5 (personal
finance) leaves **0.5 credit ≈ 90 hours of actual Japanese — roughly Novice Low to Novice
Mid, short of Novice High.**

So the two things Michigan states are in tension for this language:

- the **credit** arithmetic is satisfied at 0.5 credit remaining; but
- the **proficiency** target — "2 credits **based on students meeting a Novice High
  proficiency level**" — is not reachable in 90 hours of Japanese.

🟦 **A maximally-substituted Japanese pathway is credit-compliant and
proficiency-non-compliant.** For a Category-IV language the substitutions buy paperwork,
not proficiency. `minimum-build.md` §1 previously assumed 0.5 credit could be built to
Novice High; **that assumption is withdrawn.** Any claim of Novice High on 0.5 credit of
Japanese would be a false claim on a transcript.

The clean resolutions are: build or buy the **full 2 credits** (hits both targets and the
college floor), or **choose a Category-I language** if the goal is minimum-cost compliance
— which would abandon the house language and the K–12 head start.

## 9. Carried uncertainties

Recorded so silence is not mistaken for confirmation.

1. **"MWLS" as a distinct current framework — not found.** Only the 2007 *Standards and
   Benchmarks*. No evidence Michigan adopted the 2015 ACTFL World-Readiness Standards.
2. **Michigan publishes no 1-credit proficiency benchmark.** The "1 credit ≈ Novice Mid"
   figure is **Delaware's**, not Michigan's, and Michigan has not endorsed it.
3. **Japanese hour figures are extrapolated**, not measured — Delaware course targets ×
   the 180-hour year convention. Reasoned estimates.
4. **The MCL compilation was current through PA 20 of 2026**, early in the year's act
   sequence. A later 2026 enactment would not yet appear.
5. **HB 4156's death is inferred from absence of recorded Senate action**, not from a
   formal disposition. The stronger evidence is the compilation itself.
6. **The Delaware targets document (8/1/16) is another state's guidance** and carries no
   authority in Michigan. It was used because Michigan publishes no logographic benchmark.
7. **FSI category:** state.gov currently shows Japanese in Category IV / 2,200 hours. The
   widely-repeated "Category V" and the difficulty asterisk come from older tables and
   **must not be cited to state.gov**.
8. **ACTFL's own research brief argues development is "roughly equal across languages"**,
   which cuts against the Delaware lag. Its data still shows Chinese needing 5 years to
   median Intermediate-Mid speaking, and no learners reaching Intermediate reading in 4
   years. The tension is real and is reported rather than resolved.
9. **U-M's main admissions page was unreadable (HTTP 403).** LSA's 2-year requirement is
   verified from the U-M KB; per-college variations are not.
10. **No Michigan hours-per-credit figure exists.** Carnegie/180-hour numbers are
    conventions, not MDE requirements.
