# Source and Citation Policy

This policy is binding on every course in this package.

## 1. No invented quotations

**This package contains no quotation from any historical, governmental, journalistic, or scholarly
source.** No lesson, assessment, unit, or guide reproduces or paraphrases a source's wording as though
quoted. Where a source matters, the package names it and directs retrieval.

Any quotation used in learner work must be transcribed by the learner from the retrieved source, with a
citation precise enough for another reader to locate the passage.

## 2. No fabricated primary sources

The package does not contain constructed, simulated, composite, or "representative" primary sources
presented as genuine. Every source named in `anchor_sources` is a real, publicly identifiable document,
collection, database, or repository holding. If a facilitator wants a constructed scenario for practice,
it must be labeled as constructed and must never be cited as historical evidence.

## 3. Primary and secondary sources are always distinguished

Every lesson requires the learner to identify each source as primary or secondary, state who produced it
and when, note the perspective it represents, and name at least one thing it does not show.

## 4. Provenance and corroboration

- Every source carries its holding institution or repository.
- Claims that matter to an argument require corroboration from an independent source.
- Grade 12 Unit 3 raises this to a formal provenance and reliability audit of the learner's whole dossier.

## 5. Named repositories

Retrieval is directed to public repositories, including:

- Library of Congress (loc.gov)
- U.S. National Archives (archives.gov)
- Avalon Project, Yale Law School (avalon.law.yale.edu)
- Michigan History Center / Archives of Michigan
- United Nations Digital Library (digitallibrary.un.org)
- U.S. Census Bureau (census.gov)
- Bureau of Labor Statistics (bls.gov)
- Federal Reserve Economic Data, FRED (fred.stlouisfed.org)

Facilitators may substitute an equivalent public, licensed, library, or family-approved holding of the
same document.

## 6. Multiple perspectives

Contested questions are represented with more than one well-sourced perspective. The package does not
resolve contested historical or policy questions on the learner's behalf, and it does not flatten real
differences between perspectives into a single settled answer.

## 7. Human dignity

War, genocide, enslavement, oppression, and displacement are handled with accurate context, documented
evidence, and human dignity. No learner is required to represent an identity or to argue for an injustice
in simulation.

## 8. Citation standard

Any recognized standard may be used consistently. Grade 12 Unit 3 names published citation standards.
The requirement is locatability: a reader must be able to find the exact source from the citation.
