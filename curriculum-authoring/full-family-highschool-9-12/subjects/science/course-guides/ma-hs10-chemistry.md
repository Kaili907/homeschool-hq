# Chemistry — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-hs10-chemistry`  
**Grade:** 10  
**Credit role:** Satisfies the chemistry-or-physics credit by name under MCL 380.1278b(1)(b)  
**Sessions:** 108 (36 weeks × 3 sessions)  
**Session length:** 55–75 minutes

## Description

A household-safe laboratory chemistry course covering the full HS-PS1 band from measurement and atomic structure through bonding, reactions, stoichiometry, energy, kinetics, equilibrium, and nuclear change. Because the course is named Chemistry, it satisfies the second statutory credit directly and no department determination of benchmark equivalency is required. No investigation uses concentrated acids or bases, compressed gases, a fume hood, or any chemical needing institutional disposal, and the prohibition on mixing household cleaning products is stated in every unit and cannot be disabled.

## Capstone

A chemistry engineering defense: analyse a materials or energy challenge, model a chemical solution with its energy and nuclear trade-offs, evaluate against prioritised criteria, and defend the revisions.

## Anchoring phenomena

Every unit opens on an observable phenomenon rather than a definition.

| Unit | Phenomenon |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Two people measure the same spoon of salt and get masses that differ by more than the salt they think they added. |
| 2 | Published footage shows sodium and potassium, neighbours in one column, reacting with water in strikingly similar ways while argon beside them does nothing at all. Neither metal is handled in this course. |
| 3 | Candle wax, table salt, and copper wire all look solid, but only one conducts, only one shatters, and only one melts in a warm room. |
| 4 | Recorded mass and temperature data show steel wool left damp in air gaining mass and warming, while identical wool kept dry does not change. |
| 5 | Baking soda and vinegar in an open cup lose mass as they fizz; the same reaction in a sealed bag does not. |
| 6 | One instant cold pack and one hand warmer are both sealed pouches you squeeze, and they do opposite things. |
| 7 | A glow stick snapped in ice water stays bright far longer than an identical one in warm water. |
| 8 | A sealed bottle of fizzy water looks perfectly still until the cap comes off. |
| 9 | A smoke detector contains a radioactive source, runs for a decade, and is safe to hold - while a comparable mass of the same isotope in another form would not be. |

## Investigations, hazards, and alternative paths

Each unit's Day 7 is an investigation, and Day 9 rebuilds the performance task with the same
materials and the same safety set. Every hazard, mitigation, supervision level, safe-order step,
stop condition, and disposal step below is rendered into the learner-visible lesson flow on those
days — none of it lives only in the guardian record. Every investigation has a no-special-equipment
alternative that meets the same learning target: a first-class path, not a consolation.

### Unit 1 — Density determination with repeated trials and stated uncertainty

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** kitchen scale reading to 1 g or better, measuring cup or graduated container, water, salt or sugar, small inert solid objects an adult has approved, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Spilled water makes floors slippery and can reach electrical outlets. | Work over a tray on a low surface away from outlets, and wipe spills immediately. |
| chemical | Household substances must never be combined experimentally. | This unit uses water, salt, and sugar only. Never mix cleaning products; bleach with ammonia or acid releases toxic gas. This prohibition is not disableable. |
| physical | An unknown solid chosen for the density measurement can be hazardous in itself. | An adult approves every solid. Never use a battery, a magnet, a medicine, a sealed pouch, or anything carrying a printed hazard symbol. |

**Safe order**

1. Confirm no cleaning product of any kind is in the work area. If one is, stop and remove it before anything else.
2. Set the tray on a low surface away from every outlet, cord, and the scale's battery compartment.
3. Have an adult approve each solid before it is weighed; nothing with a hazard symbol, no battery, magnet, medicine, or sealed pouch.
4. Zero the scale, then take mass before volume, recording each trial as it is made.
5. Wipe every spill the moment it happens.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop all work if any cleaning product is brought to the work area.
- Stop if liquid approaches an outlet, cord, or the scale's battery compartment.

**Disposal.** Pour salt and sugar solutions down the drain with the tap running, dry the scale and the surface, and return solids to their labelled containers. Nothing is combined with anything not on the materials list.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Determine density from a supplied set of published mass and volume measurements, computing uncertainty by hand. Paper and calculator only.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own repeated mass and volume trials. Accepted reference densities are looked up for comparison only, after the learner reports their own value.

### Unit 2 — Emission-spectrum identification and periodic trend analysis

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** published periodic-trend dataset, published emission-spectrum reference images, graph paper or spreadsheet, calculator, card tube and craft knife (adult-cut viewing slit), a CD, DVD, or sheet diffraction grating, an ordinary household lamp, night light, or LED

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Looking into a bright light source, or at the sun, damages vision permanently. | View household lamps only, from at least an arm's length, never the sun and never a laser. No optical device is ever pointed at the sun or at another person. This prohibition is not disableable. |
| physical | Cutting the viewing slit in the card tube uses a sharp blade. | An adult cuts the slit; the learner assembles and tapes only. No cutting tool is required of the learner. |

**Safe order**

1. An adult cuts the viewing slit in the card tube; the learner assembles and tapes only.
2. Tape the CD or grating over the far end and check the tube in a dim room before any bright source is used.
3. View household lamps only, from at least an arm's length. Never the sun, never a laser, never through any lens toward a bright source.
4. Sketch each spectrum before comparing it with the published reference images.
5. Graph the published periodic-trend data and write the predictions down before checking them.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if any light source is uncomfortable to look toward, and step back.
- Stop immediately if anyone points the spectroscope, or any lens or grating, toward the sun.

**Disposal.** Recycle the card tube, return the CD or grating to its sleeve, and file or recycle the printed spectra. No flame, fuel, heat source, or chemical is used anywhere in this investigation, so there is nothing hazardous to dispose of.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Skip the spectroscope build entirely: identify elements from published emission-spectrum images alone, and graph atomic radius, ionization energy, and electronegativity across periods and down groups from published data. Paper and calculator only. Both paths meet the same target.

**Data provenance.** Published periodic-property data and published emission-spectrum images the family retrieves, plus the learner's own sketches, graphs, and predictions. No identification is stated before observation.

### Unit 3 — Inferring particle forces from solubility, conductivity, and melting behaviour

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** table salt, sugar, cooking oil, water, clear plastic cups, spoons, thermometer, STAGE A only: rubbing alcohol (adult-dispensed), STAGE B only: 9V battery with taped terminals, leads, and a small LED, warm water below 50 degrees Celsius, published melting-point and conductivity tables

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | Rubbing alcohol is flammable, its vapour is heavier than air and pools at bench level, and it is harmful if swallowed. | STAGE A runs with no battery, charger, flame, hob, pilot light, heater, or lamp anywhere in the room. An adult dispenses no more than 30 mL per cup, caps the bottle, and removes it from the room at once. Nothing is ever tasted. |
| chemical | The poured alcohol samples - not just the bottle - are the ignition risk, and a shorting battery is an ignition source. | STAGE A ends when every POURED SAMPLE is down the drain, the cups washed and dried, and the room ventilated ten minutes - not when the bottle is capped. Only then may a battery enter, and STAGE B runs on a different surface. |
| chemical | Passing current through salt water is electrolysis: it produces hydrogen at one lead and chlorine gas at the other. | Each conductivity test is a touch of one to two seconds. Leads are never left standing in a solution. If bubbles form at a lead, disconnect at once, ventilate, and move to the published table. |
| physical | Battery testing risks a short circuit that heats the leads. | Use one 9V battery only - NEVER mains electricity, never a lithium or button cell. Keep the LED in the circuit at all times, tape the terminals before and after use, keep leads apart between tests, and let an adult make every connection. |
| physical | Warm water can scald. | An adult pours every warm sample and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius. Temperature is judged with the thermometer, never with a hand. |

**Safe order**

1. STAGE A and STAGE B never overlap. Stage A is finished and cleared before any battery leaves its packaging.
2. STAGE A: an adult confirms no battery, charger, flame, hob, pilot light, heater, or lamp is in the room, then pours no more than 30 mL of alcohol into each labelled cup and takes the bottle out of the room.
3. Observe and record the alcohol solubility results with the bottle gone and no ignition source present.
4. END OF STAGE A IS A DISPOSAL STEP, NOT A CAPPED BOTTLE: pour every poured alcohol sample down a running cold tap, wash and dry every cup, wipe the tray, and ventilate the room for at least ten minutes.
5. An adult then confirms out loud that no alcohol - bottle, cup, spill, or smell - is anywhere in the room, and that the surface is dry.
6. STAGE B runs on a DIFFERENT surface, with water-based samples only. Alcohol is never one of the liquids tested with the battery, and its conductivity is taken from the published table.
7. An adult builds the circuit with the LED always in it, touches each sample for one to two seconds, and disconnects between tests.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop and put the battery back in its packaging if any cup, spill, or smell of alcohol is still in the room.
- Never dip a lead, electrode, or wire into alcohol or into any liquid that is not on the STAGE B list.
- Stop and disconnect if bubbles appear at a lead, if a lead or the battery warms, or if the LED stays lit after a test.
- Stop if the thermometer reads above 50 degrees Celsius.
- Never connect anything in this lesson to a wall outlet.

**Disposal.** Alcohol samples go down the drain with the cold tap running at THE END OF STAGE A, before any battery appears; the cups are washed, dried, and put away. Salt, sugar, and oil solutions go down the drain with the tap running after Stage B. Disconnect the battery, tape its terminals, store it away from steel wool and loose metal, and wipe the surface. Nothing from this investigation is ever sealed, capped, or stored while wet. Alcohol and a battery are never left out together at any point.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Compare only salt, sugar, and oil in water at room temperature - solubility and mixing behaviour alone - and take every melting-point and conductivity figure from the published tables. No battery, no alcohol, no heat, and therefore no ignition risk at all. This is the path to choose if the two stages cannot be separated in time and space exactly as written.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own solubility and conductivity observations plus published melting-point tables. Rankings are inferred by the learner from what they observe.

### Unit 4 — Oxidation of steel wool and acid-base reactions with a natural indicator

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** plain steel wool (no soap), vinegar, baking soda, red cabbage, warm water below 50 degrees Celsius, poured by an adult, thermometer, clear cups, a wide OPEN metal or ceramic dish for the oxidation run, a scale used with a disposable tray under the sample, never the sample directly, gloves

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | Steel wool in vinegar is an iron-and-acid reaction that slowly releases hydrogen, which is flammable in air across a wide range of concentrations. | The oxidation run is NEVER put in a bag, jar, or any container with a lid. It stays in a wide open dish in a ventilated room, with no flame, hob, pilot light, heater, battery, charger, or static source anywhere near it. |
| chemical | Vinegar is a mild acid that stings eyes and open cuts. | Wear eye protection and gloves, cover any cut with a waterproof dressing, work over a tray, and rinse splashes with water for 15 minutes. |
| chemical | Combining household chemicals other than those listed can generate toxic gas. | Use ONLY vinegar, baking soda, and cabbage indicator. Never add bleach, ammonia, drain cleaner, or any cleaning product. This prohibition is not disableable. |
| physical | Warm water scalds, and household hot taps commonly deliver water above 55 degrees Celsius. | Cabbage indicator is extracted with water an adult has poured and checked at below 50 degrees Celsius. Hot tap water is never used directly, and temperature is judged with the thermometer, never with a hand. |
| physical | Steel wool fibres cut skin, and damp finely divided steel wool self-heats as it oxidises. | Wear gloves. Keep steel wool away from every battery and heat source, and never let it touch a 9V battery from an earlier unit. Warm or damp steel wool is never bagged, boxed, or binned - see the disposal steps. |

**Safe order**

1. Work in a ventilated room with no flame, hob, pilot light, heater, battery, charger, or spark source present: steel wool in vinegar releases hydrogen, which is flammable.
2. Put on eye protection and gloves, and cover any cut with a waterproof dressing.
3. An adult pours the water for the cabbage indicator and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius. Hot tap water is never used directly.
4. Make the indicator and complete the vinegar and baking-soda colour work first; that strand uses no steel wool at all.
5. Weigh the dry steel wool in a disposable tray on the scale - it never touches the scale surface, which stays in food use - then set it in a WIDE OPEN metal or ceramic dish. Never a bag, jar, box, or anything with a lid.
6. Leave the open dish undisturbed in a ventilated place away from paper, cloth, and anything that can burn, and re-weigh only once it is completely cool and dry.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop immediately if any cleaning product is brought near the work area.
- Stop if the steel wool or its dish feels warm: leave it open and undisturbed, ventilate the room, and wait until it is cold.
- Stop if the thermometer reads above 50 degrees Celsius.
- Never seal, bag, cap, or cover the oxidation run, and never open any inflated container near a flame, hob, or battery.

**Disposal.** NOTHING FROM THIS INVESTIGATION IS EVER SEALED. Leave the steel wool spread out in its open dish, well away from anything combustible, until it is completely cold and dry - normally overnight. Then rinse it under running water until the water runs clear, squeeze it out, and put it into the OUTDOOR waste bin still wet and cold, loose, never bagged and never with paper or a battery. If the dish is warm at any point, stop, leave the room ventilated, and wait. Pour the vinegar, baking-soda, and cabbage solutions down the drain with the tap running. Bin the disposable weighing tray; it never returns to food use.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run the cabbage-indicator colour scale with vinegar and baking soda only - no steel wool, no hydrogen, no cool-down - and analyse published mass-change data for the rusting reaction. Kitchen materials only. This is the path to choose if the household cannot leave an open dish ventilated and undisturbed overnight.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own mass measurements before and after oxidation, and their own indicator colour observations. Published data is used only for comparison.

### Unit 5 — Open versus closed system mass accounting

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** baking soda, vinegar, resealable freezer bags, clear cups, kitchen scale reading to 1 g or better, tray, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | Vinegar stings eyes, and the reaction produces carbon dioxide. | Wear eye protection and work in a ventilated room. At the quantities fixed below the carbon dioxide is harmless; the quantities are what keep it that way, so they are part of the procedure and not a suggestion. |
| physical | A closed bag holding a gas-producing reaction can burst and spray liquid. | The closed trial is SEALED COMPLETELY - a part-sealed bag is not a closed system - and is made safe by volume: half a teaspoon of baking soda and 20 mL of vinegar in a one-litre bag. Flat on a tray, pointed away, never held. |

**Safe order**

1. Work over a tray in a ventilated room with eye protection on.
2. Run the OPEN system first: weigh the cup of vinegar and the separate baking soda, combine them, and re-weigh once fizzing has stopped.
3. For the CLOSED system measure exactly: no more than half a teaspoon of baking soda and 20 mL of vinegar, in a one-litre freezer bag with the air pressed out.
4. SEAL THE BAG COMPLETELY. A part-sealed bag is not a closed system and cannot answer this unit's question; the small quantity and the free space, not a loose seal, are what keep it safe.
5. Lay the sealed bag flat on the tray at arm's length, pointed away from every face. Never hold, squeeze, or lift it while it reacts.
6. Weigh it without lifting it off the tray, and wait until fizzing has completely stopped before opening a corner.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop, do not touch the bag, and let an adult carry the whole tray outdoors if the bag becomes firm or taut.
- Stop if the quantities on the materials list cannot be measured accurately, and run the published-data path instead.
- Stop if liquid contacts the eyes and rinse for 15 minutes.

**Disposal.** Open the bag by one corner only, over the sink, away from every face; pour the contents down the drain with the tap running and rinse the bag before recycling or binning it. Wipe the tray and wash hands. Never re-seal a bag that still holds gas, and never store a sealed bag from this investigation.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Do the mass accounting from a supplied table of published open- and closed-system masses, balancing the equation and computing expected gas mass by hand. Paper and calculator only.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own before-and-after masses in both systems. The mass of gas lost is derived by the learner; no expected number is supplied first.

### Unit 6 — Measuring temperature change in dissolution and reaction

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** baking soda, vinegar, Epsom salt, pure calcium chloride ice-melt or food-grade calcium chloride in its original labelled container (optional), water, a thermometer kept for these investigations and never used with food or drink, disposable cups for the calcium chloride route - one standing inside a second for insulation - binned after a single use; no mug, tumbler, flask, bottle, or drinking cup ever holds calcium chloride, a disposable stirring stick for the calcium chloride route, binned after a single use, an insulated drinking cup for the Epsom-salt, baking-soda, and vinegar trials only; it never holds calcium chloride, kitchen scale used only with a disposable tray under the sample, so no sample touches the scale surface and it stays in food use, gloves, timer, one commercial cold pack and one hand warmer, OBSERVED SEALED and never opened

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | A cold pack holds a salt that irritates skin and eyes and is harmful if swallowed; a torn hand warmer releases self-heating iron powder reaching about 65 degrees Celsius, an inhalation and ignition hazard. | OBSERVED FROM THE OUTSIDE ONLY: never cut, torn, punctured, or opened, and the contents never touched or tasted. If one leaks, an adult bags and bins it and contact is rinsed 15 minutes. Not disableable. |
| chemical | Calcium chloride releases substantial heat when dissolving and can burn skin and eyes, and pouring water onto the solid spatters hot solution. | OPTIONAL. An adult handles the solid from its original labelled container - never bulk road salt. Eye protection and gloves; under one teaspoon; SOLID IS ALWAYS ADDED TO THE WATER, never the reverse; never touched warm. |
| chemical | Ice-melt calcium chloride is not a food grade and leaves residue on whatever holds, stirs, or measures it. | The calcium chloride route runs in disposable cups with a disposable stirrer, binned after one use. No mug, tumbler, flask, bottle, or drinking cup holds it, and nothing that touches it returns to food or drinking use. |
| physical | Solutions can become hot enough to burn. | Use an insulated cup on a tray, stir with the thermometer rather than a finger, judge temperature only from the thermometer, and stop if the solution exceeds 50 degrees Celsius. |

**Safe order**

1. Read the phenomenon with the commercial cold pack and hand warmer SEALED. They are squeezed and felt through the pouch only, and are never cut, torn, punctured, or opened.
2. Put on eye protection; if the calcium chloride route runs, put on gloves too and let the adult handle the solid from its original labelled container.
3. If the calcium chloride route runs, set out its DISPOSABLE cups and stirrer now. Calcium chloride is never put in a mug, tumbler, flask, bottle, or drinking cup, and nothing that touches it returns to food or drinking use.
4. Set the insulated cup on a tray and record the starting temperature before anything is added.
5. ALWAYS ADD THE SOLID TO THE WATER, a little at a time, never water to the solid. Keep every quantity under one teaspoon.
6. Stir with the thermometer, not a finger, and read temperature only from the thermometer.
7. Stop at 50 degrees Celsius and let the solution stand untouched until it is cool.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if anyone cuts, tears, punctures, or opens a commercial cold pack or hand warmer.
- Stop if the thermometer reads above 50 degrees Celsius.
- Stop if calcium chloride contacts skin and rinse thoroughly for 15 minutes.
- Skip the calcium chloride route entirely if eye protection and gloves are not available.

**Disposal.** Let every solution cool to room temperature in its OPEN cup before it is moved; never cap or store a warm solution. Pour cooled Epsom-salt, baking-soda, and vinegar solutions down the drain with the tap running. Dilute a cooled calcium chloride solution with plenty of cold water as it goes down the drain, then bin the disposable cups and stirrer that held it; they never return to food or drinking use. The cold pack and the hand warmer go into the household bin STILL SEALED - never opened, drained, or cut.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use Epsom salt and baking soda with vinegar only - both mild - to observe cooling and mild warming, or work entirely from a published table of dissolution enthalpies and bond energies. Kitchen materials or paper.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own temperature-versus-time measurements, plus published bond-energy tables for the model. No temperature change is predicted numerically before measurement.

### Unit 7 — Rate of an effervescent or enzymatic reaction under varied conditions

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** effervescent tablets or baking soda, vinegar at several dilutions, cold, room-temperature, and warm water, two identical glow sticks for the temperature strand, bent to activate and never cut, bitten, or opened, clear cups, timer, thermometer, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | A glow stick holds an internal glass ampoule, irritant esters, and hydrogen peroxide. | Bend to activate; never cut, bite, open, or mouth a glow stick. If one splits, nobody touches it: rinse any contact 15 minutes and an adult bags and bins it. This prohibition is not disableable. |
| chemical | Vinegar and effervescent tablets irritate eyes. | Wear eye protection, work over a tray, and rinse splashes with water. |
| physical | Warm water can scald and glass can break with thermal shock. | An adult pours every warm sample and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius. Use plastic or unbreakable cups only. |

**Safe order**

1. Bend the glow stick to activate it; never cut, bite, or open one, and never put one in a mouth.
2. An adult prepares the warm water and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius before any cup is placed.
3. Put on eye protection and set every cup on a tray.
4. Run one condition at a time, from cold to warm, recording each timing before anything is changed.
5. Never cap, seal, or cover a cup while it is fizzing.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if the thermometer reads above 50 degrees Celsius. Water temperature is judged with the thermometer, never with a hand.
- Stop if a glow stick splits or leaks, and let an adult bag and bin it.
- Never seal, cap, or cover a container during an effervescent reaction.

**Disposal.** Pour all vinegar and effervescent solutions down the drain with the tap running, rinse and dry the cups, and wipe the tray. A cup is never sealed, capped, or covered while it is still fizzing. A spent glow stick goes into the household bin intact. If one has split or leaked, nobody touches it: an adult bags it and bins it and any contact is rinsed for 15 minutes.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Vary only surface area and concentration at room temperature - whole versus crushed tablet, full-strength versus diluted vinegar - eliminating all heating. Or analyse a published rate-versus-temperature dataset. Kitchen materials or paper.

**Data provenance.** Learner-timed reaction completions across their own conditions. Rate relationships are inferred from the learner's plotted data.

### Unit 8 — Shifting a visible equilibrium with a natural indicator

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** red cabbage indicator, vinegar, baking soda solution, carbonated water, clear cups, droppers, thermometer, a cool water bath and a warm water bath below 50 degrees Celsius, poured by an adult, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | Acidic and basic solutions sting eyes and irritate skin. | Wear eye protection, work over a tray, use small volumes with droppers, and rinse any splash with water for 15 minutes. |
| chemical | Household chemical mixing beyond the listed materials can produce toxic gas. | Use ONLY cabbage indicator, vinegar, baking soda, and carbonated water. Never introduce a cleaning product. This prohibition is not disableable. |
| physical | A sealed carbonated bottle is a pressure vessel, and warming or shaking it turns the cap into a projectile. | A carbonated bottle is never warmed above 40 degrees Celsius, never shaken while closed, and is always opened slowly with the cap pointed away from every face. |
| physical | Warm water can scald. | An adult pours the warm bath and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius. Temperature is judged with the thermometer, never with a hand. |

**Safe order**

1. Confirm no cleaning product is in the work area; only cabbage indicator, vinegar, baking soda, and carbonated water are used.
2. Put on eye protection and set the droppers and cups on a tray.
3. An adult prepares the baths and confirms with the thermometer that the warm bath reads below 50 degrees Celsius, and that any closed carbonated bottle stays below 40.
4. Change one condition at a time and record the colour before making the next change.
5. Never shake a closed carbonated bottle; open it slowly, with the cap pointed away from every face.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if any solution contacts the eyes and rinse for 15 minutes.
- Stop if the thermometer reads above 50 degrees Celsius, or above 40 degrees Celsius for any closed carbonated bottle.
- Stop all work if a cleaning product enters the work area.

**Disposal.** Pour all indicator, vinegar, baking-soda, and carbonated solutions down the drain with the tap running, rinse the cups and droppers, and wipe the tray. Open and empty any carbonated bottle before recycling it. No solution is stored or reused.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Observe the carbon-dioxide equilibrium in carbonated water alone - sealed versus open, cool versus warm - with no indicator chemistry, or reason from a published equilibrium dataset. Kitchen materials or paper.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own colour and effervescence observations across conditions. Direction of shift is concluded by the learner from what they see.

### Unit 9 — Half-life modelling and a materials trade-off analysis

- **Supervision:** `none`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** 100 coins or dice, container, graph paper or spreadsheet, calculator, published decay dataset, published materials-property data

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | A domestic smoke detector contains a sealed radioactive source and is safe only while it stays intact. | A smoke detector is never opened, dismantled, or taken apart, and its source is never removed or handled. A working smoke alarm is a safety device and stays installed and intact. This prohibition is not disableable. |
| physical | Large numbers of small counters spread and can be a choking hazard. | Work in a shallow tray, keep counters away from young children, and count them back at the end. |
| emotional | Nuclear topics can raise anxiety about weapons or accidents. | Content stays on decay physics, energy scale, and engineering trade-offs; the learner may pause or redirect to the materials strand at any point. |

**Safe order**

1. Set the shallow tray down and move young children out of the work area before counters come out.
2. Count the 100 counters in and record the starting number.
3. Run each generation, removing and recording before the next throw.
4. Never open, dismantle, or take apart a smoke detector; it is discussed as a phenomenon and never handled as equipment.
5. Count every counter back in and confirm the total before leaving.

**Stop conditions**
