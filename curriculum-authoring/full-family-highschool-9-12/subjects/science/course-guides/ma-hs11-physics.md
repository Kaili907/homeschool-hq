# Physics — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-hs11-physics`  
**Grade:** 11  
**Credit role:** Third science credit  
**Sessions:** 108 (36 weeks × 3 sessions)  
**Session length:** 55–75 minutes

## Description

A quantitative laboratory physics course covering forces, momentum, gravitation and electrostatics, energy, thermal physics, fields and induction, waves, and electromagnetic radiation. Placed in Grade 11 so it follows Algebra I and Geometry and runs alongside Algebra II - a Manuel Academy sequencing decision, not a state requirement. All electrical work uses single low-voltage cells; mains electricity and lasers are excluded from the course entirely.

## Capstone

A physics engineering defense: design, build, and refine an energy-conversion or information-transmission device, and justify it with wave and energy principles plus the learner's own trial data.

## Anchoring phenomena

Every unit opens on an observable phenomenon rather than a definition.

| Unit | Phenomenon |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | A ball rolled down a ramp covers far more distance in its third second than its first, even though nothing pushes it. |
| 2 | A loaded wagon and an empty one, pulled with what feels like the same effort, reach very different speeds. |
| 3 | An egg dropped onto a folded towel survives a fall that shatters it on bare tile. |
| 4 | A charged balloon lifts hair against the pull of the entire Earth. |
| 5 | A bouncing ball returns to a lower height every bounce, yet nothing about it looks like it is losing anything. |
| 6 | A metal spoon and a wooden spoon left in the same room feel like different temperatures to the hand. |
| 7 | A bicycle light with no battery glows brighter the faster the wheel turns. |
| 8 | A straw in a glass of water looks broken at the surface, and the break shifts as you move your head. |
| 9 | A phone in a metal box loses signal completely, while the same phone behind a wooden door does not. |

## Investigations, hazards, and alternative paths

Each unit's Day 7 is an investigation. Every one has a no-special-equipment alternative that meets
the same learning target — the alternative is a first-class path, not a consolation.

### Unit 1 — Timing accelerated motion on a ramp

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** board or stiff cardboard for a ramp, ball or toy car, measuring tape, stopwatch or phone timer, books to set ramp height, masking tape, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | A ball leaving a ramp can trip someone or strike an object. | Set the ramp so the run ends against a soft barrier, clear the floor path, keep the ramp low, and keep pets and young children out of the area. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if the ball repeatedly leaves the intended path.
- Stop if anyone enters the run area.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Analyse a published motion dataset or a frame-numbered motion diagram supplied on paper, extracting velocity and acceleration by hand. No ramp, no timing, paper only.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own repeated timing trials over measured distances. Acceleration is computed from the learner's data, never supplied.

### Unit 2 — Varying mass and force on a rolling cart

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** toy car or cart, string, pulley or smooth table edge, small weights or coins, measuring tape, timer, kitchen scale, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Falling weights can land on feet or break flooring. | Keep masses small, place a cushion or towel below the drop zone, keep feet clear, and never stand under the hanging mass. |
| physical | String under tension can snap back. | Use light loads only, inspect the string before each run, and keep faces away from the string line. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if the string frays or the pulley slips.
- Stop if anyone's feet are in the drop zone.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use a published force-mass-acceleration dataset and a supplied set of free-body diagrams; derive the relationship graphically. Paper and calculator only.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own timing and mass measurements across conditions. The proportionality is fitted by the learner from their own plotted data.

### Unit 3 — Impact-protection design and collision trials

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** raw eggs or a small fragile object, padding materials from the household, measuring tape, tray or tarp for the landing zone, toy cars for the collision strand, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Dropping objects from height risks falls and impacts. | Drop from standing height at most, never from a ladder, chair, or window. The learner stands clear of the landing zone. |
| physical | Raw egg is a salmonella exposure and a slip hazard. | Contain every drop in a tray or tarp, wear gloves or wash hands thoroughly after each trial, disinfect the area, and never eat or drink in the work area. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if anyone climbs on furniture to increase drop height.
- Stop and clean thoroughly if raw egg contacts skin, and wash hands before touching anything else.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use a sealed water balloon, a small beanbag, or a marshmallow instead of an egg - no biological hazard, no cleanup - or run the collision strand with toy cars only and analyse published crash-test data for the protective design. 

**Data provenance.** Learner's own survival counts, drop heights, and cart velocities. Impulse comparisons come from the learner's own trials.

### Unit 4 — Electrostatic force behaviour and inverse-square modelling

- **Supervision:** `none`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** balloons, wool or hair, small paper pieces, thread, ruler, flashlight and paper (light-intensity inverse-square route), notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Balloons can pop suddenly and are a choking hazard for young children. | Keep balloon fragments away from young children, and anyone with a latex allergy uses the light-intensity route instead. |
| physical | Static discharge is startling though harmless at this scale. | Keep all static work away from electronics and never perform static work near a computer or a fuel source. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop static work near any electronic device.
- Switch to the light route if anyone present has a latex allergy.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Demonstrate the inverse-square relationship with a flashlight and a ruler, measuring illuminated area against distance, then apply the same mathematics to gravitation and Coulomb's law using published masses and charges. Household items and paper.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own separation-versus-effect observations and their own calculations from published masses, charges, and distances.

### Unit 5 — Energy accounting for a bouncing ball or a pendulum

- **Supervision:** `none`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** bouncy ball or pendulum bob on string, measuring tape, timer, kitchen scale, spreadsheet or graph paper, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | A swinging pendulum or bouncing ball can strike a person or object. | Clear a radius larger than the string length, keep the bob light, swing at small angles, and keep faces and screens out of the swing plane. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if the bob or ball leaves the cleared area.
- Stop if the string shows any sign of fraying.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Build the energy budget from a supplied table of published bounce heights or pendulum periods, computing the model by hand. Paper and calculator only.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own height, mass, and time measurements. Energy losses are computed by the learner from their own numbers.

### Unit 6 — Thermal equilibration in an insulated system

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** insulated cups or a thermos, thermometer, warm and cool water, kitchen scale, timer, notebook, assorted materials for the insulation strand

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Hot water scalds, and the risk rises with temperature. | Keep all water below 50 degrees Celsius. An adult measures and pours every warm sample. The learner never carries an open container of warm water. |
| physical | Thermometers can break; mercury thermometers must never be used. | Use a digital or alcohol thermometer only. If any thermometer breaks, an adult clears it. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if water exceeds 50 degrees Celsius.
- Stop and let an adult clear any broken thermometer.
- Never use a mercury thermometer for any part of this unit.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use a published cooling-curve dataset, or run the whole investigation at room temperature versus refrigerator temperature - no heating at all. Both meet the equilibration target.

**Data provenance.** Learner-recorded temperature-versus-time series for their own system. Final equilibrium temperature is measured, then compared with the learner's own prediction.

### Unit 7 — Electromagnet construction and induction with a coil and magnet

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** insulated copper wire, iron nail, D-cell or AA battery with holder, compass, strong magnet, coil of many wire turns, sensitive multimeter or LED, paper clips

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | A wire connected directly across a battery heats rapidly and can burn skin or start a fire. | Always include the nail or coil as resistance, use a single 1.5 V cell - NEVER mains electricity and never a lithium or button cell - connect only for a few seconds at a time, and disconnect between trials. An adult supervises every connection. |
| physical | Strong neodymium magnets pinch skin severely and destroy electronics and cards. | Handle magnets one at a time, keep them well away from phones, laptops, bank cards, and any medical device such as a pacemaker, and store them separated. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop immediately and disconnect if the wire, battery, or nail becomes warm.
- Stop if a magnet approaches any electronic device or anyone with an implanted medical device.
- Never connect any part of this investigation to a wall outlet.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Map a magnetic field with a compass and a permanent magnet only - no battery, no circuit - and study induction from published data or a supplied diagram sequence. Compass and paper only.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own compass deflections, paper-clip counts, and meter or LED observations across conditions.

### Unit 8 — Wave speed in a spring and light behaviour at boundaries

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** long spring or rope, measuring tape, timer, shallow tray of water, flashlight or LED, CD or DVD as a diffraction grating, clear container, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | A stretched spring released under tension snaps back hard and can injure eyes. | Two people hold the ends, never stretch beyond about twice the resting length, keep faces away from the line of the spring, and release tension slowly and deliberately. |
| physical | Bright light sources can damage vision. | Use a flashlight or LED only. NO LASER of any kind is used in this unit. Never look directly into any light source or point one at another person. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if the spring is stretched near its limit or if anyone is in its recoil path.
- Stop if any light source is pointed toward a face.
- Do not substitute a laser pointer for the flashlight under any circumstances.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Measure wave speed on a rope on the floor - no tension, no recoil - and observe diffraction with a CD held under a household lamp. Or work from published wave-speed tables and a supplied refraction diagram. No spring, no bright source.

**Data provenance.** Learner-timed pulse round trips over measured lengths and their own boundary observations. Wave speed is computed from the learner's own data.

### Unit 9 — Signal transmission, shielding, and an energy-conversion device build

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** small radio or phone, aluminium foil, cardboard boxes, assorted household materials, small motor or LED, battery, published claim article about radiation, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Building a device involves cutting and small parts. | An adult performs all cutting with sharp tools, the learner uses blunt scissors or pre-cut parts, and small parts stay away from young children. |
| physical | Battery-powered builds can short and heat. | Single 1.5 V cells only - never mains electricity, never a lithium or button cell. Disconnect between tests and stop if anything warms. |
| online | Evaluating published radiation claims means reading sources of uncertain quality. | An adult approves each source in advance. No account creation, sign-in, purchase, or personal information is required, and the learner never contacts a site owner. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if any component becomes warm.
- Stop if a source requests an account, payment, or personal information.
- Never connect any build to a wall outlet.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run the shielding test with a radio and household materials only - no build - and complete the capstone as a documented paper design with a modelled energy budget rather than a physical device.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own signal-strength observations across shielding materials and their own device performance trials. Published claims are evaluated, never restated as findings.
