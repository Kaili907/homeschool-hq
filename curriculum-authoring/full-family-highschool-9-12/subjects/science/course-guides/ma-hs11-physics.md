# Physics — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-hs11-physics`  
**Grade:** 11  
**Credit role:** Third science credit  
**Sessions:** 108 (36 weeks × 3 sessions)  
**Session length:** 55–75 minutes

## Description

A quantitative laboratory physics course covering forces, momentum, gravitation and electrostatics, energy, thermal physics, fields and induction, waves, and electromagnetic radiation. Placed in Grade 11 so it follows Algebra I and Geometry and runs alongside Algebra II - a Manuel Academy sequencing decision, not a state requirement. All electrical work uses single low-voltage cells; mains electricity and lasers are excluded from the course entirely.

## Capstone

A physics engineering defense: design, build, and refine an energy-conversion or information-transmission device, and justify it with wave and energy principles plus the learner's own trial data.

## Anchoring phenomena

Every unit opens on an observable phenomenon rather than a definition.

| Unit | Phenomenon |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | A ball rolled down a ramp covers far more distance in its third second than its first, even though nothing pushes it. |
| 2 | A loaded wagon and an empty one, pulled with what feels like the same effort, reach very different speeds. |
| 3 | An egg dropped onto a folded towel survives a fall that shatters it on bare tile. |
| 4 | A charged balloon lifts hair against the pull of the entire Earth. |
| 5 | A bouncing ball returns to a lower height every bounce, yet nothing about it looks like it is losing anything. |
| 6 | A metal spoon and a wooden spoon left in the same room feel like different temperatures to the hand. |
| 7 | A bicycle light with no battery glows brighter the faster the wheel turns. |
| 8 | A straw in a glass of water looks broken at the surface, and the break shifts as you move your head. |
| 9 | A phone in a metal box loses signal completely, while the same phone behind a wooden door does not. |

## Investigations, hazards, and alternative paths

Each unit's Day 7 is an investigation, and Day 9 rebuilds the performance task with the same
materials and the same safety set. Every hazard, mitigation, supervision level, safe-order step,
stop condition, and disposal step below is rendered into the learner-visible lesson flow on those
days — none of it lives only in the guardian record. Every investigation has a no-special-equipment
alternative that meets the same learning target: a first-class path, not a consolation.

### Unit 1 — Timing accelerated motion on a ramp

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** board or stiff cardboard for a ramp, ball or toy car, measuring tape, stopwatch or phone timer, books to set ramp height, masking tape, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | A ball leaving a ramp can trip someone or strike an object. | Set the ramp so the run ends against a soft barrier, clear the floor path, keep the raised end no higher than 30 cm, use a rolling object under 200 g, and keep pets and young children out of the area. |

**Safe order**

1. Clear the run and the floor beyond it, and set a soft barrier at the end before the first release.
2. Set the raised end no higher than 30 cm, tape the start line, and keep pets and young children out of the area.
3. Release from the same marked point every trial and record each timing immediately.
4. Retrieve the rolling object only after it has stopped.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if the ball repeatedly leaves the intended path.
- Stop if anyone enters the run area.

**Disposal.** Take the ramp down, peel off the masking tape, and put the ball and books away. Nothing hazardous is produced or left out.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Analyse a published motion dataset or a frame-numbered motion diagram supplied on paper, extracting velocity and acceleration by hand. No ramp, no timing, paper only.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own repeated timing trials over measured distances. Acceleration is computed from the learner's data, never supplied.

### Unit 2 — Varying mass and force on a rolling cart

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** toy car or cart, string, pulley or smooth table edge, small weights or coins, a cushion or folded towel for the drop zone below the hanging mass, measuring tape, timer, kitchen scale, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Falling weights can land on feet or break flooring. | Keep the hanging mass under 200 g, place a cushion or towel below the drop zone, keep feet clear, and never stand under the hanging mass. |
| physical | String under tension can snap back toward the face. | Wear eye protection whenever the string is under tension, use light loads only, inspect the string along its whole length before each run, and stand to the side of the string line rather than in it. |

**Safe order**

1. Put a cushion or folded towel under the drop zone and keep every foot clear of it.
2. Put on eye protection before the string is put under any tension.
3. Inspect the string along its whole length before each run and replace it at the first sign of fraying.
4. Start with the lightest load and add mass, up to 200 g, only after a clean run.
5. Stand to the side of the string line, never in it, and never under the hanging mass.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if the string frays or the pulley slips.
- Stop if anyone's feet are in the drop zone.

**Disposal.** Coil the string and put the weights back into a container out of reach of young children. Nothing hazardous is produced.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use a published force-mass-acceleration dataset and a supplied set of free-body diagrams; derive the relationship graphically. Paper and calculator only.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own timing and mass measurements across conditions. The proportionality is fitted by the learner from their own plotted data.

### Unit 3 — Impact-protection design and collision trials

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** raw eggs or a small fragile object, padding materials from the household, measuring tape, tray or tarp for the landing zone, toy cars for the collision strand, a bin liner for the broken egg and any padding that touched it, one household disinfectant spray or wipe, used as labelled and never mixed with any other cleaning product, gloves, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Dropping objects from height risks falls and impacts. | Drop from standing height at most, never from a ladder, chair, or window. The learner stands clear of the landing zone. |
| physical | Raw egg is a salmonella exposure and a slip hazard. | Contain every drop in a tray or tarp, wear gloves whenever anyone has a cut or graze, wash hands thoroughly after every trial without exception, disinfect the area, and never eat or drink in the work area. |

**Safe order**

1. Line the landing zone with the tray or tarp before any egg leaves its box.
2. Put on gloves if anyone has a cut or graze, and drop from standing height only - never from a chair, ladder, step, or window.
3. Keep everyone clear of the landing zone for every drop.
4. Clean and disinfect after every broken egg before the next trial begins.
5. Wash hands thoroughly after every trial, and never eat or drink in the work area.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if anyone climbs on furniture to increase drop height.
- Stop and clean thoroughly if raw egg contacts skin, and wash hands before touching anything else.

**Disposal.** Broken egg, shell, and any padding that touched raw egg go straight into a bin liner and into the bin - contaminated padding is never reused and never laundered with household washing. Wash the tray, tarp, and surfaces with hot soapy water and disinfect them. Bin the gloves and wash hands thoroughly before any food is handled.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use a sealed water balloon, a small beanbag, or a marshmallow instead of an egg - no biological hazard, no cleanup - or run the collision strand with toy cars only and analyse published crash-test data for the protective design. Both paths meet the same momentum and impulse target.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own survival counts, drop heights, and cart velocities. Impulse comparisons come from the learner's own trials.

### Unit 4 — Electrostatic force behaviour and inverse-square modelling

- **Supervision:** `none`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** balloons, wool or hair, small paper pieces, thread, ruler, flashlight and paper (light-intensity inverse-square route), notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Balloons can pop suddenly and are a choking hazard for young children. | No balloon is inflated where a child under four is present. Keep whole balloons and every fragment away from young children, and anyone with a latex allergy uses the light-intensity route instead. |
| physical | Static discharge is startling though harmless at this scale, but a spark near fuel vapour is not. | Keep all static work away from electronics, and never perform it near a computer, a gas hob, a petrol can, a fuel-storage shed, an aerosol, or any flammable liquid. |

**Safe order**

1. Ask about latex allergy before a balloon is opened, and check no child under four is present. If either applies, run the light-intensity route instead.
2. Move phones, laptops, cards, and every electronic device off the work surface before any static work.
3. Check that no gas hob, petrol can, aerosol, or flammable liquid is anywhere near the work area.
4. Charge and test one balloon at a time, keeping fragments off the floor.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop static work near any electronic device.
- Switch to the light route if anyone present has a latex allergy.

**Disposal.** Deflate the balloons and put whole balloons and every fragment straight into the bin - fragments are a choking hazard for young children. Recycle the paper pieces. Nothing hazardous is produced.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Measure illuminated area against distance with a flashlight and a ruler and work the relationship out from your own measurements; no relationship and no expected value is supplied in advance. Then apply the same mathematics to gravitation and Coulomb's law using published masses and charges. Household items and paper.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own separation-versus-effect observations and their own calculations from published masses, charges, and distances.

### Unit 5 — Energy accounting for a bouncing ball or a pendulum

- **Supervision:** `none`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** bouncy ball or pendulum bob on string, measuring tape, timer, kitchen scale, spreadsheet or graph paper, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | A swinging pendulum or bouncing ball can strike a person or object. | Clear a radius larger than the string length, keep the bob under 200 g, swing at small angles, and keep faces and screens out of the swing plane. |

**Safe order**

1. Clear a radius larger than the string length and move screens, faces, and breakables out of the swing plane.
2. Check the string for fraying before each set of trials.
3. Keep the bob under 200 g and release from small angles only.
4. Record each trial before changing anything, and stop the bob by hand only once it has slowed.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if the bob or ball leaves the cleared area.
- Stop if the string shows any sign of fraying.

**Disposal.** Untie and coil the string and put the ball or bob away. Nothing hazardous is produced.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Build the energy budget from a supplied table of published bounce heights or pendulum periods, computing the model by hand. Paper and calculator only.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own height, mass, and time measurements. Energy losses are computed by the learner from their own numbers.

### Unit 6 — Thermal equilibration in an insulated system

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** insulated cups or a thermos, thermometer, warm and cool water, kitchen scale, timer, notebook, assorted materials for the insulation strand, stiff card and a rigid container for an adult to clear a broken thermometer

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Hot water scalds, and the risk rises with temperature. | Keep all water below 50 degrees Celsius. An adult measures and pours every warm sample. The learner never carries an open container of warm water. |
| physical | Thermometers can break; mercury thermometers must never be used. | Use a digital or alcohol thermometer only. If any thermometer breaks, an adult clears it. |

**Safe order**

1. An adult measures and pours every warm sample and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius; the learner never carries an open container of warm water.
2. Use a digital or alcohol thermometer only. A mercury thermometer is never used for any part of this unit.
3. Record the starting temperature of each component before they are combined.
4. Combine, close the insulated cup, and read at fixed intervals rather than opening it repeatedly.
5. Let everything cool before it is emptied.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if water exceeds 50 degrees Celsius.
- Stop and let an adult clear any broken thermometer.
- Never use a mercury thermometer for any part of this unit.

**Disposal.** Pour the cooled water down the drain, dry the cups and thermos, and put the thermometer away in its case. If any thermometer breaks, an adult clears it: pieces are lifted with stiff card into a rigid container and binned separately, never with bare hands and never with a vacuum cleaner.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use a published cooling-curve dataset, or run the whole investigation at room temperature versus refrigerator temperature - no heating at all. Both meet the equilibration target.

**Data provenance.** Learner-recorded temperature-versus-time series for their own system. Final equilibrium temperature is measured, then compared with the learner's own prediction.

### Unit 7 — Electromagnet construction and induction with a coil and magnet

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** at least 1 m of thin 28-30 AWG enamelled copper wire, iron nail, single 1.5 V D-cell or AA with holder, compass, strong magnet, coil of many wire turns, multimeter set to a voltage range, paper clips, tray, tape for the cell terminals, a closed labelled box for the magnets and a labelled container for used cells

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Swallowed magnets are a surgical emergency: two or more magnets, or a magnet and any swallowed metal object, attract through the wall of the gut and cut off its blood supply. | Count the magnets out and back in every session, work over a tray, never bring one near a mouth, and store them closed and labelled out of reach of children and pets. A suspected swallow is an emergency: go at once. |
| physical | A wire connected across a battery heats rapidly and can burn skin or start a fire, and a nail is a magnetic core rather than a resistance. | Use at least 1 m of thin 28-30 AWG enamelled wire so the coil has real resistance; a short or thick wire is a short circuit even with a nail through it. One 1.5 V cell only - NEVER mains, lithium, or button cell. |
| physical | A multimeter left on a current range and placed across a battery ruptures its fuse and can arc. | The multimeter stays on a voltage range for every measurement in this investigation. A current range is never placed across a cell. |
| physical | Strong neodymium magnets pinch skin severely and destroy electronics and cards. | Handle magnets one at a time, keep them well away from phones, laptops, bank cards, and any medical device such as a pacemaker, and store them separated. |

**Safe order**

1. Count the magnets and record the total before anything else, and keep them on a tray so none can reach the floor.
2. Move phones, laptops, and bank cards well away, and keep anyone with an implanted medical device out of the work area.
3. Build the coil with at least 1 m of thin enamelled wire so it has real resistance; the nail is a core, not a resistance.
4. An adult makes every connection, with the coil always in the circuit, for a few seconds at a time, disconnecting between trials.
5. Keep the multimeter on a voltage range throughout; a current range is never placed across a cell.
6. Count every magnet back into its closed labelled box and disconnect the cell before anyone leaves the work area.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop and account for every magnet before anyone leaves the work area.
- Stop immediately and disconnect if the wire, battery, or nail becomes warm.
- Stop if a magnet approaches any electronic device, anyone with an implanted medical device, or anyone's mouth.
- Never connect any part of this investigation to a wall outlet.

**Disposal.** Disconnect and remove the cell, tape its terminals, and store used cells in a labelled container for household battery recycling - never loose in a drawer, never in the general bin, and never near steel wool. Magnets go back into a closed labelled box, counted, out of reach of children and pets. Wire and nail are put away. No lithium or button cell is used anywhere in this investigation.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Map a magnetic field with a compass and a permanent magnet only - no battery, no circuit - and study induction from published data or a supplied diagram sequence. Compass and paper only.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own compass deflections, paper-clip counts, and meter or LED observations across conditions.

### Unit 8 — Wave speed in a spring and light behaviour at boundaries

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** long spring or rope, measuring tape, timer, shallow tray of water, flashlight or LED, CD or DVD as a diffraction grating, clear container, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | A stretched spring released under tension snaps back hard and can injure eyes. | Everyone wears eye protection whenever the spring is under tension. Two people hold the ends, never stretch beyond about twice the resting length, never let go while stretched, and release slowly together. |
| physical | Bright light sources can damage vision. | Use a flashlight or LED only. NO LASER of any kind is used in this unit. Never look directly into any light source or point one at another person. |
| physical | A snapped or scored CD throws sharp shards. | Use an intact CD or a sheet diffraction grating. Never cut, score, or snap a disc. |

**Safe order**

1. Everyone in the room puts on eye protection before the spring is stretched at all.
2. Two people take the ends and everyone else stands clear of the line of the spring.
3. Stretch to no more than about twice the resting length and never let go while it is stretched.
4. Run the pulse timings, then release the tension slowly and deliberately together.
5. For the light strand use a flashlight or LED only, pointed at the tray or the intact CD and never toward a face. No laser is used, and no disc is cut, scored, or snapped.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if the spring is stretched near its limit or if anyone is in its recoil path.
- Stop if any light source is pointed toward a face.
- Do not substitute a laser pointer for the flashlight under any circumstances.

**Disposal.** Coil the spring or rope loosely and put it away, empty the water tray down the drain and dry it, and return the CD or grating to its sleeve. Nothing hazardous is produced.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Measure wave speed on a rope on the floor - no tension, no recoil - and observe diffraction with a CD held under a household lamp. Or work from published wave-speed tables and a supplied refraction diagram. No spring, no bright source.

**Data provenance.** Learner-timed pulse round trips over measured lengths and their own boundary observations. Wave speed is computed from the learner's own data.

### Unit 9 — Signal transmission, shielding, and an energy-conversion device build

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** small radio or phone, aluminium foil, cardboard boxes, assorted household materials, small motor or LED, battery, tape for the battery terminals, published claim article about radiation, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Building a device involves cutting and small parts. | An adult performs all cutting with sharp tools, the learner uses blunt scissors or pre-cut parts, and small parts stay away from young children. |
| physical | Aluminium foil laid across both terminals of a cell is a short circuit and a standard ignition demonstration. | Foil is used for shielding only and never touches both terminals of any cell. The shielding strand runs first and uses no electricity at all. |
| physical | Battery-powered builds can short and heat, and a stalled motor draws its full stall current. | Single 1.5 V cells only - never mains electricity, never a lithium or button cell. Tape the terminals before and after use, disconnect between tests, disconnect immediately if a motor stalls or will not turn, and stop if anything warms. |
| online | Evaluating published radiation claims means reading sources of uncertain quality. | An adult approves each source in advance. No account creation, sign-in, purchase, or personal information is required, and the learner never contacts a site owner. |

**Safe order**

1. An adult approves every source before reading begins, and does all cutting with sharp tools.
2. Run the shielding strand FIRST; it uses no electricity at all.
3. Keep aluminium foil for shielding only - it never touches both terminals of any cell.
4. For the energy-conversion build an adult connects one 1.5 V cell, never mains, never a lithium or button cell.
5. Run each test for a few seconds, disconnecting between tests, and disconnect at once if a motor stalls or anything warms.
6. Disconnect and remove the cell before the build is stored.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if any component becomes warm.
- Stop if a source requests an account, payment, or personal information.
- Never connect any build to a wall outlet.

**Disposal.** Disconnect and remove the cell and tape its terminals; used cells go to household battery recycling in a labelled container, never the general bin. Sweep up off-cuts and small parts and bin them away from young children. Recycle the foil and cardboard. Nothing is left connected or stored with a cell in it.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run the shielding test with a radio and household materials only - no build - and complete the capstone as a documented paper design with a modelled energy budget rather than a physical device.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own signal-strength observations across shielding materials and their own device performance trials. Published claims are evaluated, never restated as findings.
