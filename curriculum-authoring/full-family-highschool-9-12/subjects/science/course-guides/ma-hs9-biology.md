# Biology — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-hs9-biology`  
**Grade:** 9  
**Credit role:** Statutory named credit under MCL 380.1278b(1)(b)  
**Sessions:** 108 (36 weeks × 3 sessions)  
**Session length:** 55–75 minutes

## Description

A first high school laboratory science building directly on the Grade 8 middle-school band. The course develops molecular and cellular biology from foundations, then raises the Grade 8 heredity, natural selection, and ecosystem strands to the high school performance-expectation band. Biology is the credit Michigan names explicitly in MCL 380.1278b(1)(b), so it is placed in Grade 9 and banked first. Every investigation is household-safe, every investigation has a no-special-equipment alternative, and no activity anywhere in the course measures the learner's own body or health.

## Capstone

A biodiversity engineering defense: define criteria and constraints for a real conservation problem, model and simulate a solution, evaluate trade-offs, and defend the revision history with stated limitations.

## Anchoring phenomena

Every unit opens on an observable phenomenon rather than a definition.

| Unit | Phenomenon |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Two apple slices, one left plain and one coated in lemon juice, brown at very different rates on the same counter. |
| 2 | Two people with the same rare condition carry changes in the same gene, but one has mild symptoms and the other severe ones. |
| 3 | A cut on a finger closes over about a week, but a cut on the cornea can close in a day. |
| 4 | A house thermostat and a lizard on a rock both keep temperature within a range, but only one of them can be reprogrammed. |
| 5 | A large tree grows tonnes of wood while the soil beneath it barely loses mass. |
| 6 | A hectare of grassland supports thousands of insects, hundreds of mice, and perhaps one hawk. |
| 7 | An introduced species explodes for a decade, crashes, and then settles at a level nobody predicted. |
| 8 | Two siblings share both parents and about half their DNA, yet differ in almost every visible trait. |
| 9 | Antibiotic-resistant bacteria appear in hospitals within a few years of a new drug's release. |

## Investigations, hazards, and alternative paths

Each unit's Day 7 is an investigation. Every one has a no-special-equipment alternative that meets
the same learning target — the alternative is a first-class path, not a consolation.

### Unit 1 — Controlled test of enzymatic browning or catalase activity

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** apple or potato slices, lemon juice, cold and room-temperature water, 3% household hydrogen peroxide (optional catalase route), timer, ruler, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | 3% hydrogen peroxide irritates eyes and can bleach fabric and skin on contact. | Use the household 3% strength only, keep it in its original bottle, wear eye protection, work over a washable surface, and rinse any splash with water for 15 minutes. |
| physical | Slicing produce with a kitchen knife risks cuts. | An adult does all cutting, or the learner uses pre-cut produce; no knife work is required by the lesson. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if hydrogen peroxide contacts the eyes and begin rinsing immediately.
- Stop if any cut occurs and treat it before continuing.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run the browning comparison with no chemicals at all: compare apple slices in air, under water, and wrapped, timing colour change against a printed colour scale. This needs only produce, water, and a timer and meets the same target.

**Data provenance.** Learner-collected timing and colour-scale observations. No result is supplied in advance.

### Unit 2 — DNA extraction from fruit, and modelling sequence to protein

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** strawberries or banana, dish soap, table salt, water, coffee filter or fine strainer, cold rubbing alcohol (isopropyl), clear cup, resealable bag

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | Isopropyl alcohol is flammable and harmful if swallowed or splashed in eyes. | An adult dispenses and stores the alcohol, keeps it away from any heat or flame, and the learner wears eye protection. Never taste any material. |
| physical | Glass containers can break and cut. | Use plastic or unbreakable cups only. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if alcohol contacts the eyes and rinse for 15 minutes.
- Stop all work if any heat source or flame is present in the room.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Skip extraction entirely: use a printed codon table and a supplied DNA sequence to transcribe and translate by hand, then build the protein from paper or beads. This needs only paper and meets the sequence-to-function target.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own observation of the extracted material, plus a published codon table. No yield figure is fabricated.

### Unit 3 — Surface-area-to-volume modelling and observation of dividing cells

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** modelling clay or potato cubes, ruler, food colouring and water (diffusion route), microscope and prepared onion-root-tip slide (optional), notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Cutting cubes to size uses a knife. | An adult does all cutting, or the learner uses clay shaped by hand with no cutting tool. |
| physical | Microscope lamps get warm and slides have sharp edges. | Handle slides by their edges, let the lamp cool before storage, and treat the microscope step as optional. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if a slide chips or breaks and let an adult clear it.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Do the whole unit with no microscope and no cutting: use published, openly licensed onion-root-tip micrographs to count cells in each mitotic phase, and model surface-area-to-volume with hand-rolled clay cubes measured with a ruler.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own cube measurements and their own phase counts from either a real slide or a published micrograph.

### Unit 4 — Feedback regulation in a plant or a thermal model system

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** two similar potted plants, water, clear bags, thermometer, insulated container, warm and cool water, timer, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Warm water can scald. | Keep all water below 50 degrees Celsius, an adult fills the container, and the learner never handles boiling water. |
| emotional | Health and body topics can be distressing when they touch personal or family medical experience. | Examples use non-human systems and published cases only; the learner is never asked about their own or a family member's health, and may skip any example without penalty. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if water is hot enough to be uncomfortable to touch.
- Pause and move to the alternative if a health example becomes distressing.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use a published, openly licensed physiological dataset - for example thermoregulation in a non-human animal - and model the feedback loop from that data. Needs only the dataset and paper.

**Data provenance.** Learner-collected plant or thermal-model measurements, or a published non-human dataset. NO LEARNER BODY OR HEALTH MEASUREMENT IS REQUESTED ANYWHERE IN THIS UNIT.

### Unit 5 — Evidence of photosynthesis and respiration rates

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** fresh spinach or elodea, baking soda, water, clear cups, lamp or window light, syringe (optional leaf-disk route), yeast, sugar, warm water, balloon (respiration route), timer

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | Baking soda solution irritates eyes. | Wear eye protection and rinse any splash with water. |
| physical | A lamp close to a water container gets hot and mixes electricity with water. | Keep the lamp at least 30 cm away, never above the water, and plug it in away from any spill zone; an adult positions the lamp. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop immediately if any liquid reaches a lamp, cord, or outlet.
- Stop if a sealed container bulges; never fully seal a fermenting container.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run the yeast-and-sugar respiration comparison with a loosely fitted balloon and no lamp at all, or analyse a published photosynthesis-rate dataset against light intensity. Both meet the matter-and-energy target with kitchen materials or paper only.

**Data provenance.** Learner-collected rise times, bubble counts, or balloon circumference, plus a published rate dataset for comparison. No expected value is stated in advance.

### Unit 6 — Decomposition rates and a quantified local food web

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** fallen leaves or fruit scraps, two containers, soil, balance or kitchen scale, notebook, published local species list

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Decomposing material grows mould and attracts insects. | Keep containers outdoors or in a garage, covered and labelled; the learner wears gloves and washes hands after handling, and anyone with a mould or pollen allergy uses the alternative. |
| privacy | Recording an outdoor location can disclose where the family lives. | Record habitat type and general conditions only. Never record an address, GPS coordinate, or identifiable landmark. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop and switch to the alternative if anyone in the household has a mould or spore allergy.
- Do not open a container that smells strongly or looks heavily colonised; discard it outdoors unopened.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Skip the decomposition setup: build the energy budget and carbon flow entirely from a published, openly licensed ecosystem dataset and a species list, using arithmetic and a drawn web. Paper only.

**Data provenance.** Learner-collected mass-loss measurements where run, plus a published ecosystem energy dataset. Trophic efficiency is derived from data, never asserted.

### Unit 7 — Modelling population growth from a published long-term dataset

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** published population dataset, spreadsheet or graph paper, calculator, notebook, dried beans or counters (physical sampling model)

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| privacy | Online dataset searching can expose the learner to tracking or to accounts. | An adult supplies the dataset file or the exact source; no account creation, sign-in, or purchase is required. |
| physical | Small counters are a choking hazard around young children. | Keep counters away from younger siblings and count them back into the container at the end. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if a website asks for an account, payment, or personal information.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run a mark-and-recapture sampling model with two colours of dried beans in a bag to estimate an unknown population, then compare the estimate to the true count. Kitchen materials and arithmetic only.

**Data provenance.** A published long-term population dataset the family retrieves, plus the learner's own sampling counts. Growth constants are fitted by the learner, not supplied.

### Unit 8 — Statistical distribution of a trait in a non-human population

- **Supervision:** `none`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** dried beans, seeds, or leaves from one plant species (30+ specimens), ruler, graph paper, coins or dice for probability modelling, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| privacy | Family trait or pedigree work can expose genetic and health information about relatives who never consented. | This unit uses NON-HUMAN specimens and fictional pedigrees only. The learner is never asked to record family traits, family medical history, ancestry, or any relative's data. |
| emotional | Heredity topics can touch on disability, adoption, or family history painfully. | All examples are fictional or non-human; the learner may decline any example without penalty and no personal disclosure is ever requested. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop and use fictional data if any prompt seems to ask for real family information; it should not.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use a supplied fictional pedigree and a coin-flip meiosis model on paper to generate the trait distribution. No specimens needed.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own measurements of non-human specimens and their own coin or dice trials. Distributions are built from those trials, never pre-filled.

### Unit 9 — Selection simulation and a conservation design test

- **Supervision:** `none`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** two or three colours of dried beans or paper dots, patterned cloth or paper background, timer, graph paper, published conservation case study

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Timed grabbing games can knock over nearby objects. | Clear the work surface first and keep the play area away from edges and breakables. |
| emotional | Extinction and habitat loss can be genuinely upsetting. | Frame the unit around designed responses and documented recoveries; the learner may pause or choose a different case at any point. |

**Stop conditions**

- Pause if the extinction content becomes distressing and resume with a recovery case study.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run the selection simulation entirely on paper with dice determining survival probability by trait, then analyse a published conservation case study. No materials beyond paper and one die.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own predation-simulation counts across generations and a published conservation case study. Allele-frequency change is computed by the learner from their own trials.
