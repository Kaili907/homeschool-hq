# Biology — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-hs9-biology`  
**Grade:** 9  
**Credit role:** Statutory named credit under MCL 380.1278b(1)(b)  
**Sessions:** 108 (36 weeks × 3 sessions)  
**Session length:** 55–75 minutes

## Description

A first high school laboratory science building directly on the Grade 8 middle-school band. The course develops molecular and cellular biology from foundations, then raises the Grade 8 heredity, natural selection, and ecosystem strands to the high school performance-expectation band. Biology is the credit Michigan names explicitly in MCL 380.1278b(1)(b), so it is placed in Grade 9 and banked first. Every investigation is household-safe, every investigation has a no-special-equipment alternative, and no activity anywhere in the course measures the learner's own body or health.

## Capstone

A biodiversity engineering defense: define criteria and constraints for a real conservation problem, model and simulate a solution, evaluate trade-offs, and defend the revision history with stated limitations.

## Anchoring phenomena

Every unit opens on an observable phenomenon rather than a definition.

| Unit | Phenomenon |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Two apple slices, one left plain and one coated in lemon juice, brown at very different rates on the same counter. |
| 2 | Two people with the same rare condition carry changes in the same gene, but one has mild symptoms and the other severe ones. |
| 3 | A cut on a finger closes over about a week, but a cut on the cornea can close in a day. |
| 4 | A house thermostat and a lizard on a rock both keep temperature within a range, but only one of them can be reprogrammed. |
| 5 | A large tree grows tonnes of wood while the soil beneath it barely loses mass. |
| 6 | A hectare of grassland supports thousands of insects, hundreds of mice, and perhaps one hawk. |
| 7 | An introduced species explodes for a decade, crashes, and then settles at a level nobody predicted. |
| 8 | Two siblings share both parents and about half their DNA, yet differ in almost every visible trait. |
| 9 | Antibiotic-resistant bacteria appear in hospitals within a few years of a new drug's release. |

## Investigations, hazards, and alternative paths

Each unit's Day 7 is an investigation, and Day 9 rebuilds the performance task with the same
materials and the same safety set. Every hazard, mitigation, supervision level, safe-order step,
stop condition, and disposal step below is rendered into the learner-visible lesson flow on those
days — none of it lives only in the guardian record. Every investigation has a no-special-equipment
alternative that meets the same learning target: a first-class path, not a consolation.

### Unit 1 — Controlled test of enzymatic browning or catalase activity

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** apple or potato slices, lemon juice, cold and room-temperature water, 3% household hydrogen peroxide (optional catalase route), clear plastic cups plus a marker, one labelled for each route and never shared between the lemon-juice and peroxide routes, a washable tray to work over, timer, ruler, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | 3% hydrogen peroxide irritates eyes and can bleach fabric and skin on contact. | Use the household 3% strength only, keep it in its original bottle, wear eye protection, work over a washable surface, and rinse any splash with water for 15 minutes. |
| chemical | Hydrogen peroxide and lemon juice must never be combined; together they make peracetic acid, which is corrosive and irritating to the airway. | The lemon-juice route and the peroxide route run in separate labelled cups. They are never poured together, never poured into a shared waste cup, and never combined to see what happens. |
| chemical | Catalase and peroxide foam vigorously and release oxygen. | No lid, cap, film, or cover ever goes on a peroxide container. Use a wide open cup on a tray with plenty of headroom. |
| physical | Slicing produce with a kitchen knife risks cuts. | An adult does all cutting, or the learner uses pre-cut produce; no knife work is required by the lesson. |

**Safe order**

1. Put on eye protection and lay everything out on a washable tray before any container is opened.
2. An adult does all cutting; the learner takes only pre-cut produce.
3. Run the no-chemical browning comparison first - air, water, wrapped - and record it before any chemical appears.
4. Only then, if the catalase route runs, an adult pours 3% hydrogen peroxide into its own labelled open cup and caps the bottle.
5. Keep the lemon-juice cups and the peroxide cups apart on the tray. Never pour one into the other, and never put a lid on a peroxide cup.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if hydrogen peroxide contacts the eyes and rinse for 15 minutes.
- Stop if anyone moves to combine the peroxide and the lemon juice, or to cap a peroxide container.
- Stop if any cut occurs and treat it before continuing.

**Disposal.** Pour the peroxide and the lemon-juice cups down the drain SEPARATELY, each with the cold tap running, and never into a shared waste cup. Wash the cups, compost or bin the produce, and wash hands. Nothing is ever capped or sealed, and nothing is kept or reused.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run the browning comparison with no chemicals at all: compare apple slices in air, under water, and wrapped, timing colour change against a printed colour scale. This needs only produce, water, and a timer and meets the same target.

**Data provenance.** Learner-collected timing and colour-scale observations. No result is supplied in advance.

### Unit 2 — DNA extraction from fruit, and modelling sequence to protein

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** strawberries or banana, dish soap, table salt, water, coffee filter or fine strainer, cold rubbing alcohol (isopropyl), clear cup, a tray to work over, resealable bag

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | Isopropyl alcohol is flammable, its vapour is heavier than air and pools at bench level, and it is harmful if swallowed or splashed in eyes. | An adult sweeps the room for every ignition source before the bottle is opened, pours no more than 60 mL, and returns the bottle to a closed cupboard at once. Eye protection on; nothing is tasted. See the safe order. |
| physical | Glass containers can break and cut. | Use plastic or unbreakable cups only. |

**Safe order**

1. An adult sweeps the room before the bottle is opened: hob and oven off, heater off, no candle, pilot light, toaster, charger, or battery anywhere.
2. Put on eye protection. Mash the fruit and add the soap-and-salt water and filter it, all with no alcohol yet in the room.
3. An adult brings in the cold isopropyl, pours no more than 60 mL into the learner's cup, caps the bottle, and returns it to a closed cupboard at once.
4. Observe and spool the DNA with the cup on a tray, at least three metres from any appliance or outlet.
5. Dispose of the alcohol mixture before anything electrical is switched on at that surface again.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if alcohol contacts the eyes and rinse for 15 minutes.
- Stop all work if any flame, hob, pilot light, heater, charger, or battery is present in the room.
- Never seal the alcohol mixture in a bag, bottle, or any closed container.

**Disposal.** Pour the alcohol-and-fruit mixture down the drain with the cold tap running for 30 seconds, then wash and dry the cup. Ventilate the room for ten minutes before any appliance, charger, or battery is used at that surface. Never seal the mixture in a bag, bottle, or any closed container.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Skip extraction entirely: use a printed codon table and a supplied DNA sequence to transcribe and translate by hand, then build the protein from paper or beads. This needs only paper and meets the sequence-to-function target.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own observation of the extracted material, plus a published codon table. No yield figure is fabricated.

### Unit 3 — Surface-area-to-volume modelling and observation of dividing cells

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** modelling clay or potato cubes, ruler, food colouring and water (diffusion route), microscope and prepared onion-root-tip slide (optional), stiff card, thick paper, and tape for an adult to clear and wrap broken slide glass, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Cutting cubes to size uses a knife. | An adult does all cutting, or the learner uses clay shaped by hand with no cutting tool. |
| physical | Microscope lamps get warm and slides have sharp edges. | Handle slides by their edges, let the lamp cool before storage, and treat the microscope step as optional. |
| physical | Broken slide glass cuts whoever empties the bin. | An adult sweeps fragments with stiff card, wraps them in thick paper and tape, and bins them separately. Never by hand, never loose, never with a vacuum cleaner. |

**Safe order**

1. Clear and wipe the work surface before anything is set out.
2. An adult does every cut; the learner receives pre-cut cubes or shapes clay by hand with no cutting tool.
3. Measure and record every cube before any liquid is added.
4. If the microscope route runs, an adult mounts the slide and the learner handles it by its edges only.
5. Switch the lamp off and let it cool before anything is packed away.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if a slide chips or breaks and let an adult clear it as described.
- Stop if anyone reaches for a knife; all cutting is done by an adult.

**Disposal.** Clay goes back into its container and potato cubes into the compost or bin; pour coloured water down the drain and rinse the containers. An adult clears any broken slide by sweeping the fragments with stiff card, wrapping them in thick paper and tape, and binning them separately - never by hand, never loose, never with a vacuum cleaner.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Do the whole unit with no microscope and no cutting: use published, openly licensed onion-root-tip micrographs to count cells in each mitotic phase, and model surface-area-to-volume with hand-rolled clay cubes measured with a ruler.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own cube measurements and their own phase counts from either a real slide or a published micrograph.

### Unit 4 — Feedback regulation in a plant or a thermal model system

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** two similar potted plants, water, clear bags, thermometer, insulated container, warm and cool water, timer, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Warm water can scald. | Keep all water below 50 degrees Celsius, an adult fills the container, and the learner never handles boiling water. |
| emotional | Health and body topics can be distressing when they touch personal or family medical experience. | Examples use non-human systems and published cases only; the learner is never asked about their own or a family member's health, and may skip any example without penalty. |

**Safe order**

1. An adult mixes the warm water and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 50 degrees Celsius before the learner touches the container.
2. Set both plants, or both thermal containers, side by side before the timer starts.
3. Read every measurement from outside the container; the learner never carries an open container of warm water.
4. Stop at the scheduled end and let everything cool before it is moved.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if the thermometer reads above 50 degrees Celsius. Water temperature is judged with the thermometer, never with a hand.
- Pause and move to the alternative if a health example becomes distressing.

**Disposal.** Pour the cooled water down the drain, dry the containers, and return the plants to their usual place. No sample and no body or health measurement is taken or kept.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use a published, openly licensed physiological dataset - for example thermoregulation in a non-human animal - and model the feedback loop from that data. Needs only the dataset and paper.

**Data provenance.** Learner-collected plant or thermal-model measurements, or a published non-human dataset. NO LEARNER BODY OR HEALTH MEASUREMENT IS REQUESTED ANYWHERE IN THIS UNIT.

### Unit 5 — Evidence of photosynthesis and respiration rates

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** fresh spinach or elodea, baking soda, water, clear cups, lamp or window light, needleless blunt-tip syringe (optional leaf-disk route), thermometer, yeast, sugar, water below 40 degrees Celsius, balloon (respiration route), timer

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chemical | Baking soda solution irritates eyes. | Wear eye protection and rinse any splash with water. |
| physical | A lamp close to a water container gets hot and mixes electricity with water. | Use an LED lamp only - never incandescent or halogen, which crack a cup and can ignite paper at this distance. Keep it at least 30 cm away, never above the water, and plug it in away from any spill zone; an adult positions the lamp. |
| physical | Warm water scalds, and hot tap water kills the yeast anyway. | An adult mixes the yeast water and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 40 degrees Celsius. Temperature is judged with the thermometer, never with a hand. |
| physical | A syringe with a needle, or a blocked tip under vacuum, injures or sprays. | Use a needleless blunt-tip syringe only. No needle of any kind is used in this course. |

**Safe order**

1. An adult positions the LED lamp first, at least 30 cm from any container and never above it, with the cord away from the spill zone.
2. Fill and place the containers only after the lamp is fixed in position.
3. An adult mixes the yeast water and confirms with the thermometer that it reads below 40 degrees Celsius before yeast is added.
4. Fit the balloon loosely so gas can escape; never seal a fermenting container.
5. Switch the lamp off and let it cool before any container is moved or emptied.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop immediately if any liquid reaches a lamp, cord, or outlet.
- Stop if a sealed container bulges; never fully seal a fermenting container.

**Disposal.** Pour the baking-soda and yeast mixtures down the drain with the tap running, compost or bin the leaf material, wash the containers with soap, and wash hands. Leave any yeast container open until fizzing stops before it is binned. Nothing is sealed, kept, or stored.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run the yeast-and-sugar respiration comparison with a loosely fitted balloon and no lamp at all, or analyse a published photosynthesis-rate dataset against light intensity. Both meet the matter-and-energy target with kitchen materials or paper only.

**Data provenance.** Learner-collected rise times, bubble counts, or balloon circumference, plus a published rate dataset for comparison. No expected value is stated in advance.

### Unit 6 — Decomposition rates and a quantified local food web

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** fallen leaves or fruit scraps, two containers kept for this investigation only and never returned to food use, soil, a scale used with a disposable tray under the sample, never the sample directly, disposable trays, gloves, notebook, published local species list

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Decomposing material grows mould, and mould aerosol is far more dangerous to an asthmatic than to someone with hay fever. | Anyone with asthma, an immune condition, or a mould or spore allergy uses the alternative and opens nothing. Containers stay outdoors, labelled, covered LOOSELY so gas escapes - never sealed. Gloves on; open outdoors only. |
| physical | Mould-colonised matter on a food scale contaminates everything weighed afterwards. | Weigh in a disposable tray placed on the scale; the sample never touches the scale surface. The containers, trays, and gloves belong to this investigation only and are binned at the end. None of it returns to kitchen or food use. |
| privacy | Recording an outdoor location can disclose where the family lives. | Record habitat type and general conditions only. Never record an address, GPS coordinate, or identifiable landmark. |

**Safe order**

1. Label two containers and a set of disposable trays for this investigation only; nothing here has ever been used for food and nothing returns to food use.
2. Confirm nobody in the household has asthma, an immune condition, or a mould or spore allergy. If anyone does, run the alternative and open nothing.
3. Put on gloves before touching soil or scraps, and set the containers up outdoors or in a garage, out of reach of pets and young children.
4. Cover LOOSELY with cloth or punctured foil so gas escapes; never seal a container.
5. Weigh by placing the sample in a disposable tray on the scale - the sample never touches the scale surface.
6. Open containers outdoors only, at arm's length, with the face turned away, and wash hands thoroughly before coming indoors.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop and switch to the alternative if anyone in the household has asthma, an immune condition, or a mould or spore allergy.
- Stop if a container has been sealed rather than loosely covered; let an adult open it outdoors, away from faces.
- Do not open a container that smells strongly or looks heavily colonised; bag it unopened and bin it outdoors.

**Disposal.** Bag the containers and contents unopened and put them in the OUTDOOR waste bin. Do not compost indoors and do not empty anything into a food-waste caddy. The container is never sealed while it is decomposing - it is bagged only at the very end, for the outdoor bin. The containers, trays, and gloves are binned with them and never return to kitchen or food use.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Skip the decomposition setup: build the energy budget and carbon flow entirely from a published, openly licensed ecosystem dataset and a species list, using arithmetic and a drawn web. Paper only.

**Data provenance.** Learner-collected mass-loss measurements where run, plus a published ecosystem energy dataset. Trophic efficiency is derived from data, never asserted.

### Unit 7 — Modelling population growth from a published long-term dataset

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** published population dataset, spreadsheet or graph paper, calculator, notebook, dried beans or counters (physical sampling model), a shallow tray to count the beans in, a labelled container the beans are counted back into and stored in, out of reach of young children

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| privacy | Online dataset searching can expose the learner to tracking or to accounts. | An adult supplies the dataset file or the exact source; no account creation, sign-in, or purchase is required. |
| physical | Small counters are a choking hazard around young children, and dried beans used as counters are no longer food - raw kidney beans in particular are toxic if eaten. | Keep counters away from younger siblings, count them back into a labelled container at the end, and never return beans used as counters to food storage or eat them. |

**Safe order**

1. An adult supplies the dataset file or the exact source before any browsing begins.
2. Count the beans into their container in a shallow tray, with young children out of the room.
3. Run each sampling trial and record it before mixing again.
4. Count every bean back into the container and confirm the total before leaving the work area.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if a website asks for an account, payment, or personal information.

**Disposal.** Beans and counters go back into a labelled container stored out of reach of young children. Beans used as counters are never eaten and never returned to food storage. Dataset files stay on the adult's device; nothing is uploaded.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run a mark-and-recapture sampling model with two colours of dried beans in a bag to estimate an unknown population, then compare the estimate to the true count. Kitchen materials and arithmetic only.

**Data provenance.** A published long-term population dataset the family retrieves, plus the learner's own sampling counts. Growth constants are fitted by the learner, not supplied.

### Unit 8 — Statistical distribution of a trait in a non-human population

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** dried beans, seeds, or leaves from one plant species (30+ specimens), ruler, graph paper, coins or dice for probability modelling, gloves, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Collecting leaves without identifying the plant risks irritant sap, poison ivy, oak or sumac, and pesticide residue. | Collect only from a plant an adult has identified as safe. Never from a plant with milky sap, never poison ivy, oak, or sumac, and never from a plant or verge that has been treated with pesticide. Wear gloves and wash hands afterwards. |
| physical | Small counters and dried beans are a choking hazard around young children and are no longer food. | Keep counters away from younger siblings, count them back into a labelled container, and never return them to food storage or eat them. |
| privacy | Family trait or pedigree work can expose genetic and health information about relatives who never consented. | This unit uses NON-HUMAN specimens and fictional pedigrees only. The learner is never asked to record family traits, family medical history, ancestry, or any relative's data. |
| emotional | Heredity topics can touch on disability, adoption, or family history painfully. | All examples are fictional or non-human; the learner may decline any example without penalty and no personal disclosure is ever requested. |

**Safe order**

1. An adult identifies every plant before a single leaf is taken; nothing is collected from a plant with milky sap, from poison ivy, oak, or sumac, or from anywhere treated with pesticide.
2. Wear gloves to collect, and wash hands before handling anything else.
3. Measure and record each of the 30 or more specimens before any grouping or graphing.
4. Run the coin or dice probability trials separately, recording every trial including the ones that look wrong.
5. No family trait, photograph, pedigree, or medical detail is collected at any point.

**Stop conditions**

- Stop and use fictional data if any prompt seems to ask for real family information; it should not.
- Stop if a plant cannot be identified by an adult as safe to handle, and use the supplied specimens instead.

**Disposal.** Return leaves and seeds to the garden or compost, put beans back in their labelled container out of reach of young children, and wash hands. Beans used as counters are never eaten. No specimen and no family information is stored.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use a supplied fictional pedigree and a coin-flip meiosis model on paper to generate the trait distribution. No specimens needed.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own measurements of non-human specimens and their own coin or dice trials. Distributions are built from those trials, never pre-filled.

### Unit 9 — Selection simulation and a conservation design test

- **Supervision:** `none`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** two or three colours of dried beans or paper dots, patterned cloth or paper background, timer, graph paper, published conservation case study

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Timed grabbing games can knock over nearby objects. | Clear the work surface first and keep the play area away from edges and breakables. |
| emotional | Extinction and habitat loss can be genuinely upsetting. | Frame the unit around designed responses and documented recoveries; the learner may pause or choose a different case at any point. |

**Safe order**

1. Clear the whole work surface and move screens and breakables out of reach before any timed round.
2. Agree the rules and the timer length, then run one practice round untimed.
3. Run each timed generation and record the counts immediately, before the next round starts.
4. Count every counter back into its container at the end.

**Stop conditions**

- Pause if the extinction content becomes distressing and resume with a recovery case study.

**Disposal.** Beans, paper dots, and cloth go back into a labelled container out of reach of young children; beans used as counters are never eaten. Case-study printouts are filed or recycled. No location is recorded and nothing is uploaded.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run the selection simulation entirely on paper with dice determining survival probability by trait, then analyse a published conservation case study. No materials beyond paper and one die.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own predation-simulation counts across generations and a published conservation case study. Allele-frequency change is computed by the learner from their own trials.
