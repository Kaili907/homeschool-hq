# Earth, Space, and Environmental Systems — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-hs12-earth-space-environmental`  
**Grade:** 12  
**Credit role:** Encouraged fourth credit; statute names Earth science, astronomy, environmental science, and geology  
**Sessions:** 108 (36 weeks × 3 sessions)  
**Session length:** 55–75 minutes

## Description

An integrated synthesis course covering the full HS-ESS1, HS-ESS2, and HS-ESS3 bands - the 19 performance expectations most often left uncovered by a three-year disciplinary sequence. Placed last because it genuinely depends on the other three: stellar nucleosynthesis needs Chemistry's atomic model, the carbon cycle needs Biology's photosynthesis and respiration, and climate modelling needs all three plus quantitative reasoning. The course is data-intensive and low-hazard; most investigations use published datasets the family retrieves, and the sun is never observed directly by any means.

## Capstone

The Grade 12 capstone: analyse a global challenge, build a computational simulation of coupled Earth and human systems, evaluate competing solutions on prioritised criteria and trade-offs, refine, and defend with stated limitations.

## Anchoring phenomena

Every unit opens on an observable phenomenon rather than a definition.

| Unit | Phenomenon |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Two published graphs of the same climate record look like they tell opposite stories, purely because of their axes. |
| 2 | The sun has been radiating enormous power for billions of years without burning out or collapsing. |
| 3 | Nearly every distant galaxy's light is shifted toward red, and the farther away it is, the more shifted it is. |
| 4 | A comet spends decades far from the sun and then whips through the inner solar system in weeks. |
| 5 | Identical fossil species appear on coastlines now separated by an entire ocean. |
| 6 | Removing vegetation from a hillside changes the temperature, the water table, and the air quality of a valley kilometres downstream. |
| 7 | Two cities at the same latitude have average winter temperatures nearly twenty degrees apart. |
| 8 | Earth's atmosphere contains abundant oxygen, which is chemically reactive and should not persist without something continuously producing it. |
| 9 | Two counties facing the same water shortage adopt opposite policies, and both can cite evidence. |

## Investigations, hazards, and alternative paths

Each unit's Day 7 is an investigation. Every one has a no-special-equipment alternative that meets
the same learning target — the alternative is a first-class path, not a consolation.

### Unit 1 — Scale modelling and critical analysis of published representations

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** published geoscience dataset, graph paper or spreadsheet, measuring tape, string, calculator, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| online | Locating datasets online can expose the learner to tracking or account prompts. | An adult supplies the dataset file or exact source. No account creation, sign-in, or purchase is required. |
| physical | Building a large scale model outdoors involves walking measured distances. | Stay on the property or in a park away from roads and water, with an adult aware of the route. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if a site requests an account, payment, or personal information.
- Stop outdoor work near any road, water body, or unstable ground.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Do the scale model on paper at desk scale rather than walking it outdoors, and analyse a printed dataset supplied by the adult. Paper only.

**Data provenance.** Published datasets the family retrieves plus the learner's own graphs and scale calculations.

### Unit 2 — Solar energy measurement and spectral element identification

- **Supervision:** `direct-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** thermometer, dark container of water, timer, measuring cup, CD or diffraction grating, published stellar spectra, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Looking at the sun causes permanent eye damage. | NEVER look at the sun directly, through any lens, grating, CD, camera, or filter. All solar work is done by measuring warming water in shade-versus-sun, with the learner's back to the sun. |
| physical | Working outdoors in sun risks heat and sunburn. | Limit exposure, wear a hat and sunscreen, keep drinking water available, and work in the cooler part of the day. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop instantly if anyone looks toward the sun through any optical device.
- Stop and move indoors at any sign of overheating.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Skip all outdoor solar work: compute the sun's power output and lifetime from published solar-constant and mass data, and identify elements from published stellar spectra images. Paper and calculator only. This is the DEFAULT path.

**Data provenance.** Published solar constant, solar mass, and stellar spectra, plus the learner's own warming measurements if the outdoor route is used. Fusion rates are computed by the learner.

### Unit 3 — Expansion modelling and analysis of published redshift data

- **Supervision:** `none`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** balloon and marker, or elastic band and ruler, published galaxy redshift and distance dataset, graph paper or spreadsheet, calculator, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Balloons pop and are a choking hazard for young children. | Keep fragments away from young children; anyone with a latex allergy uses the elastic-band model. |
| emotional | Cosmological scale and deep time can be unsettling for some learners. | Pace the content, keep the focus on evidence and reasoning, and allow the learner to pause at any point. |

**Stop conditions**

- Switch to the elastic-band model if anyone present has a latex allergy.
- Pause if the scale content becomes distressing.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Model expansion with dots drawn on an elastic band stretched along a ruler, then fit published redshift-distance data by hand. No balloon, paper and ruler only.

**Data provenance.** Published galaxy redshift and distance measurements the family retrieves. The learner fits the relationship themselves; no expansion rate is supplied.

### Unit 4 — Orbital prediction from published solar system data

- **Supervision:** `none`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** published orbital dataset for planets or moons, spreadsheet or graph paper, calculator, string, pins, and cardboard for ellipse construction, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Pins used to draw ellipses can prick or be stepped on. | Push pins into a cardboard backing on a table, count them out and back in, and keep them away from young children and off the floor. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop and account for every pin before leaving the work area.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Construct ellipses with a compass and ruler rather than pins and string, and complete all orbital prediction arithmetically from the published dataset. Paper only.

**Data provenance.** Published planetary and satellite orbital periods and radii. The learner derives and tests the period-radius relationship from that data.

### Unit 5 — Seafloor age mapping and a convection model

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** published seafloor age and crustal age datasets, coloured pencils, graph paper, clear heatproof container, warm water, cool water, food colouring (convection route), notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Warm water used for the convection model can scald, and glass can crack from thermal shock. | Keep water below 50 degrees Celsius, use a plastic or heatproof container, and let an adult pour. |
| physical | Food colouring stains surfaces and clothing. | Work over a tray with old clothing or an apron. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if water is uncomfortable to touch.
- Stop if a container shows any crack.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Do the convection strand from a published thermal-convection diagram sequence and complete the seafloor age mapping with coloured pencils on a printed map. No water, paper only.

**Data provenance.** Published seafloor age, radiometric age, and fossil distribution data. Spreading rates are calculated by the learner from the map.

### Unit 6 — Erosion, infiltration, and water property testing

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** soil, sand, and gravel, trays or baking pans, water, measuring cup, timer, ice, thermometer, published watershed dataset, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | Soil and outdoor materials carry bacteria and sharp fragments. | Wear gloves, keep hands away from the face, wash thoroughly afterwards, and never use soil from an area treated with pesticide or near a roadway. |
| physical | Water and soil together make surfaces slippery. | Work outdoors or over a large tray, and clean spills immediately. |
| privacy | Photographing or logging a field site can reveal where the family lives. | Record substrate type, slope, and conditions only. No address, GPS coordinate, or identifiable landmark is recorded, and no photograph is required. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if soil source is unknown or possibly contaminated.
- Stop outdoor work near any road, steep bank, or water body.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Compare infiltration through sand, gravel, and packed soil in three cups on a tray indoors, or work entirely from a published watershed dataset. Kitchen materials or paper.

**Data provenance.** Learner's own infiltration times, sediment transport observations, and temperature readings, plus a published watershed dataset for comparison.

### Unit 7 — Energy balance modelling and carbon cycle quantification

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** two identical containers, thermometer, clear covering, lamp or sunlight, published climate and carbon flux datasets, spreadsheet or graph paper, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical | A lamp used as a light source gets hot and must stay away from water. | Keep the lamp at least 30 cm from containers, never above water, and let an adult position it. Allow it to cool before moving. |
| emotional | Climate content can produce genuine anxiety about the future. | Pair every impact with documented response options and human agency, keep the framing on evidence and design, and let the learner pause or redirect at any time. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if any liquid approaches the lamp or its cord.
- Pause and move to the response-and-design strand if the content becomes distressing.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Run the energy-balance comparison in sunlight rather than under a lamp, or skip the physical model entirely and build the energy budget and carbon-cycle quantification from published flux data. Paper and calculator only.

**Data provenance.** Published climate records, carbon flux estimates, and model outputs the family retrieves, plus the learner's own container temperature series. Forecasts are the learner's own, with stated uncertainty.

### Unit 8 — Atmospheric change evidence and a regional resource and hazard analysis

- **Supervision:** `none`
- **Guardian visibility:** `summary`
- **Materials:** published atmospheric composition and banded-iron-formation data, published hazard and resource maps, graph paper, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| privacy | Mapping local hazards and resources can disclose where the family lives. | Analyse a region at county scale or larger, or a region the family does not live in. Never record a home address or precise location. |
| emotional | Natural hazard content can be frightening, especially after a local event. | Keep the framing on preparation, mitigation, and documented responses; the learner may choose a different region at any point. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if the analysis begins to require the family's precise location.
- Switch regions if hazard content becomes distressing.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Use a supplied printed regional case study rather than searching sources, and complete the argument on paper. No online work required.

**Data provenance.** Published atmospheric, geologic, resource, and hazard data. The argument and its evidence weighting are the learner's own.

### Unit 9 — Computational simulation of a coupled resource and sustainability system

- **Supervision:** `nearby-adult`
- **Guardian visibility:** `confirmation-required`
- **Materials:** spreadsheet or simple programming environment, published resource, energy, and population datasets, graph paper, calculator, notebook

**Hazards**

| Kind | Hazard | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| online | Building a simulation may involve downloading tools or datasets. | An adult approves and installs any software and supplies every dataset. No account creation, sign-in, purchase, or personal information is required. |
| emotional | Sustainability and population content can provoke anxiety or touch on contested politics. | Keep the work on evidence, explicit criteria, and transparent trade-offs; represent competing positions fairly and let the learner choose the challenge they analyse. |

**Stop conditions**

- Stop if any tool or site requests an account, payment, or personal information.
- Pause and reselect a challenge if the content becomes distressing.

**No-special-equipment alternative.** Build the simulation as an iterated spreadsheet or as a hand-computed table over time steps on paper. No programming environment and no downloads required.

**Data provenance.** Published resource, energy, and population datasets. All simulation outputs are generated by the learner's own model and reported as model output, never as observed fact.
