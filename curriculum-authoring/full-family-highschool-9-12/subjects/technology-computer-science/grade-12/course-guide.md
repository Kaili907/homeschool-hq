# Grade 12 Technology and Computer Science — Course Guide

**Course ID:** `ma-g12-technology`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Instructional sessions:** 48  
**Typical session:** 45–75 minutes (60–90 in capstone units)  
**Cadence:** weekly, with a double block during the capstone units

## Course description

A Grade 12 computer-science course completing the 9-12 progression through advanced algorithms, systems and concurrency, privacy and security engineering, computing ethics and policy, and a substantial defended capstone carried over twenty-four sustained sessions.

## Course outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will:

1. Demonstrate the listed Michigan-aligned knowledge and practices through independent, accessible evidence.
2. Explain reasoning, cite evidence, document process, or justify design choices rather than supply unsupported answers.
3. Transfer learning to an unfamiliar task, text, situation, dataset, performance, or project.
4. Use feedback and error evidence to revise without shame or character judgments.
5. Complete the course capstone: **A substantial Grade 12 computing capstone, built across twenty-four sessions, with a validated problem, an architecture record, automated tests, accessibility conformance, a security and privacy engineering review, an ethics and impact analysis, and a defense in a private-capable format.**

## Instructional model

Six-session units run: Launch and diagnostic; Explicit model; Guided practice; Application or project; Mastery check; Correction and reflection. Twelve-session units run: Launch and diagnostic; Concept model A; Guided practice A; Independent application A; Concept model B; Guided practice B; Investigation or close reading; Reteach and varied practice; Performance task build; Synthesis and review; Unit assessment; Correction and reflection.

Every topic in a unit receives at least one instructional lesson before any
assessment or correction lesson. Mastery-check, assessment, synthesis, and
correction lessons are scoped to the whole unit rather than to a single topic.
Lessons are resumable by segment, media is optional, and a readable fallback is
always specified.

## Mastery and grading

- One correct answer never establishes mastery.
- Unit assessments combine concept, representation or source use, application, error or claim analysis, connection, performance evidence, and reflection. Capstone units add a defense-and-response-to-critique prompt.
- Suggested reporting: **Secure**, **Developing**, or **Not Yet**, supported by evidence rather than a single percentage.
- Reassessment uses fresh items or a new application after targeted instruction.
- Approved breaks, accommodations, voice/no-voice choices, and alternate response modes are not failures.
- Parent summaries show target, completion, evidence type, and next step; they exclude raw private reflections, diagnosis language, and unnecessary personal data.

## Scope and sequence

| Unit | Title | Days | Standards anchors | Performance task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Advanced Algorithms and Computational Limits | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Algorithms and Programming | Solve a constrained optimization or graph problem and defend the algorithm choice with complexity reasoning. |
| 2 | Systems, Concurrency, and Performance | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Computing Systems; Algorithms and Programming | Profile and optimize a supplied program, documenting measurements, tradeoffs, and correctness under concurrency. |
| 3 | Applied Cybersecurity, Privacy Engineering, and Risk | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Networks and the Internet — Cybersecurity; Impacts of Computing | Produce a security and privacy engineering review for a fictional system with a risk register and an incident-response plan; all work stays in a sandbox and no live system is tested. |
| 4 | Computing Ethics, Law, Policy, and Societal Impact | 6 | Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing | Write an evidence-based policy analysis of a computing practice with stakeholder, legal, and equity findings. |
| 5 | Capstone Design, Build, and Test | 12 | Michigan Computer Science: Algorithms and Programming; Impacts of Computing | Build the capstone system to a testable milestone with verified requirements, automated tests, and accessibility evidence. |
| 6 | Capstone Review, Defense, and Transition | 12 | Michigan Computer Science: Impacts of Computing; Algorithms and Programming | Defend the capstone in a chosen private or public mode with complete documentation, a response to independent review, and a maintenance and transition plan. |

## Capstone

A substantial Grade 12 computing capstone, built across twenty-four sessions, with a validated problem, an architecture record, automated tests, accessibility conformance, a security and privacy engineering review, an ethics and impact analysis, and a defense in a private-capable format.

The capstone may be presented publicly, privately, in writing, orally, through a demonstration, or in another accessible format that preserves the learning targets.
