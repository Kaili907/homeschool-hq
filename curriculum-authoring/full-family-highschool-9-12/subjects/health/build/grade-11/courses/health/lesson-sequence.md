# Grade 11 Health — Lesson Sequence

36 instructional days.

| Day | Unit | Day in unit | Phase | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 2 | 1 | 2 | Explicit model | reading a study summary without over-reading it |
| 3 | 1 | 3 | Guided practice | regulated versus unregulated products and what a label legally means |
| 4 | 1 | 4 | Application or project | evaluating a health service: credential, scope, cost, and accountability |
| 5 | 1 | 5 | Mastery check | conflict of interest and funding disclosure |
| 6 | 1 | 6 | Correction and reflection | the cost of being wrong: how to size a risk before acting |
| 7 | 2 | 1 | Launch and diagnostic | how chronic conditions develop and why single causes are rare |
| 8 | 2 | 2 | Explicit model | modifiable and non-modifiable factors, stated without moralizing |
| 9 | 2 | 3 | Guided practice | genetics, environment, access, and stress as real contributors |
| 10 | 2 | 4 | Application or project | sleep, movement, and recovery as long-horizon inputs |
| 11 | 2 | 5 | Mastery check | screening, early detection, and routine care over a lifetime |
| 12 | 2 | 6 | Correction and reflection | living well with an ongoing condition, and why stigma harms outcomes |
| 13 | 3 | 1 | Launch and diagnostic | air, water, housing, and neighborhood conditions as health inputs |
| 14 | 3 | 2 | Explicit model | food access, transportation, and distance to care |
| 15 | 3 | 3 | Guided practice | environmental hazards and how communities learn about them |
| 16 | 3 | 4 | Application or project | public health functions and how a community responds to an outbreak |
| 17 | 3 | 5 | Mastery check | reading community-level health data honestly |
| 18 | 3 | 6 | Correction and reflection | who has standing to change a community health condition |

| 25 | 5 | 1 | Launch and diagnostic | individual risk versus population-level risk |
| 26 | 5 | 2 | Explicit model | prevention approaches with evidence behind them |
| 27 | 5 | 3 | Guided practice | harm-reduction concepts and the arguments made for and against them |
| 28 | 5 | 4 | Application or project | policy levers: access, pricing, marketing limits, and enforcement |
| 29 | 5 | 5 | Mastery check | stigma as a barrier to treatment-seeking |
| 30 | 5 | 6 | Correction and reflection | evaluating a prevention campaign for accuracy and effect |
| 31 | 6 | 1 | Launch and diagnostic | choosing a scoped, achievable health promotion goal |
| 32 | 6 | 2 | Explicit model | audience analysis and message framing without fear appeals |
| 33 | 6 | 3 | Guided practice | accuracy, citation, and avoiding overclaim in advocacy |
| 34 | 6 | 4 | Application or project | partners, permissions, and realistic constraints |
| 35 | 6 | 5 | Mastery check | defining a measurable outcome before starting |
| 36 | 6 | 6 | Correction and reflection | presenting a proposal and taking critique |
