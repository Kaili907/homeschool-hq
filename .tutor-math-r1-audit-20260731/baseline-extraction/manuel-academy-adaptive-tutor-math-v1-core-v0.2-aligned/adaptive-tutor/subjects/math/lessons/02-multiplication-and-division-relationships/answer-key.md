# Multiplication and Division Relationships — Answer Key With Reasoning

> Adult/instructor material. The learner-facing player should reveal these only according to each item’s answer policy.

# Diagnostic answer key

## math-md-d01 — Which picture represents 4 groups of 6?
**Answer:** A. An array with 4 rows of 6
**Reasoning:** Four groups with six in each group can be shown as four rows of six.

## math-md-d02 — Use 7 × 8 = 56. Which division equation is in the same fact family?
**Answer:** C. Both A and B
**Reasoning:** Multiplication and division use the same three numbers in inverse equations.

## math-md-d03 — What does 24 ÷ 6 ask in a “groups of 6” interpretation?
**Answer:** B. Find how many groups of 6 fit in 24
**Reasoning:** Division can ask how many equal groups of the divisor fit in the dividend.

## math-md-d04 — Which partial products correctly decompose 23 × 6?
**Answer:** A. 20 × 6 and 3 × 6
**Reasoning:** Split 23 into 20 + 3, multiply each part by 6, then combine.

## math-md-d05 — A teacher has 156 markers and puts 12 in each bin. Which equation can check the number of full bins?
**Answer:** B. 12 × ? = 156
**Reasoning:** The unknown number of bins times 12 markers per bin must equal 156.

## math-md-d06 — Thirty students ride in vans that hold 8 students each. Explain what the remainder means and how many vans are needed.
**Acceptable evidence:**
- Computes 30 ÷ 8 = 3 remainder 6.
- Explains 3 vans are full and 6 students still need a van.
- Concludes 4 vans are needed.
**Reasoning:** The remainder represents students who still need transportation, so the context requires one additional van.

# Guided-practice answer key

## math-md-g01 — Which equation represents 9 equal groups of 7?
**Answer:** D. Both B and C describe the same relationship
**Reasoning:** 9 × 7 = 63 and 63 ÷ 9 = 7 are related equations.

## math-md-g02 — What is 18 × 7 using (10 × 7) + (8 × 7)?
**Answer:** A. 126
**Reasoning:** The partial products are 10 × 7 = 70 and 8 × 7 = 56; combining them gives 126.

## math-md-g03 — What is 144 ÷ 12?
**Answer:** C. 12
**Reasoning:** Because 12 × 12 = 144.

## math-md-g04 — Draw or describe an array for 16 × 5 and split it into two easier rectangles.
**Acceptable evidence:**
- Examples: 10 × 5 and 6 × 5; 8 × 5 and 8 × 5.
- Partial products total 80.
- Connects both rectangles to the full array.
**Reasoning:** A valid decomposition preserves all 16 groups and combines partial products to 80.

## math-md-g05 — Fifty-two stickers are shared equally among 6 students. Which statement best describes the result?
**Answer:** A. Each gets 8 and 4 are left
**Reasoning:** 6 × 8 = 48, leaving 4 stickers. The context may decide what to do with leftovers.

## math-md-g06 — Explain how 13 × 9 can help solve 117 ÷ 9.
**Acceptable evidence:**
- States 13 × 9 = 117.
- Uses inverse relationship to conclude the missing factor is 13.
- May write the fact family.
**Reasoning:** Multiplication checks division: if 13 groups of 9 make 117, then 117 divided into groups of 9 makes 13 groups.

# Independent-mastery answer key

## math-md-m01 — What is 26 × 8 using an area model?
**Answer:** C. 208
**Reasoning:** 20 × 8 = 160 and 6 × 8 = 48; 160 + 48 = 208.

## math-md-m02 — What is 216 ÷ 18?
**Answer:** C. 12
**Reasoning:** Because 18 × 12 = 216, division by 18 returns the missing factor 12.

## math-md-m03 — Write all four equations in the fact family for 9, 14, and 126.
**Acceptable evidence:**
- 9 × 14 = 126
- 14 × 9 = 126
- 126 ÷ 9 = 14
- 126 ÷ 14 = 9
**Reasoning:** The two multiplication and two division equations use the same three numbers.

## math-md-m04 — A bakery packs 95 rolls in trays that hold 12. How many trays are needed to hold every roll?
**Answer:** C. 8
**Reasoning:** Seven trays hold 84 rolls and 11 remain, so an eighth tray is needed.

## math-md-m05 — Compare 5 groups of 12 and 12 groups of 5. What stays the same, and what changes in a story?
**Acceptable evidence:**
- Both products are 60.
- Number of groups and size of each group switch roles.
- Units/context may make the two stories different.
**Reasoning:** The commutative property preserves the product, but the contextual meaning of each factor can differ.

## math-md-m06 — Solve 17 × 6 with a decomposition, then use your result to write a related division equation.
**Acceptable evidence:**
- Decomposes 17 as 10 + 7 or another valid split.
- Gets 102.
- Writes 102 ÷ 6 = 17 or 102 ÷ 17 = 6.
**Reasoning:** Partial products combine to 102, and division reverses the multiplication relationship.
