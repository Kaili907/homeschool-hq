# Multistep Word-Problem Reasoning

**Sequence ID:** `math-seq-multistep-word-problems-v1`  
**Lesson ID:** `math-lesson-04-multistep-word-problem-reasoning`  
**Version:** `1.0.0`  
**Grade band:** 4–6  
**Summary:** Use organizers, units, hidden questions, step diagrams, and checks to reason through multistep problems.

## Instructional commitments

- Do not reveal a final answer before the learner attempts the current step and explains reasoning.
- Present exactly one step or one question at a time.
- Ask the learner to explain how they know before advancing.
- Treat incorrect answers as evidence for routing, not as a character judgment.
- Use warm, specific, non-shaming language. Never label a learner as bad at math.
- Do not diagnose dyscalculia or any medical, developmental, or learning condition.
- Do not complete graded homework. Offer concept instruction or a similar ungraded example instead.
- Never claim mastery from one correct response; require repeated evidence across representations.
- Never require a camera, microphone, or identifiable child photo.

## Prerequisites

- Interpret the four operations in one-step situations.
- Multiply and divide whole numbers at the learner’s instructional level.
- Add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers.
- Read and label common measurement and money units.

## Objectives

- Identify the final question, relevant data, and answer unit.
- Name hidden intermediate questions and order dependent steps.
- Represent relationships with diagrams, equations, and organizers.
- Check reasonableness and explain how the result answers the story.

## Diagnostic assessment

Administer one item at a time. Ask the learner to explain the choice before showing correctness. Record the response as evidence and route only after enough evidence is available.

### 1. A school store opens 8 boxes with 6 notebooks in each box. It sells 19 notebooks. Which plan finds how many remain?
- A. 8 + 6 − 19
- B. 8 × 6 − 19
- C. 19 − 8 × 6
- D. 8 × (6 − 19)

### 2. A class has 24 students. Tickets cost $12 per student, and the bus fee is $85. Which expression gives the total trip cost?
- A. 24 + 12 + 85
- B. 24 × 12 + 85
- C. 24 × (12 + 85)
- D. 85 − 24 × 12

### 3. A problem says a library has 7 shelves with 24 books each, 38 books are checked out, and the library opened in 1998. Which number is not needed to find books currently on shelves?
- A. 7
- B. 24
- C. 38
- D. 1998

### 4. After calculating 9 boxes × 12 shirts = 108 shirts, what should you ask next if 47 shirts were sold?
- A. Is 108 even?
- B. How many shirts remain after 47 are sold?
- C. What is 9 + 12?
- D. Can I stop because I used multiplication?

### 5. Thirty-five tickets cost $8 each, plus a $45 room fee. A group pays $350. Which order is correct for finding change?
- A. 350 − 45, then × 8
- B. 35 × 8, then + 45, then subtract from 350
- C. 35 + 8 + 45, then subtract from 350
- D. 350 ÷ 35, then + 45

### 6. A garden has 6 rows of 14 plants. Twenty-nine plants are given away. Explain how you would solve and check how many remain without calculating the final answer yet.
- Response: explain with words, an equation, or the named representation.


## Likely misconception patterns and distinguishing evidence

### Chooses operations from one keyword
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-wp-keyword-only`
**Likely evidence:**
- Sees “left” and always subtracts, even when the structure differs.
- Sees “each” and multiplies without identifying known and unknown quantities.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Remove the keyword and ask for a diagram.
- Use two stories with the same keyword but different structures.
**First response:** A keyword can be a clue, but the quantities and their relationships decide the operation.
**Route:** `reteach-wp-relationships`

### Uses every number whether relevant or not
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-wp-use-all-numbers`
**Likely evidence:**
- Includes a date, room number, or unused count in a calculation.
- Cannot explain what a number measures.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Ask for units beside every number.
- Ask which quantities connect to the question.
**First response:** Not every number must be used. Let’s label what each number measures and connect only the needed quantities.
**Route:** `reteach-wp-relevant-data`

### Stops after an intermediate result
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-wp-one-step-stop`
**Likely evidence:**
- Finds total made but not how many remain.
- Answers the first hidden question rather than the stated question.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Ask “What does this number answer?”
- Underline the final question and compare units.
**First response:** That number answers an important first step. Now let’s return to the question and see what is still missing.
**Route:** `reteach-wp-answer-target`

### Uses correct operations in the wrong order
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-wp-wrong-order`
**Likely evidence:**
- Subtracts before finding the total produced.
- Treats a fee as part of a per-person amount.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Build a step diagram with arrows.
- Ask which quantity must exist before the next calculation can happen.
**First response:** You selected useful operations. Let’s put them in dependency order so each step creates what the next step needs.
**Route:** `reteach-wp-step-order`

### Combines quantities with incompatible units
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-wp-unit-mismatch`
**Likely evidence:**
- Adds students to dollars or boxes to individual items.
- Writes a numeric answer without the requested unit.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Label every quantity and operation with units.
- Ask what one product or quotient represents.
**First response:** The numbers need matching meanings before they can be combined. Let’s attach a unit to every quantity.
**Route:** `reteach-wp-units`

### Accepts a computation without checking reasonableness or the question
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-wp-no-check`
**Likely evidence:**
- Reports more items remaining than were made.
- Does not notice negative or impossible context results.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Estimate before exact calculation.
- Substitute the result back into the story.
**First response:** The calculation is not the last step. Let’s check the size, unit, and whether it answers the actual question.
**Route:** `reteach-wp-check`

## Visual explanation plan

Representations: word-problem-organizer, step-diagram, array, number-line.

- Organizer fields use plain language and can be read aloud.
- Units appear in text, not only color.
- Problems remain solvable with pencil and paper only.

## Spoken explanation

**Voice direction:** warm, strategic, unhurried, nonjudgmental

- `wp-n01` — A multistep word problem is a relationship puzzle, not a keyword hunt.
- `wp-n02` — Begin by stating exactly what the question asks and the unit the answer must use.
- `wp-n03` — Label every number with its meaning, then mark any information that is not connected to the question.
- `wp-n04` — Use a drawing, array, table, or organizer to show how quantities are related.
- `wp-n05` — Name the hidden first question. Its answer becomes information for the next step.
- `wp-n06` — Put operations in dependency order and keep units attached to the calculations.
- `wp-n07` — Pause after each step and ask what that result means in the story.
- `wp-n08` — Estimate before or after exact work to check the size of the result.
- `wp-n09` — Use an inverse operation or substitute the result back into the story when possible.
- `wp-n10` — The solution is complete only when the final number, unit, and explanation answer the original question.

## Show Me mode

**Purpose:** Watch a story become a two-step diagram without revealing the final result at the start.
**Practice example:** `7 shelves × 24 books, then 38 checked out`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** What is the final question asking us to count?
**Evidence sought:** Books currently on shelves.
**Hint if needed:** Underline the requested unit.
**Visual command:** `wp-board-01`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** What hidden quantity must be found before subtracting checked-out books?
**Evidence sought:** The total books before checkout.
**Hint if needed:** Which two quantities describe equal groups?
**Visual command:** `wp-board-03`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** After both steps, how can you check the result?
**Evidence sought:** Add checked-out books back to the remaining amount or estimate.
**Hint if needed:** Reverse the last operation.
**Visual command:** `wp-board-02`

## Talk Me Through It mode

**Purpose:** The learner narrates a plan before computing.
**Practice example:** `9 boxes of 12 shirts; 47 sold`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** Tell me what each number measures.
**Evidence sought:** 9 boxes, 12 shirts per box, 47 shirts sold.
**Hint if needed:** Attach a unit to every number.
**Visual command:** `wp-board-01`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** Which quantity must be created first?
**Evidence sought:** Starting total shirts from 9 × 12.
**Hint if needed:** The amount sold must be removed from what total?
**Visual command:** `wp-board-02`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** What check will show your final result answers the story?
**Evidence sought:** Remaining + sold = starting total, with shirts as the unit.
**Hint if needed:** Reverse the subtraction and read the question again.
**Visual command:** `wp-board-02`

## Let’s Do One Together mode

**Purpose:** Alternate organizer fields and calculations.
**Practice example:** `35 tickets at $8 plus $45 fee; paid $350`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** I identified the final unit as dollars of change. You write the first hidden question.
**Evidence sought:** What is the cost of 35 tickets?
**Hint if needed:** Use tickets × dollars per ticket.
**Visual command:** `wp-board-04`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** I will add the room fee after the ticket total. You explain why it is added only once.
**Evidence sought:** It is a one-time fee, not a per-ticket fee.
**Hint if needed:** Compare the units and wording.
**Visual command:** `wp-board-04`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** You find the change and estimate to check it. What should the estimate be near?
**Evidence sought:** About $25 to $30.
**Hint if needed:** Round the total cost near $325.
**Visual command:** `wp-board-05`

## Different Example mode

**Purpose:** Transfer the organizer to a non-money context.
**Practice example:** `6 rows of 14 plants; 29 given away`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** Which model represents the starting number of plants?
**Evidence sought:** An array with 6 rows of 14.
**Hint if needed:** Identify equal groups.
**Visual command:** `wp-board-03`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** Why must multiplication happen before subtraction?
**Evidence sought:** The amount given away is removed from the full starting total.
**Hint if needed:** Ask which number the 29 is part of.
**Visual command:** `wp-board-02`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** What makes a final result reasonable?
**Evidence sought:** It is less than 84, nonnegative, and the units are plants.
**Hint if needed:** Estimate and check the requested unit.
**Visual command:** `wp-board-01`

## Reteaching branches

### reteach-wp-relationships
Targets: math-mis-wp-keyword-only
- Remove keywords and draw the relationship.
- Compare two stories sharing the same keyword.
- Choose the operation from known and unknown quantities.

### reteach-wp-relevant-data
Targets: math-mis-wp-use-all-numbers
- Attach units to every number.
- Connect quantities to the final question with arrows.
- Cross out one unrelated fact and explain why.

### reteach-wp-answer-target
Targets: math-mis-wp-one-step-stop
- Underline the final question.
- Name the hidden first question.
- After each calculation, state what the result answers.
- Continue until the original question is answered.

### reteach-wp-step-order
Targets: math-mis-wp-wrong-order
- Write each needed quantity in a box.
- Draw arrows from prerequisites to later steps.
- Compute only after the dependency order is clear.

### reteach-wp-units
Targets: math-mis-wp-unit-mismatch
- Label every quantity.
- Check which units can be added or subtracted.
- Explain product or quotient units in words.
- Write the final unit.

### reteach-wp-check
Targets: math-mis-wp-no-check
- Estimate the expected range.
- Use an inverse check.
- Read the final sentence with the answer inserted.
- Reject impossible sizes or units.

## Prerequisite-remediation branch

### prerequisite-remediation-wp-one-step
Targets: operation meaning, known/unknown quantities, units
- Sort one-step stories by relationship, not keyword.
- Draw one model for each operation.
- Match equations to stories and explain units.
**Exit evidence:** Solve and explain four one-step relationship types across two attempts.

## Parent or teacher review note

**Look for:**
- Does the learner name the final unit before calculating?
- Can the learner state what each intermediate result means?
- Does the learner check reasonableness and answer the actual question?
**Avoid:**
- Telling the operation from a keyword.
- Correcting arithmetic before checking the learner’s plan.
- Counting an unexplained number as full success.
**Review prompt:** Ask: “What question did your first step answer, and what was still missing?”

## No-media fallback

- Fold paper into six organizer boxes: ask, know, extra, hidden question, steps, check.
- Draw arrows between dependent steps.
- Write units beside every number.
- Use a rough estimate and inverse check on paper.

The sequence remains fully functional without images, animation, audio, a camera, or an identifiable learner photo.
