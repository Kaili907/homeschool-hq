# Student Runtime Privacy Report

## Status

Student Runtime integration tests exercise the accepted bridge's non-echoing privacy boundary. Raw answers, learner responses, transcripts, direct contact details, credentials, diagnosis language, and adult-private notes are prohibited from persistence-bound projections. Safety results use reason codes and do not contain disclosure excerpts.

## Verified behavior

- Known raw-answer and transcript keys fail persistence inspection.
- Email addresses and United States phone numbers are detected as direct identifiers.
- Known credential forms and diagnosis language fail persistence inspection.
- Learner-facing email and credential content is removed or replaced.
- Urgent and uncertain safety results declare that raw and matched text were not stored.
- Adult-review proposals contain opaque codes and explicitly exclude raw text and direct identifiers.

## Data-minimization requirements

The runtime must construct persisted events, checkpoints, traces, diagnostics, and outbox proposals from strict allowlists. Boolean claims such as `rawAnswerIncluded: false` are not sufficient if arbitrary sibling fields can be serialized. Free-form learner text must remain transient and must never enter the event ledger, checkpoint, outbox, quarantine detail, analytics, console output, screenshots, deterministic traces, or test snapshots.

Opaque identifiers must be generated by the runtime and must not be learner-supplied strings containing names or other identifying information. Protected draft and Tutor recovery references must remain opaque references; their underlying content belongs behind their respective sidecars.

## Residual limitations and required tests

The bridge privacy scan is defense in depth, not a general PII detector. Name removal only recognizes a narrow leading `First Last,` form, and persistence scanning does not generally identify names, postal addresses, usernames, student numbers, or international phone formats. Credential detection should additionally cover prose credentials, bearer tokens, JWT-like strings, SSH/private-key material, and connection strings.

Final integration tests should use spy sinks and captured logging to prove that embedded names, addresses, handles, international phone numbers, credential variants, prompt injection, disclosures, and transcripts never reach any persistence or telemetry boundary. Archive inspection should also scan generated traces, source maps, reports, screenshots, and built assets for seeded canary secrets.
