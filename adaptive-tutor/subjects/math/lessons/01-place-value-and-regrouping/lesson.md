# Place Value and Regrouping

**Sequence ID:** `math-seq-pv-regroup-v1`  
**Lesson ID:** `math-lesson-01-place-value-regrouping`  
**Version:** `1.0.0`  
**Grade band:** 4–6  
**Summary:** Build, compare, compose, decompose, add, and subtract multi-digit whole numbers using base-ten equivalence.

## Instructional commitments

- Do not reveal a final answer before the learner attempts the current step and explains reasoning.
- Present exactly one step or one question at a time.
- Ask the learner to explain how they know before advancing.
- Treat incorrect answers as evidence for routing, not as a character judgment.
- Use warm, specific, non-shaming language. Never label a learner as bad at math.
- Do not diagnose dyscalculia or any medical, developmental, or learning condition.
- Do not complete graded homework. Offer concept instruction or a similar ungraded example instead.
- Never claim mastery from one correct response; require repeated evidence across representations.
- Never require a camera, microphone, or identifiable child photo.

## Prerequisites

- Count, read, and write whole numbers to at least 10,000.
- Recall basic addition and subtraction facts.
- Understand that 10 ones equal 1 ten and 10 tens equal 1 hundred.

## Objectives

- Identify a digit’s value through the hundred-thousands place.
- Compose, decompose, compare, and order multi-digit numbers.
- Add and subtract with regrouping, including across zeros.
- Use models, equations, and estimates to explain and check work.

## Diagnostic assessment

Administer one item at a time. Ask the learner to explain the choice before showing correctness. Record the response as evidence and route only after enough evidence is available.

## Likely misconception patterns and distinguishing evidence

### Names the digit instead of its value
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-pv-digit-vs-value`
**Likely evidence:**
- Chooses 7 or 7,000 for the 7 in 472,615.
- Says the place name but does not multiply by its unit value.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Ask for the same digit in two positions, such as 7 in 70,507.
- Ask the learner to build the number with a place-value chart.
**First response:** You found the digit. Now let’s connect its position to how much it is worth.
**Route:** `reteach-pv-place-value-chart`

### Treats zero placeholders as removable
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-pv-zero-skip`
**Likely evidence:**
- Reads 402,306 as 42,36 or decomposes it without the thousands/hundreds positions.
- Aligns numbers by visible digits rather than by place.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Compare 4,023 and 423 on a chart.
- Ask which position the zero is holding.
**First response:** A zero can hold a place even when it adds no amount. Let’s keep every column visible.
**Route:** `reteach-pv-zero-placeholders`

### Believes regrouping changes the total
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-pv-regroup-changes-number`
**Likely evidence:**
- Says 14 ones and 1 ten 4 ones are different amounts.
- Adds a carried ten without removing ten ones.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Trade 10 ones for 1 ten and recount.
- Write 14 = 10 + 4 beside the model.
**First response:** Regrouping changes the form, not the amount. Let’s make a fair trade of ten for one unit in the next place.
**Route:** `reteach-pv-trade-equivalence`

### Stops when the next place is zero
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-pv-borrow-zero`
**Likely evidence:**
- Cannot regroup in 5,002 − 187.
- Changes a zero directly to 10 without showing where the unit came from.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Use 1 thousand → 10 hundreds → 9 hundreds and 10 tens → 9 tens and 12 ones.
- Ask the learner to point to the source of every regrouped unit.
**First response:** The next column is zero, so we need to trace the trade to the first place that has a unit available.
**Route:** `reteach-pv-across-zeros`

### Subtracts the smaller digit from the larger digit in each column
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-pv-big-minus-small`
**Likely evidence:**
- Computes 42 − 18 as 36.
- Ignores which digit belongs to the whole minuend or subtrahend.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Ask whether 42 − 18 should be more or less than 30.
- Model the ones column with regrouping.
**First response:** You compared the two digits, but subtraction keeps the order of the whole numbers. Let’s regroup the top number instead.
**Route:** `reteach-pv-subtraction-order`

### Aligns multi-digit numbers from the left edge
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-pv-left-align`
**Likely evidence:**
- Writes 4,083 over 2759 so 4 aligns with 2.
- Produces place-value errors even with accurate facts.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Ask the learner to label the ones column first.
- Compare left-aligned and right-aligned layouts.
**First response:** The digits need to line up by place, not by the left edge. Let’s anchor the ones column first.
**Route:** `reteach-pv-alignment`

## Visual explanation plan

Representations: base-ten-blocks, place-value-chart, column-arithmetic, number-line.

- Every visual command includes text alternative.
- Color is never the only indicator.
- No visual is required; all moves are also stated in words.

## Spoken explanation

**Voice direction:** warm, clear, unhurried, nonjudgmental

- `pv-n01` — Place value tells both which digit we see and how much that digit is worth.
- `pv-n02` — A digit’s position gives its unit: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, and beyond.
- `pv-n03` — Zeros matter because they hold positions, even when they add no amount.
- `pv-n04` — Regrouping is an equal trade. Ten ones become one ten, and the total stays the same.
- `pv-n05` — For addition and subtraction, line up digits by place and begin at the ones.
- `pv-n06` — When a column totals ten or more, regroup one unit into the next larger place.
- `pv-n07` — When subtracting across zeros, trace the trade to the first place that has a unit available.
- `pv-n08` — Do not switch to big digit minus small digit. Keep the order of the whole subtraction problem.
- `pv-n09` — Estimate before or after solving to check whether the exact answer has a reasonable size.
- `pv-n10` — Explain each trade. Your explanation gives stronger evidence than a single correct number.

## Show Me mode

**Purpose:** Watch a complete worked example while predicting each next move.
**Practice example:** `36,482 + 7,659`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** Before calculating, where should the ones digits be placed?
**Evidence sought:** Align 2 above 9 in the ones column.
**Hint if needed:** Find the rightmost digit in each number.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-03`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** The ones total 11. What trade keeps the value the same?
**Evidence sought:** Trade 10 ones for 1 ten and leave 1 one.
**Hint if needed:** Think: 11 = 10 + 1.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-02`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** After adding each place and regrouping, how can an estimate check the result?
**Evidence sought:** Use rounded values and compare the size.
**Hint if needed:** Round 36,482 and 7,659 to convenient thousands.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-05`

## Talk Me Through It mode

**Purpose:** The learner explains the plan aloud or in writing; the tutor asks one focused question at a time.
**Practice example:** `50,307 − 8,946`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** What do you notice in the ones column?
**Evidence sought:** 7 is too small to subtract 6? Actually 7 − 6 can be done, so no regrouping yet.
**Hint if needed:** Check the top and bottom ones digits.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-03`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** What happens in the tens column, where 0 − 4 cannot be done with whole-number regrouping?
**Evidence sought:** Trace a regroup from the hundreds or farther left.
**Hint if needed:** Look left for the first place with a unit available.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-04`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** How will you check whether your difference is reasonable?
**Evidence sought:** Estimate 50,000 − 9,000 ≈ 41,000.
**Hint if needed:** Round each number to the nearest thousand.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-05`

## Let’s Do One Together mode

**Purpose:** Alternate responsibility between tutor and learner.
**Practice example:** `27,685 + 14,739`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** I aligned the ones. You add the ones and describe the regroup.
**Evidence sought:** 5 + 9 = 14; write 4 ones and regroup 1 ten.
**Hint if needed:** Say both what is written and what is moved.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-03`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** I added the tens including the regrouped ten. Which column comes next?
**Evidence sought:** Hundreds.
**Hint if needed:** Move one place left.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-03`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** You finish the remaining columns. What estimate will you use as a final check?
**Evidence sought:** About 28,000 + 15,000 = 43,000.
**Hint if needed:** Use nearby thousands.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-05`

## Different Example mode

**Purpose:** Transfer the same idea to regrouping across zeros.
**Practice example:** `90,004 − 27,568`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** Which place first needs a regroup?
**Evidence sought:** The ones place because 4 − 8 needs regrouping.
**Hint if needed:** Start at the rightmost column.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-04`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** Where is the first available unit to the left?
**Evidence sought:** The ten-thousands place (9 ten-thousands).
**Hint if needed:** Pass through each zero place carefully.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-04`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** Explain why every trade keeps the number equal to 90,004.
**Evidence sought:** Each unit becomes ten units of the next smaller place.
**Hint if needed:** Name the base-ten equivalence at each move.
**Visual command:** `pv-board-02`

## Reteaching branches

### reteach-pv-place-value-chart
Targets: math-mis-pv-digit-vs-value, math-mis-pv-left-align
- Build the number in a labeled chart.
- Say digit, place, and value as three separate facts.
- Move the same digit to a new place and compare values.
- Return to one diagnostic transfer item.

### reteach-pv-zero-placeholders
Targets: math-mis-pv-zero-skip
- Use empty columns with zero cards.
- Compare numbers that differ only around zeros.
- Align by the ones column.
- Explain what each zero is holding.

### reteach-pv-trade-equivalence
Targets: math-mis-pv-regroup-changes-number
- Build 14 with ones.
- Trade ten ones for one ten.
- Write 14 = 10 + 4.
- Apply the same trade inside one addition column.

### reteach-pv-across-zeros
Targets: math-mis-pv-borrow-zero, math-mis-pv-big-minus-small
- Start with a base-ten model such as 3,002.
- Trace one unit through each zero place.
- Record every changed digit on a chart.
- Check the difference with addition.

## Prerequisite-remediation branch

### prerequisite-remediation-pv-base-ten
Targets: 10 ones = 1 ten, place names through thousands
- Bundle counters into groups of ten.
- Match 34, 3 tens 4 ones, and 30 + 4.
- Practice two-digit addition without regrouping before one regrouping trade.
**Exit evidence:** Explain and model two base-ten trades correctly on separate examples.

## Parent or teacher review note

**Look for:**
- Does the learner name digit, place, and value separately?
- Can the learner explain where a regrouped unit came from?
- Does the learner use an estimate or inverse operation to check?
**Avoid:**
- Speed pressure before place-value accuracy.
- Calling a regrouping error careless without checking the model.
- Treating one correct worksheet item as mastery.
**Review prompt:** Ask: “Show me one trade that keeps the number equal. How do you know?”

## No-media fallback

- Draw a six-column place-value chart on paper.
- Use dots for ones, lines for tens, squares for hundreds, and labeled boxes for larger places.
- Read every visual command aloud as a text instruction.
- Use scrap paper for column alignment and estimates.

The sequence remains fully functional without images, animation, audio, a camera, or an identifiable learner photo.
