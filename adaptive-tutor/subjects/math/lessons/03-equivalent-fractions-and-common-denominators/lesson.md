# Equivalent Fractions and Common Denominators

**Sequence ID:** `math-seq-equivalent-fractions-v1`  
**Lesson ID:** `math-lesson-03-equivalent-fractions-common-denominators`  
**Version:** `1.0.0`  
**Grade band:** 4–6  
**Summary:** Use fraction bars, number lines, multiples, and area models to build equivalence and operate with unlike denominators.

## Instructional commitments

- Do not reveal a final answer before the learner attempts the current step and explains reasoning.
- Present exactly one step or one question at a time.
- Ask the learner to explain how they know before advancing.
- Treat incorrect answers as evidence for routing, not as a character judgment.
- Use warm, specific, non-shaming language. Never label a learner as bad at math.
- Do not diagnose dyscalculia or any medical, developmental, or learning condition.
- Do not complete graded homework. Offer concept instruction or a similar ungraded example instead.
- Never claim mastery from one correct response; require repeated evidence across representations.
- Never require a camera, microphone, or identifiable child photo.

## Prerequisites

- Recognize numerator and denominator.
- Partition the same-size whole into equal parts.
- Recall multiplication facts and list multiples.

## Objectives

- Generate and explain equivalent fractions.
- Find useful common denominators.
- Add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators.
- Compare fractions using models and common units.

## Likely misconception patterns and distinguishing evidence

### Applies whole-number size rules to denominators
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-fr-whole-number-rules`
**Likely evidence:**
- Says 1/8 is greater than 1/4 because 8 is greater than 4.
- Chooses the fraction with the larger denominator when numerators match.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Compare equal-size fraction bars.
- Place both fractions between 0 and 1 on a number line.
**First response:** The denominator tells how many equal parts make the whole. More equal parts means each part is smaller.
**Route:** `reteach-fr-unit-size`

### Scales only the numerator or only the denominator
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-fr-one-sided-scale`
**Likely evidence:**
- Changes 2/3 to 4/3 or 2/6.
- Cannot explain why multiplying by n/n keeps value equal.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Overlay fraction bars before and after scaling.
- Ask what form of 1 was multiplied.
**First response:** For an equivalent fraction, the numerator and denominator must be scaled by the same nonzero factor.
**Route:** `reteach-fr-equivalence`

### Adds numerators and denominators
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-fr-add-denominators`
**Likely evidence:**
- Computes 2/5 + 1/3 as 3/8.
- Treats unlike-size pieces as directly countable.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Ask whether fifths and thirds are the same-size pieces.
- Use fraction bars to rename both fractions in equal-sized parts.
**First response:** We can combine only pieces with the same name. Let’s rename both fractions with a common denominator first.
**Route:** `reteach-fr-common-units`

### Believes the product is the only possible common denominator
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-fr-common-product-only`
**Likely evidence:**
- Uses 48 for 3/8 and 1/4 and cannot see 8.
- Finds a correct but unnecessarily large denominator and becomes error-prone.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- List multiples of both denominators.
- Ask for the least shared multiple and another valid shared multiple.
**First response:** The product can work, but a smaller shared multiple may make the work easier.
**Route:** `reteach-fr-common-denominator`

### Compares fraction models built from different-size wholes
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-fr-unequal-wholes`
**Likely evidence:**
- Calls a larger physical 1/4 greater than a smaller physical 1/2 without checking the whole.
- Ignores the equal-whole condition.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Make both bars the same total length.
- Ask what the whole is in each model.
**First response:** Fraction comparisons need the same-size whole. Let’s make the wholes equal before comparing the parts.
**Route:** `reteach-fr-same-whole`

### Simplifies by subtracting the same number from numerator and denominator
**Misconception ID:** `math-mis-fr-simplify-subtract`
**Likely evidence:**
- Changes 6/8 to 4/6.
- Cannot identify a common factor.
**Distinguishing evidence:**
- Ask whether the value stays at the same number-line point.
- Use division by a common factor and multiply back to check.
**First response:** Simplifying divides both numbers by the same common factor; subtracting does not preserve the ratio.
**Route:** `reteach-fr-simplify`

## Visual explanation plan

Representations: fraction-bar, number-line, area-model, multiples-list, step-diagram.

- Every model specifies an equal-size whole.
- Patterns or labels supplement shading.
- All visual reasoning is duplicated in words and equations.

## Spoken explanation

**Voice direction:** warm, precise, unhurried, nonjudgmental

- `fr-n01` — A fraction names a number using equal parts of a whole.
- `fr-n02` — The denominator tells how many equal parts make one whole; the numerator tells how many parts are counted.
- `fr-n03` — Equivalent fractions name the same value with different-size units.
- `fr-n04` — Equivalent fractions land at the same point on a number line.
- `fr-n05` — A common denominator is a shared unit. The least common denominator is often the easiest shared unit.
- `fr-n06` — To add or subtract fractions, rename them with equal-size pieces before combining numerators.
- `fr-n07` — Do not add denominators. The denominator names the unit and stays the same once the units match.
- `fr-n08` — Comparisons are fair only when the wholes are the same size.
- `fr-n09` — Simplify by dividing the numerator and denominator by the same common factor.
- `fr-n10` — Use bars, number lines, and equations to explain why the value stays equal.

## Show Me mode

**Purpose:** Watch unlike fractions become equal-size units before combining.
**Practice example:** `2/3 + 1/4`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** Why can’t thirds and fourths be counted together yet?
**Evidence sought:** They are different-size units.
**Hint if needed:** Look at the widths of the pieces.
**Visual command:** `fr-board-03`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** What is the least common denominator of 3 and 4?
**Evidence sought:** 12.
**Hint if needed:** List multiples until one matches.
**Visual command:** `fr-board-04`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** After renaming both fractions in twelfths, what operation is left?
**Evidence sought:** Add the numerators while keeping denominator 12.
**Hint if needed:** Now the pieces have the same name.
**Visual command:** `fr-board-03`

## Talk Me Through It mode

**Purpose:** The learner explains every equivalence used in a subtraction.
**Practice example:** `5/6 − 1/4`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** What common denominator will you use, and why?
**Evidence sought:** 12 because it is divisible by 6 and 4.
**Hint if needed:** List or reason about common multiples.
**Visual command:** `fr-board-04`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** How will you rename 5/6 and 1/4 without changing their values?
**Evidence sought:** 10/12 and 3/12 using equal scaling.
**Hint if needed:** Multiply each numerator and denominator by the same factor.
**Visual command:** `fr-board-01`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** How can a bar or number line verify the difference?
**Evidence sought:** Show the distance from 3/12 to 10/12 as 7/12.
**Hint if needed:** Use equal twelfth units.
**Visual command:** `fr-board-02`

## Let’s Do One Together mode

**Purpose:** Alternate choosing a common unit and performing a rename.
**Practice example:** `3/5 + 1/2`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** I listed multiples of 5. You name the least common multiple with 2.
**Evidence sought:** 10.
**Hint if needed:** Which listed multiple is also even?
**Visual command:** `fr-board-04`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** I renamed 3/5 as 6/10. You rename 1/2.
**Evidence sought:** 5/10.
**Hint if needed:** Multiply top and bottom by 5.
**Visual command:** `fr-board-03`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** You combine the tenths and decide whether the result is less than, equal to, or greater than one. Explain.
**Evidence sought:** 11/10, greater than one.
**Hint if needed:** Ten tenths make one whole.
**Visual command:** `fr-board-03`

## Different Example mode

**Purpose:** Transfer from addition to comparison.
**Practice example:** `5/6 compared with 7/9`

### Step 1
**Tutor asks:** What shared denominator can represent sixths and ninths?
**Evidence sought:** 18.
**Hint if needed:** Find a common multiple of 6 and 9.
**Visual command:** `fr-board-04`

### Step 2
**Tutor asks:** Rename both fractions in eighteenths.
**Evidence sought:** 15/18 and 14/18.
**Hint if needed:** Scale 6 by 3 and 9 by 2.
**Visual command:** `fr-board-05`

### Step 3
**Tutor asks:** Which is greater, and how does the model prove it?
**Evidence sought:** 5/6 is greater by 1/18.
**Hint if needed:** Compare equal-size pieces.
**Visual command:** `fr-board-05`

## Reteaching branches

### reteach-fr-unit-size
Targets: math-mis-fr-whole-number-rules
- Use same-length wholes.
- Partition into halves, fourths, and eighths.
- Compare unit sizes.
- Mark fractions on a number line.

### reteach-fr-equivalence
Targets: math-mis-fr-one-sided-scale
- Overlay equivalent bars.
- Name the scaling factor.
- Write multiplication by n/n.
- Check the same number-line point.

### reteach-fr-common-units
Targets: math-mis-fr-add-denominators
- Place unlike pieces side by side.
- Rename with one shared unit.
- Combine only numerators after units match.
- Estimate whether the result is reasonable.

### reteach-fr-common-denominator
Targets: math-mis-fr-common-product-only
- List multiples of both denominators.
- Circle all shared multiples.
- Choose the least useful shared multiple.
- Compare with the product denominator.

### reteach-fr-same-whole
Targets: math-mis-fr-unequal-wholes
- Reset bars to equal total length.
- Compare the same fractions again.
- State the equal-whole requirement.

### reteach-fr-simplify
Targets: math-mis-fr-simplify-subtract
- Find a common factor.
- Divide numerator and denominator by that factor.
- Multiply back to check equivalence.
- Confirm on a number line.

## Prerequisite-remediation branch

### prerequisite-remediation-fr-parts
Targets: equal parts, numerator/denominator, unit fractions
- Fold or draw equal-size paper bars.
- Label total equal parts and selected parts.
- Order 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, and 1/6 with the same whole.
**Exit evidence:** Correctly explain numerator, denominator, and unit-fraction size on three models over two attempts.

## Parent or teacher review note

**Look for:**
- Does the learner keep the whole the same size?
- Can the learner explain why scaling top and bottom together preserves value?
- Does the learner name the shared unit before adding or subtracting?
**Avoid:**
- Teaching “butterfly” cross-multiplication without meaning.
- Accepting denominator addition as a small arithmetic slip.
- Using differently sized food pictures as comparison proof.
**Review prompt:** Ask: “What unit are these pieces named in now, and why is that a fair unit?”

## No-media fallback

- Draw equal-length fraction bars with a ruler or folded paper.
- Use a hand-drawn zero-to-one number line.
- List denominator multiples in two rows and circle matches.
- State each renaming equation aloud.

The sequence remains fully functional without images, animation, audio, a camera, or an identifiable learner photo.
